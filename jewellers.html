Where the day goes

Six jobs in a jewellery business that a person should not be doing.

None of them are hard. All of them are constant, and all of them land on the same counter, the same three phones and the same partner desk.

💰

The rate call

"Aaj ka rate kya hai?" arrives all day, in every language, and it is the same forty seconds every time — 22K, 24K, making charges, wastage. It lands on a counter salesperson who was mid-sale, and during a rate spike it lands on all of them at once.

💍

The bridal lead that cools between visits

A bridal order is three to five visits, a design revision or two, an advance, and a final fitting. The drop-off is not at the first visit, it is in the silence after it, when the follow-up call belonged to a person who had a showroom to run.

📅

Scheme instalments nobody chased

Gold savings schemes only pay if the instalment lands every month. Missed dues, renewals and maturities are a calling job with a due date, and the due date always falls on a day the counter is busy.

🪔

Festival peaks you cannot staff for

Akshaya Tritiya, Dhanteras, Diwali, Onam, Pongal. Demand triples for nine days and headcount does not. Every call that goes to voicemail in that window is a customer who walked into the shop across the road.

📦

The wholesale side of the counter

Retail partners want the catalogue, the margin, the MOQ, the order status and the ledger. Partner-sales heads want the IIJS stall booked out and the overdue invoice to have a payment date. Both are phone-and-WhatsApp work, and both are done by the same three people.

⚖️

Answers that change by counter

Hallmarking, buyback deduction, making charges, exchange purity, certificate reissue. Every branch answers slightly differently, and the customer only notices the day the two answers disagree.

The phone half of this runs on how the voice layer works; the chat half on WhatsApp agents in detail. Neither is the product. The product is the job getting done.

End to end

AI agents for jewellery retail, and the work they own.

Not a chatbot answering one question. A workflow that starts at an enquiry and ends with a booking, a payment date or a CRM field somebody can act on.

Workflow A

Festival campaign to booked showroom visit

  1. Marketing loads the opted-in segment from the CRM with store, language and last-purchase category.
  2. The agent calls inside the TRAI 9am–9pm window, DND-suppressed, disclosing that it is an AI on the first line.
  3. It opens in the customer's language and follows them if they switch mid-call (Hindi to Tamil, English to Malayalam).
  4. It names the festival offer and one collection that matches what they bought last, not the whole catalogue.
  5. It agrees the store, the day and the time, and books the slot against the branch calendar.
  6. WhatsApp confirmation goes out on an approved template with the map link and the consultant's name.
  7. The booking, language, objection and outcome are written to the CRM as fields, not as a recording.
  8. Anyone who asked for a callback from a human is queued with the context already captured.
  9. A no-show at T+1 gets one reschedule attempt, then stops. STOP is honoured on the spot.
Workflow B

Bridal enquiry to third visit

  1. Enquiry arrives from the website form, a Meta lead ad, or an inbound missed call.
  2. Callback inside the minute: occasion, wedding date, region and rituals, rough budget, nearest showroom.
  3. The WhatsApp agent sends a curated set — budget and occasion filtered, not the full lookbook — and answers on purity, certification and making charges from the knowledge base.
  4. Consultation booked into the bridal counter's calendar with the consultant matched by language.
  5. Visit happens. The showroom conversation is captured with spoken consent and filed to the CRM as notes and follow-up tasks.
  6. Whatever was promised in the room — a design in another weight, a stone swap, a quote — becomes a tracked task with an owner and a date.
  7. T+3 follow-up on WhatsApp referencing the specific piece, not a generic nudge.
  8. Advance paid, order enters the karigar job queue; status questions from then on are self-serve.
  9. Post-delivery: certificate and care instructions on WhatsApp, CSAT captured, daily-wear cross-sell scheduled for the anniversary.
Workflow C

Wholesale partner desk: catalogue to ledger

  1. Retail partner messages the partner WhatsApp number or calls the partner desk.
  2. Agent identifies the partner account and serves the wholesale catalogue with margin, MOQ and lead time.
  3. Order captured with quantity, weight range and design codes; written to the order system with a reference.
  4. Manufacturing and QC stage updates go out as they change, so "kab tak?" stops being a phone call.
  5. Ledger and outstanding balance answered on request, from the receivables export.
  6. Invoice past its credit period triggers a courtesy call: the balance stated once, a payment date asked for, no pressure.
  7. Payment date and partner commitment are written back against the invoice; receivables sees a date, not a note.
  8. Before IIJS: partner invited, the day that suits them captured, badge link sent, stall slot held.
  9. Disputes and quality complaints are captured structured (batch, design code, issue, photos) and routed to a human with a complete file.

On the wholesale side the same partner book can carry risk monitoring on your trade partners, so the desk knows which ledger to chase first. Campaign imagery and virtual try-on for rings, earrings and necklaces are separate products and run alongside, not inside, these workflows.

Each one owns a job with a start and a finish. You do not buy a channel. You fill a seat.

Questions we get

What jewellery brands ask first.

What can AI actually do for a jewellery retailer?

The four things that already happen by phone and WhatsApp every day: answering gold-rate, collection and store-timing enquiries, booking showroom and bridal consultations, running festival and birthday outreach to an opted-in list, and chasing gold-scheme instalments. On the wholesale side it also runs the partner desk — catalogue, order status, ledger and overdue-invoice calls.

Can an AI agent quote today's gold rate?

Yes, from your rate feed, not from its own memory. It quotes 22K and 24K per gram at your store's price, explains making charges and wastage the way your counter explains them, and where branches price differently it quotes the branch the caller is asking about. If the feed is stale it says so instead of guessing.

How does it handle bridal enquiries?

It captures the occasion, the wedding date, the region and its rituals, the rough budget and the nearest showroom, then books the consultation with a consultant who speaks the customer's language. Between visits it follows up on WhatsApp against the specific piece discussed, and every promise made in the showroom becomes a tracked task with a date.

Which languages do customers get?

Twenty-two Indian languages in one agent, switching mid-call when the caller does, so someone who starts in English and moves to Tamil is followed rather than restarted. Outside India the same agent runs in the local language and calling hours, which is how the Gulf and European deployments work.

Is outbound calling to customers legal in India?

Within the rules, yes. Calls run inside the TRAI 9am–9pm window against a DND-suppressed, opted-in list, and the agent discloses that it is an AI. WhatsApp runs on approved templates inside the 24-hour window, and STOP is honoured immediately. Showroom conversations are recorded only after a spoken consent disclosure.

Do we need to replace our jewellery ERP or CRM?

No. The agents attach to what you already run. A scheduled export is enough to answer order, repair and scheme questions on day one; a live connection makes the answers fresher and can be added later. Nothing is installed inside your ERP to start a pilot, which is usually the difference between this quarter and next year.

What does it cost, and how long until it is live?

Fourteen days to a working line on your own collections, store list and campaign calendar, usually starting with one workflow — appointment booking or festival outreach. Pricing follows call and message volume and the number of stores, not seats, so a single boutique and a sixty-store chain each pay for what they use. We size it with you before anything is signed.
