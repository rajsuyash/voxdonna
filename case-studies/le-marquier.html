01 — The context

Premium products.
A phone line that could not keep up.

Le Marquier builds planchas and outdoor cooking equipment in France. The product is expensive, so the service has to match it. In grilling season it stopped matching.

THE CALLERS

Three languages, one small team.

Customers call from France, the UK, the US and Italy. The support desk is a handful of people at head office, strongest in French, thinner in English and Italian.

THE PATTERN

The same six questions.

Product care and seasoning, warranty terms, spare parts, the nearest dealer, order status, delivery dates. Expert answers to questions that never change.

THE COST

€70,000 a year, and climbing.

Peak-season hold times ran past fifteen minutes. The annual support bill sat at €70,000 and grew with the catalogue, not with the revenue.

02 — On the call

What Donna actually does
between hello and resolved.

01 / LANGUAGE

Answers in the caller’s language.

French, English or Italian, picked up from the first sentence. No menu, no “press 2”, no apology for the accent.

One line, three languages
02 / PRODUCT

Knows the catalogue.

Trained on Le Marquier’s product data, care guides and support documentation, so a seasoning question gets the same answer at 21:00 as at 11:00.

Answers from approved material
03 / WARRANTY

Takes the claim while the caller is still on the line.

Model, purchase date, description of the fault. The claim is opened during the call instead of after three emails.

Claim raised in one conversation
04 / DEALERS

Finds the nearest dealer.

By postal code, from the current dealer list. A buyer who wanted to see the plancha in person gets an address rather than a promise to call back.

A next step, not a note
05 / ORDERS

Reads order status from the system of record.

Connected to the ERP, so delivery questions are answered from the live record instead of a guess.

Status without a human lookup
06 / ESCALATION

Hands over when it should.

Complaints, safety questions and anything outside the approved answers go to a person, with the conversation attached. Escalation runs at 2% of calls.

2% reach a human

03 — The rollout

Live in eight weeks.
Not live on day one.

Nothing was switched over on faith. The agent took a fifth of the call volume first, and the rest only after the transcripts said it should.

How Voxdonna voice agents work ↗

  1. W1–2

    Listen to what already happens.

    Six months of call recordings reviewed. The top 50 call types accounted for 90% of the volume, and that list became the scope.

  2. W3–4

    Train the agent and pick the voice.

    Conversation flows built on the product catalogue, warranty terms and care guides, then tuned until the tone matched the brand rather than a call centre.

  3. W5–6

    Connect the systems.

    ERP, CRM and knowledge base wired in so order status and warranty entries are real. The internal team tested it before any customer did.

  4. W7–8

    Soft launch at 20%.

    One call in five went to Donna. Real conversations drove the corrections. Full rollout followed once the escalations stopped being about things she should have known.

05 — How it was built

The engineering,
written up separately.

The short version: one knowledge base in French rather than three, language detection on the first utterance, an ERP connection for anything with a number in it, and an escalation rule that fires before the caller has to ask for a person. The long version — the model choices, the latency work and the two things that had to be rebuilt — is in the founder’s write-up, building the Le Marquier French voice agent.
