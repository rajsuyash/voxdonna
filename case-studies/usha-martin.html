01 — The customer

Wire rope,
sold into the hard industries.

Usha Martin Limited describes itself as one of the world’s leading manufacturers of wire ropes, operating in over 75 countries, with plants in Ranchi and abroad.

THE PRODUCT

A component nobody gets to ignore.

Rope goes into cranes, mining, oil and offshore, ports, elevators, fishing and ropeways. In every one of those, the rope is the part holding the load. A question about it is never a low-stakes question.

THE BUYER

Industrial accounts, not consumers.

The caller is a maintenance head, a site engineer, a procurement manager or a distributor. They know the vocabulary, they have the paperwork in front of them, and they want a name and a time, not sympathy.

THE SPREAD

Time zones that do not cooperate.

Selling into more than 75 countries means an inbound complaint can land at any hour against a support desk that keeps Indian office hours. The gap is structural, not a staffing mistake.

02 — The problem

The complaint is not the problem.
The silence after it is.

01 / TIMING

Failures do not keep office hours.

A rope is inspected when the shift allows and it is replaced when the crane stops. Neither event checks the calendar. Complaints arrive at night, on Sundays, and during Indian public holidays from customers who do not share them.

Inbound, unscheduled
02 / TRIAGE

Every caller says urgent.

A stopped crane and a query about a test certificate arrive on the same line in the same tone. Without a consistent first question, the order in which they get handled is decided by whoever picked up.

Severity set on the call
03 / RECORD

A complaint taken by voice tends to stay in voice.

Notes get written later, or into an email, or not at all. The case in the CRM is created after the fact, with whatever the person still remembered.

Case created during the call
04 / COST OF SLOW

Silence gets read as an answer.

On a safety-critical part, a customer who waits does not wait quietly. They stop the asset, they call the distributor, they open a claim, and they start pricing the competitor. None of that is recoverable by replying on Monday.

First response is the product

03 — What we designed

One line, one script,
one place the case lands.

The agent is designed to do the part that is identical on every call: identify the account, classify the complaint, set severity, capture the evidence a quality engineer will ask for anyway, and put it in Salesforce before hanging up.

It is not designed to decide whether a rope is defective. That stays with the people qualified to say so.

How Voxdonna voice agents work ↗

  1. 01

    Identify the account and the rope.

    Order or invoice reference, plant, rope construction and diameter, application. The same opening questions every time, so two engineers reading two cases are reading the same fields.

  2. 02

    Classify into ten categories.

    The category list was drawn with Usha Martin’s quality team and covers what goes wrong in service and what goes wrong before the rope gets there: condition and wear in the field, specification and material mismatches, certification and documentation, and delivery and packaging.

  3. 03

    Set severity, commit to a callback.

    Severity is decided on the call against written criteria, and the caller is told the callback window before the line drops. Safety-critical cases page the on-call quality engineer immediately rather than waiting in a queue.

  4. 04

    Create the case in Salesforce.

    Account, category, severity, transcript and the captured fields are written to a Salesforce case during the conversation. The engineer opens a record, not a voicemail.

  5. 05

    Hand material returns to the RMA portal.

    Where the outcome is a return, the agent connects to Usha Martin’s existing RMA portal so the authorisation is raised in the system the warehouse already works from.

  6. 06

    Escalate to a human, with the context attached.

    Anything outside the ten categories, anything with an injury in it, and anything where the caller asks for a person goes to the QA on-call rota with the transcript and the draft case already written.

04 — The constraints

It runs in their cloud,
because it had to.

Data residency was set before the first design session, and it decided the architecture rather than decorating it.

01 / RESIDENCY

Indian data stays in India.

The agent is being deployed into Usha Martin’s own AWS India account. Call audio, transcripts and case data are stored in region. Nothing about the deployment depends on a vendor tenant somewhere else.

02 / NETWORK

Inside the VPC, not beside it.

The services sit in a private network inside the customer’s VPC. Salesforce and the RMA portal are reached over controlled egress, and the customer’s security team holds the keys to the account the whole thing runs in.

03 / STATE

RDS Postgres as the record.

Case state, category mapping and the severity audit trail live in managed Postgres under the customer’s own backup and retention policy. There is no second copy of the complaint history anywhere else.

04 / CONSEQUENCE

The constraint shaped the build.

Running in someone else’s account means every component has to be deployable, upgradable and auditable by their team. That ruled out anything we could not hand over, and it is why the design took the shape it did rather than the shape that would have been faster.

05 — The second track

What the field sales team
said in the meeting.

Scoped alongside the complaint line, on the same cloud footprint.

Usha Martin’s field sales engineers spend their week in plants and at ports, and the record of those conversations is whatever gets typed up afterwards. The second track records the call, summarises it, and posts the summary into MS Teams where the regional manager already reads everything else. Same principle as the complaint line: capture the thing while it is still accurate, and put it where somebody will actually see it.

If your own field team is the only place your customer intelligence lives, the pattern is the same one described in AI agents for manufacturers.
