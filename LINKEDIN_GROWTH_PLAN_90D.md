## Executive Summary

**500 in 90 days without founder cross-post is the 70th-percentile outcome, not the median.** Honest expected value: 350-450 followers. The constraint isn't content quality — it's that company pages get **1.6% of follower reach** in 2026 (down 60-66% since 2024) and account for only **5% of feed allocation vs 65% for personal profiles**. ([Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages))

What unlocks the curve: **the 150-follower threshold.** LinkedIn officially boosts pages by ~9x in algorithmic velocity once they cross 150 followers. ([LinkedIn Marketing Solutions](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/linkedin-pages/best-practices), [PPC.land](https://ppc.land/linkedins-3-step-strategy-for-exponential-follower-growth-on-company-pages/)) That's the single biggest milestone — it's not Day 90, it's wherever you cross 150 (likely Day 30-45).

Three paths from this research:

| Path | Day-90 result | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Organic-only, current setup** | **350-450** (median outcome) | 3-4 posts/wk Donna voice, 30 min/day page-comments on Tier 1, page admin invites maxed |
| **B. Add paid follower ads $500-800 over 90 days** | **500 (high confidence)** | Path A + LinkedIn Follower Ads boosted at $10-50/post |
| **C. Allow founder to COMMENT on page posts (not re-share)** | **450-550** | Path A + you spending 5 min/post commenting in first 60 min ([growleads](https://growleads.io/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-text-vs-video-reach/)) |

Founder *commenting* is different from cross-posting. Comments in the first 60 min after a page post drive ~3x first-hour engagement velocity, which decides total reach. The "no cross-post" rule you locked still holds; commenting on your own company page from your personal profile is a separate behavior.

## 1. Feasibility (no-cross-post baseline)

- Small pages (<5K followers) get 16 impressions per 100 followers vs 3.2 for 100K+ pages — relatively, you're advantaged ([Closely](https://blog.closelyhq.com/linkedin-company-page-benchmarks-followers-engagement-and-growth-rates/))
- New-page baseline growth: **1.5%/month** for pages <5K followers (LinkedIn benchmark), or **40.75% annually** for 1K-5K pages

**What founder/employee amplification typically delivers** (and we forfeit):
- Employee posts get **2.75x impressions** + **5x engagement** vs company page ([Refine Labs](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages))
- Employee re-shares reach **561% further** ([Refine Labs])
- Without amplification, you forfeit **60-80% of typical page reach** [unverified estimate — no clean isolation study exists]

**No case study of a B2B page hitting 500 in 90 days WITHOUT founder amplification is documented in public literature** ([blendcollective Biometaan case = 90 followers/30 days WITH employee amplification](https://www.blendcollective.eu/post/grow-linkedin-followers-by-500-in-30-days-case-study-6-successful-linkedin-account-2024)). The closest documented case studies (Cleo, $8M ARR SaaS via Favikon, Refine Labs aggregate) all rely heavily on founder-led content.

**Paid Follower Ads CPF for B2B SaaS targeting: $4-8 per follower** [directional, not academically verified]. Hitting 500 = $2,000-4,000 max paid; we model $500-800 to close the gap from organic median (400) to target (500).

### Length sweet spots
- **Medium-form (300-800 chars)** is the workhorse
- **Long-form (800-1500 chars)** reserved for case-study posts (Le Marquier)
- **Short (<300 chars)** only for polls + reactive replies

### Critical 2026 signals
- **Saves** are a new ranking signal: posts with saves get +35% secondary distribution. Design carousels to be saveable (checklists, frameworks). ([dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now))
- **Topic-match throttling**: LinkedIn cross-references post topic against page industry. Stay tight on voice AI / CX / call automation. Don't post about politics, generic productivity, etc.
- **External link in body = 60% reach penalty**. Link-in-first-comment workaround is ALSO penalized in 2026. Native content only; DM the link on request. ([growleads](https://growleads.io/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-text-vs-video-reach/))

## 3. Posting Cadence

- **3-4 posts/week to start, scale to 5 max.** Past 5/wk = diminishing returns + spam flag. ([Buffer 2M-post study](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/), [LinkBoost](https://blog.linkboost.co/linkedin-posting-frequency-best-practices-2026/))
- **Best windows (LinkedIn-specific, distinct from Twitter):**
  - Primary: Tue-Thu 10-11 AM local time (Paris CET / NY ET overlap = 14:00-16:00 UTC)
  - Secondary: Wed 11 AM-4 PM, Thu 1-5 PM
  - Avoid: Mon, weekends
  - ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-linkedin/), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/))
- **Escalation pattern**: Start 3/wk (Tue 10am carousel, Wed 11am text, Thu 1pm poll-or-video). At Day 30 if engagement holds, add Fri 10am. Never exceed 5/wk on a new page.

### Critical: first-hour velocity rule
The first 60 minutes after publishing decides total reach. Reply to every comment immediately. ([heysid](https://www.heysid.com/resources/employee-advocacy-on-linkedin-b2b-guide)) This is where founder-comment (not cross-post) becomes the lever — see §4.

## 5. Failure Modes (in order of how often they kill B2B pages)

1. **Press-release voice** — "Thrilled to announce" content gets <2% engagement. ([LinkBoost](https://blog.linkboost.co/linkedin-algorithm-changes-2026/))
2. **Product-only posts** — No insight/story = no saves/shares = low Depth Score.
3. **Inconsistent posting (gaps >7 days)** — Algorithm de-prioritizes irregular pages.
4. **No engagement on others' content** — Pages that only broadcast die.
5. **External link in body** — 60% reach reduction. Link-in-first-comment workaround now ALSO penalized.
6. **Vanity metrics** — Chasing followers vs saves/DMs/pipeline. 10k followers + zero pipeline = canonical failure.
7. **No founder visibility AT ALL** — Pages with zero face stall hardest. Soft-violating "no cross-post" via founder COMMENTS is the workaround.

## 6. LinkedIn-Specific Tactics

- **PDF carousels — STRONGLY YES.** Top engagement format, build them as numbered frameworks (e.g. "5 things every B2B CS team gets wrong about voice AI") so they're saveable.
- **LinkedIn Newsletter — defer to Month 2.** 150% YoY growth, 40-50% open rate (push notification), but cold-start expects 0-100 subs months 1-3. Don't launch until you have ~150 page followers.
- **Polls — yes, 1/week max.** Cheap engagement; reach multiplier 1.78x. Use as a "feeder" post that surfaces non-followers to the page.
- **Hashtags — 3-5 per post, END of post.** SEO-only role in 2026 (following is dead). Recommended: `#VoiceAI #ConversationalAI #CustomerExperience #ContactCenter` + occasional French: `#IAvocale #RelationClient`. ([ContentIn](https://contentin.io/blog/do-hashtags-work-on-linkedin/), [Sprout hashtags](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/linkedin-hashtags/))
- **LinkedIn Live — NO for a 0-follower page.** Reach depends on existing audience. Revisit at 500+.
- **Page admin invitations** — LinkedIn gives Super Admins 250 invitation credits/month. Use them. Most pages forget this. ([LinkedIn business](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/linkedin-pages/best-practices))

## 8. Donna LinkedIn Voice — 6 Example Posts

LinkedIn posts can run to 3000 chars. Donna voice still applies: one specific number, short paragraphs (LinkedIn rewards readable structure), no em-dashes, no AI-vocab, hook in first 1-2 lines (LinkedIn truncates feed at ~210 chars).

### Post 1 — Le Marquier customer story (~1,050 chars)
> Le Marquier sells premium French outdoor cooking equipment. They were drowning in 2,500 customer calls a month.
>
> Their CS team was three people. The math wasn't working.
>
> So we plugged Donna in.
>
> Six weeks later: 98% of those calls handled without a human. Average call: 4 minutes. CS cost down 80%.
>
> The team didn't shrink. They got promoted. They stopped reading tracking numbers and started solving the calls that actually matter, the ones where someone's grandfather's barbecue arrived dented and they need a human who cares.
>
> That's the thing nobody says out loud about voice AI in 2026. It's not about replacing the team. It's about firing the boring parts of the job so the humans can do the work that made them want the job in the first place.
>
> Le Marquier didn't buy software. They bought their team's calendar back.
>
> If you run a B2C operation in France doing more than 1,000 calls a month and your CS team is reading SKU numbers off a screen, you already know what to do.

### Post 2 — Contrarian (~780 chars)
> Every voice AI demo opens with "indistinguishable from a human."
>
> Wrong goal.
>
> Your customers don't want to be tricked. They want to be helped. Fast. Without a 14-minute hold queue and a script reader in Manila who can't override the refund policy.
>
> The benchmark isn't human. The benchmark is "did this resolve in 4 minutes without making me hate the brand."
>
> Voice AI that pretends to be human is a magic trick. Voice AI that openly says "I'm Donna, I can refund you in 30 seconds, what's your order number" is infrastructure.
>
> One of those gets unplugged the day someone catches it lying. The other one is still working in 2030.
>
> Pick the second one.

### Post 3 — CS economics (~520 chars)
> A French SMB call center seat costs roughly €38,000/year fully loaded.
>
> Three seats handles maybe 1,500 calls a month.
>
> A voice agent handles 2,500 calls a month at 4% of that.
>
> The conversation B2B founders are still having in 2026 is "can we afford to add headcount." The conversation they should be having is "why are we still adding headcount for a problem we solved 18 months ago."
>
> Math doesn't care about your org chart. It just keeps being math.

### Post 4 — French AI scene observation (~720 chars)
> Spent the week in Paris meeting French B2B founders.
>
> Pattern I keep seeing: brilliant tech, terrified of selling. Every demo starts with "but is the model from Mistral or OpenAI" and ends without anyone agreeing on what the product is for.
>
> Here's a free observation. American voice AI companies are 6 months behind on the tech and 18 months ahead on the GTM. They sell first, build later. We do the opposite and then wonder why Gradium and pyannoteAI raise abroad.
>
> French AI in 2026 has 1 thing the US doesn't: customers who actually pay invoices on time. Use the advantage. Sell.

### Post 5 — Behind-the-scenes (~680 chars)
> Built a voice agent last week. Sounded perfect in the demo. Took 1 production call and immediately told a customer their order had shipped from Lyon.
>
> The order shipped from Bayonne. The agent had hallucinated a city.
>
> Spent the next 6 hours hard-binding every factual claim to a database lookup. No retrieval, no answer. Boring engineering. The kind nobody puts in a launch video.
>
> That's 80% of building voice agents in 2026. Glamour is the demo. The product is paranoid plumbing that refuses to make anything up.
>
> The brands that win at voice are the ones who treat hallucination like a P0 bug, not a personality quirk.

### Post 6 — Direct CTA (~360 chars)
> You run a French B2B doing more than 1,000 customer calls a month.
>
> Your CS team is reading tracking numbers off a screen.
>
> You already know what this conversation is about.
>
> DM "Donna" and we'll show you what 4 minutes per call and 80% cost reduction looks like on your actual call volume.
>
> No deck. 1 call. We figure it out together.

### Counter-examples (what NOT to write)

1. *"Thrilled to announce that VoxDonna has partnered with Le Marquier to deliver a robust, end-to-end conversational AI solution! We're excited to leverage our comprehensive voice platform to unlock new value in the customer experience landscape. Learn more on our website! [link]"*
   → Violates: banned words (thrilled/excited/robust/leverage/comprehensive/landscape), corporate boilerplate, vague claims, external link in body (60% reach penalty), no specific number, no story, no Donna.

2. *"In today's rapidly evolving AI landscape, businesses must delve into the nuanced challenges of customer engagement. At VoxDonna, we deliver pivotal solutions that empower B2B leaders to navigate this complex paradigm. What does AI mean to your business? Let us know in the comments!"*
   → Violates: pure AI-vocab bingo (delve/nuanced/pivotal/landscape/paradigm/empower), no specifics, no number, weak question ending. Reads like ChatGPT default voice — opposite of Donna's "I know what I know."
