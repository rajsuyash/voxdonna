What it actually means

Three ways to get an order
off paper and into the system.

"Sales order automation" gets used for three genuinely different approaches. Knowing which one you have — or need — changes the whole conversation.

01 / EDI

A fixed electronic document.

Both sides agree on a structured format in advance. Reliable once set up, but every new partner or document variant needs its own integration project.

Best for: large, repeat partners on a standard format.
02 / RPA

A script against a fixed layout.

Robotic process automation reads the same fields from the same place on the same template, every time. Fast and cheap for one unchanging form, fragile the moment the layout shifts.

Best for: one partner, one template, rarely changes.
03 / AI AGENT

Reads the order like a person would.

Handles a scanned PDF, a reordered column, a free-text email or a WhatsApp message, then checks what it read against your price list and stock before posting anything.

Best for: mixed formats, free-text orders, exceptions.

Where EDI still wins

EDI isn't going away.
It just doesn't cover everyone.

For the partners who already have it, EDI is still the cheapest, most reliable option — structured, predictable, and built for exactly this job.

Where it wins

Large, repeat trading partners who send the same structured document every time. No ambiguity to resolve, no judgment calls — just a format both systems already agree on.

Where it breaks down

Smaller buyers without an EDI setup, one-off or seasonal partners, and anyone who emails a PDF or writes "same as last time, add 50 units" instead of sending a document. At most distributors, that's a large share of total order volume — and EDI has no answer for it.

What AI adds

What an AI agent does
that RPA can't.

RPA and AI agents both automate data entry. The difference is what happens when the input doesn't match the script exactly.

01 / UNSTRUCTURED PDFS

Reads a scan, not just a template.

A photographed purchase order, a scanned fax, a PDF exported from a different system each month — an AI agent extracts the line items regardless of layout. RPA needs the layout to hold still.

02 / FREE-TEXT EMAILS

Understands "the usual, but more."

Buyers don't always send a form. An AI agent can parse an email that references last quarter's order, a phone call, or a WhatsApp message, and turn that into a structured line-item list.

03 / EXCEPTIONS

Escalates instead of guessing.

An unrecognised SKU, a price mismatch, a quantity that doesn't make sense against stock — these go to a person with the context attached, rather than getting posted on a best guess.

Before you ask

The practical
questions.

Is EDI obsolete now that AI agents exist?

No. EDI is still the right choice for high-volume, standardised orders with large repeat partners who already have the integration in place. It breaks down with smaller buyers, one-off partners and anyone who sends a PDF or a free-text email instead of an EDI document — which is most of the order flow at a typical distributor.

What's the real difference between RPA and an AI agent for order entry?

RPA follows a fixed script against a fixed layout: the same fields, in the same place, every time. An AI agent reads the order the way a person would, so it can handle a scanned PDF, a reordered column, or an email that says "same as last time but 200 more units" without a new script for each variation. RPA still wins on cost for a single, truly unchanging layout.

Will an AI agent just make things up when an order is unclear?

Not if it is scoped correctly. It should post an order only when every required field matches a value it can verify against your price list, stock and customer records. Anything it cannot verify — an unrecognised SKU, a price that does not match, a missing quantity — goes to a person instead of getting guessed.

Does this replace our EDI connections or our ERP?

No. The agent sits in front of the order inbox and writes into your existing ERP or SAP instance through the access you grant it. Partners who already send EDI keep sending EDI. The agent picks up everything else: the PDFs, the emails and the orders that never had an EDI connection in the first place.

How long does it take to get a sales order agent live?

That depends on your order formats, your ERP's write rules and how many exceptions the first weeks of real traffic surface. A pilot usually starts read-only against a sample of real orders, then earns write access once accuracy is proven on cases your team has checked.

What does a sales order automation project cost?

It depends on order volume, how many formats you receive and which ERP or SAP modules it needs to write to, so it is scoped before it is priced. You get the build cost, the ongoing running cost and the launch plan in writing before work starts.
