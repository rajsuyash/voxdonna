## 1. Company Overview

### 1.1 History and Global Presence
Joyalukkas traces its roots to 1956, when Alukka Joseph Varghese founded a jewellery business in Thrissur, Kerala, which later evolved into the Alukkas jewellery brand. In 1987, the group expanded into the Gulf region under the Alukkas name, establishing a strong presence in the UAE and neighbouring countries. After restructuring in the early 2000s, Joy Alukkas led further expansion across India and internationally, rebranding the business as Joyalukkas with the ambition to “ornament the world.”[^1]

Today, Joyalukkas operates over 190 showrooms across 13 countries, serving more than 11 million customers and employing over 10,000 people. The brand has a significant presence in India, the Middle East, Singapore, Malaysia, the UK, USA and Australia. In India specifically, third‑party retail intelligence estimates around 91 store locations across 70 cities in 13–14 states and territories.[^2][^3][^1]

### 1.2 Founder and Leadership
The brand’s growth is driven by the visionary leadership of Joy Alukkas, Chairman and Managing Director of Joyalukkas Group. His father, Alukka Joseph Varghese, laid the original foundation for the jewellery business in Thrissur. International operations are led by John Paul Alukkas, Non‑Executive Director of Joyalukkas India and CEO of Joyalukkas Jewellery International Operations, who has 16+ years’ experience in marketing, branding and strategic planning and is an alumnus of Harvard Business School and the International Gemological Institute.[^1]

The India entity lists a board comprising managing directors, executive directors and non‑executive independent directors, including Alukkas Varghese Joy (Managing Director) and John Paul Joy Alukkas (Non‑Executive Director), among others.[^1]

### 1.3 Mission, Vision and Brand Values
Joyalukkas’ stated vision is “to ornament the world,” reflecting its ambition to be a preferred global destination for jewellery. The mission is “to improve and enrich life everywhere by offering extraordinary lifestyle solutions backed by incomparable value addition and innovation, adhering to globally approved processes and norms, and creating a successful value chain for our associates.”[^1]

Brand communications emphasize trust, quality, customer delight, and innovation in design, supported by hallmarked jewellery and international certifications. The company positions itself as a premium shopping destination for gold and diamond jewellery that embellishes everyday moments and milestones with elegance and sophistication.[^4][^1]

### 1.4 Brand Positioning and Ambassadors
Joyalukkas is positioned as “the World’s favourite jeweller,” a premium yet widely accessible brand catering to men, women and children across diverse regions and cultures. It emphasizes both traditional Indian designs and contemporary styles, with specific focus on bridal collections, diamond jewellery and temple jewellery.[^5][^4][^1]

Over time, the brand has associated with several celebrity ambassadors including Hrithik Roshan, Shreya Ghoshal, Kangana Ranaut, Kajol Devgan, Allu Arjun, Suresh Gopi and Kiccha Sudeep, reinforcing its image as a fashionable, aspirational yet trustworthy jeweller.[^1]

### 1.5 Awards and Recognitions
Official narratives highlight that Joyalukkas was the first jewellery retailer to be awarded both ISO 9001:2008 and ISO 14001:2004 certifications, reflecting its focus on quality management and environmental standards. The brand has also been awarded the Dubai Quality Award certification by H.H. Sheikh Mohammed Bin Rashid Al Maktoum, and has held Superbrand status in the UAE for eight consecutive years from 2010.[^1]

Joyalukkas has received Middle East Retailer of the Year (2005) and multiple national jewellery and design awards, strengthening its reputation for excellence in customer service and design innovation.[^1]

### 1.6 Key Statistics (High‑Level)
- 11+ million customers served globally.[^1]
- 190+ showrooms worldwide.[^1]
- 13 countries of operation.[^2][^1]
- 10,000+ employees.[^1]
- ~91 store locations in India across 70 cities in 13–14 states and territories (third‑party estimate, not an official Joyalukkas figure).[^3]

Where store counts or detailed distribution per city/state are needed, the voice agent should refer customers to the official store locator or customer care, as such granular data is not fully available from official sources.

## 3. Gold Savings Schemes

### 3.1 Overview
Joyalukkas offers structured jewellery purchase plans often referred to as “Gold Schemes” or “Jewellery Purchase Schemes,” designed to help customers save towards future jewellery purchases with benefits on making charges and stone value.[^16][^6]

Officially documented scheme types on the Joyalukkas India site include:
- Cash Saving Scheme (Easy Gold Payment – Monthly and Single).[^16]
- Weight Fix Scheme (Advance Smart Gold – Monthly and Single).[^16]
- Easy Gold Purchase Scheme (10‑month multiple advance scheme) explained in a detailed PDF brochure.[^6]

### 3.2 Cash Saving Scheme – Easy Gold Payment (Monthly)

**Eligibility & Duration**
- Available to individuals who complete KYC formalities and enroll via any Joyalukkas showroom or online channel.[^6][^16]
- Minimum monthly slab from ₹1,000 onwards.[^16]
- Duration: 10 monthly payments; purchases typically permitted from around the 300th day and up to a defined end period as per scheme terms.[^6][^16]

**Benefits**
- No making charge on gold jewellery (up to 18% off tag making charge).[^16]
- Substantial discounts on making charges and stones for precious stone, rosecut and diamond jewellery (e.g., 60–75% off MC and 25% off stone value, as per official scheme tables).[^6][^16]
- Ability to purchase from any Joyalukkas showroom in India at prevailing board rate on purchase day, applied to accumulated value.[^6][^16]

**Payment Options**
- Monthly payments can be made by cash, demand draft, debit card, credit card, NEFT, RTGS, UPI, local cheques, post‑dated cheques and online via www.joyalukkas.com.[^6]

**Redemption Rules**
- After completing 10 installments and on or after the 10th month, the customer can purchase eligible jewellery by availing scheme discounts.[^6]
- Redemption is in‑store only; online redemption is not currently offered.[^16][^6]
- Customers who have completed more than 180 days but less than 300 days can purchase jewellery by paying additional making charges; full discount eligibility applies only after defined maturity.[^6]
- If the scheme is not redeemed within a maximum period (e.g., up to around 345 days from enrolment), the accumulated amount may be transferred to the customer’s registered bank account, as per scheme terms.[^6]

**Limitations**
- Advance amounts do not earn interest.[^6]
- Failure to pay installments on time (beyond 60 days) can lead to discontinuation of the plan or loss of special discounts.[^6]
- Easy Gold scheme benefits cannot be clubbed with other showroom offers.[^6]
- Taxes and government levies (including GST) are borne by customers at the time of purchase.[^6]
- Excess purchase beyond scheme amount gets discount only on the scheme portion; no discount on the additional amount.[^6]
- Preclosure of enrollment renders customers ineligible for scheme discounts.[^6]

### 3.3 Cash Saving Scheme – Easy Gold Payment (Single)

**Eligibility & Duration**
- Requires a single advance payment of at least ₹1 lakh.[^16]
- Duration typically measured from premium receipt date, with purchase allowed from around the 300th day, redeemable at any showroom in India.[^16]

**Benefits**
- Enhanced making charge benefits relative to monthly scheme, including higher MC discount caps on gold (up to 20% off tag MC) and similar discounts on precious, rosecut and diamond jewellery.[^16]

Other payment, redemption and limitation terms broadly follow the same framework as the monthly Easy Gold Payment scheme, subject to brochure‑specific details.[^16][^6]

### 3.4 Weight Fix Scheme – Advance Smart Gold (Monthly)

**Eligibility & Duration**
- Minimum monthly slab from ₹5,000 onwards.[^16]
- Ten monthly installments; gold weight is fixed at the time of each payment, shielding customers from future gold rate increases.[^16]

**Benefits**
- Gold weight frozen at payment date; customers receive jewellery corresponding to accumulated gold weight at maturity.[^16]
- Making charge discounts similar in principle to Cash Saving schemes but with slightly lower MC caps in official tables (e.g., up to 14% off tag MC for gold in the monthly plan).[^16]

**Limitations and Payment Rules**
Payment options, redemption windows and limitations closely mirror the Cash Saving scheme, including no interest on advances, non‑clubbing with other offers and redemption in‑store only.[^16][^6]

### 3.5 Weight Fix Scheme – Advance Smart Gold (Single)

**Eligibility & Duration**
- Single advance payment plan from ₹1 lakh onwards, with gold weight fixed on premium receipt date.[^16]

**Benefits & Rules**
- Offers protection against gold price fluctuations, plus making charge and stone discounts similar to Advance Smart Gold monthly plan.[^16]
- Redemption and limitations follow general scheme rules, including in‑store redemption and adherence to specified time frames.[^6][^16]

### 3.6 Frequently Asked Scheme Questions (Condensed)

From official FAQ content on the jewellery purchase plan:[^16]

## 10. Knowledge Articles

Each article is kept within ~300–500 words for optimal RAG retrieval.

### 10.1 Gold Purity (18K, 22K, 24K)

Gold purity refers to the proportion of pure gold in an alloy. Jewellery is rarely made in 24 karat (24K) purity because pure gold is soft and prone to scratching and deformation. Instead, jewellers use alloys like 22K and 18K, which blend gold with other metals such as copper, silver and zinc to improve strength.[^28]

22K gold contains approximately 91.6% pure gold and 8.4% other metals. It is commonly used for traditional Indian jewellery such as necklaces, bangles, chains and bridal ornaments because it offers a good balance of richness in colour and durability for everyday wear. Hallmark stamps like “916” usually indicate 22K purity. 18K gold contains about 75% pure gold and 25% other metals. It is widely used for diamond jewellery, designer pieces and international‑style collections because the harder alloy holds gemstones more securely and supports intricate settings.[^28]

24K gold (99.9% purity) is typically sold as coins and bars for investment rather than for complex jewellery, although some simple pieces may be made from 24K in certain cultures. In India, gold jewellery sold by reputed brands like Joyalukkas will generally be hallmarked to certify purity in line with BIS norms, such as 22K or 18K, and customers receive invoices mentioning the karat, weight and making charges. The AI voice agent should always remind callers that exact purity for a specific piece is printed on its tag and invoice, and encourage them to confirm with the showroom for any item‑specific details.[^28][^4]

### 10.2 Hallmarking and BIS Compliance

Hallmarking is the official process used in India to certify the purity and fineness of gold jewellery and artefacts. The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) operates the hallmarking scheme, under which licensed jewellers can sell hallmarked jewellery carrying a BIS logo, purity mark (e.g., 22K 916) and identification marks of the assaying centre and jeweller. This system protects consumers from under‑karating and misrepresentation of gold content.[^28]

Major jewellery brands, including Joyalukkas, emphasize that their gold jewellery is hallmarked and complies with Indian standards, though detailed BIS registration numbers or centre IDs may not be published online. When a customer inquires about hallmarking, the AI voice agent can explain that hallmarked jewellery has undergone independent purity testing and carries visible marks on the piece. For any specific item, the agent should advise checking the hallmark on the jewellery itself and reviewing the invoice, which normally specifies karat value, net and gross weight and making charges.[^4]

If a customer suspects an issue with purity, the appropriate course is to request testing at an authorized assaying centre or raise a query at the store of purchase. Since hallmark policies can change over time with BIS regulations, the agent should refrain from giving outdated regulatory details and instead encourage contacting customer care or visiting official BIS resources for the latest norms.[^28]

### 10.3 Diamond 4Cs (Cut, Colour, Clarity, Carat)

Diamond quality is commonly described using the “4Cs”: Cut, Colour, Clarity and Carat weight. Cut refers to how well a diamond has been shaped and faceted, affecting its brilliance and sparkle. Excellent or ideal cuts reflect light efficiently, while poorly proportioned cuts appear dull even if the diamond is otherwise high quality. Colour grades range from D (colourless) to Z (light yellow or brown), with near‑colourless stones being more valuable than visibly tinted ones.[^28]

Clarity describes the presence of inclusions (internal features) and blemishes (surface features). Grades such as IF (internally flawless), VVS, VS, SI and I indicate increasing visibility of inclusions under magnification and sometimes to the naked eye. Carat is the unit of diamond weight; larger stones are rarer and more expensive, but two diamonds of the same carat can vary significantly in value depending on cut, colour and clarity. Reputed jewellers often sell certified diamonds accompanied by grading reports from labs like IGI or GIA, which record the 4Cs for each stone.[^28]

When customers ask about diamond quality at Joyalukkas, the AI voice agent should explain the 4Cs conceptually and emphasize that actual grading for a piece is specified on its certificate and invoice. The agent must avoid guessing the 4Cs for any item without accessing the certificate and should direct customers to view the diamond report or consult in‑store experts for detailed comparisons and recommendations.[^4]

### 10.4 Jewellery Care – Cleaning and Storage

Proper jewellery care extends the life and appearance of gold, diamond, silver and gemstone pieces. For everyday cleaning of gold and diamond jewellery, customers can soak items briefly in a mild soap solution, gently brush with a soft toothbrush to remove dirt, and rinse thoroughly with clean water before patting dry with a lint‑free cloth. Chemicals such as bleach, chlorine and harsh detergents should be avoided, as they can damage metals and loosen stones over time.[^28]

Storage is equally important. Jewellery should be kept in soft pouches or lined boxes, ideally with individual compartments to prevent scratching and tangling. Gold and diamond pieces are best stored separately from silver and costume jewellery to minimize chemical reactions and tarnish. Customers should remove jewellery before swimming, exercising or doing heavy manual work to avoid exposure to sweat, impact and abrasive surfaces. For delicate pieces with gemstones, professional periodic cleaning and inspection at a trusted jeweller is recommended to check for loose settings or wear on clasps.[^28]

The AI voice agent can share these general care tips while clearly stating that complex cleaning (e.g., ultrasonic cleaning, polishing or replating) should be done by professionals. For any item under warranty or purchased from Joyalukkas, customers should be encouraged to bring it to the showroom for tailored care advice and services.[^4]

### 10.5 Gold Investment Basics

Gold is widely regarded as a store of value and hedge against inflation, especially in India where physical gold plays a cultural and financial role. Investment options include jewellery, coins and bars, and financial instruments like gold ETFs and sovereign gold bonds. Jewellery offers emotional and aesthetic value but carries making charges and design premiums; coins and bars, typically sold at close to bullion rates with minimal making charges, are better for pure investment.[^28]

When buying gold for investment at a jeweller, customers should check purity (karat), hallmarking, weight and buyback policies. 24K coins and bars are preferred for straightforward valuation, while 22K jewellery may incur deductions on making charges and stone weight during buyback or exchange. Joyalukkas sells gold and silver coins and bars, which can be purchased directly or through its gold schemes, giving customers flexibility to accumulate value over time.[^28][^6]

The AI voice agent should avoid offering financial advice or predictions on gold prices and instead focus on explaining product types and generic considerations. For investment decisions, customers should consult financial advisors or do independent research, and for buyback specifics they must refer to official Joyalukkas buyback policies and discuss terms with the showroom.[^11]

### 10.6 Wedding Jewellery Buying Guide (High‑Level)

Wedding jewellery planning typically starts with deciding the overall budget and prioritizing key pieces such as the bridal necklace set, long haram, earrings, bangles, mangalsutra and sometimes armlets, waist belts and anklets, depending on regional customs. Brides should consider factors like outfit colours, ceremony themes (traditional vs. contemporary), gold purity, and the mix of plain gold and stone‑set pieces. Heavy temple jewellery or heritage designs work well for main ceremonies, while lighter sets can be reserved for receptions and pre‑wedding events.[^5]

At Joyalukkas, brides often explore specialized bridal collections such as Sita Kalyanam and Krishna Leela for temple jewellery, alongside branded lines like Eleganza, Apurva and Pride for statement designs. ### 10.7 Diamond Buying Guide (High‑Level)

Buying diamond jewellery involves balancing quality and budget using the 4Cs, metal type and design preferences. Customers should first decide whether they want solitaire‑style pieces (single large stones) or multi‑stone designs, and whether the metal will be gold (often 18K) or platinum. - Exact jewellery prices, discounts, or offers.
- Store opening/closing times for specific dates.
- Exchange, return, refund or buyback percentages and eligibility beyond high‑level descriptions.
- Availability of particular SKUs, sizes or collection pieces.
- Warranty coverage, lifetime maintenance promises, or insurance terms.
- Hallmark or diamond certification lab details for individual items.

### 15.2 When to Transfer to a Human

Automatic transfer to a human agent is required when:
- The customer requests any monetary valuation (price, buyback amount, exchange value).
- The call involves an unresolved complaint or legal threat.
- The customer disputes a policy or demands exceptions.
- The customer is planning a large purchase (e.g., wedding sets) and wants detailed design/price guidance.
- The caller shows signs of distress or confusion that require empathetic human handling.

### 15.3 Compliance and Privacy Requirements

The AI voice agent should:
- Collect only necessary personal data (name, phone, email, city, order ID) for service and escalation.
- Inform callers that their data may be used to contact them about their request and will be handled in line with Joyalukkas’ privacy policy.[^16]
- Avoid storing sensitive payment details (card numbers, CVV, OTP); instead, direct customers to secure payment gateways.

### 15.4 Handling Abusive Callers

If a caller uses abusive or offensive language:
- Respond calmly, stating that respectful communication is required to continue the call.
- If abuse continues, inform the caller that the call may be terminated and offer to connect them to a supervisor if appropriate.

### 15.5 Handling Unknown Questions

- Acknowledge the limitation: “I’m sorry, that information is not publicly available or I don’t have access to it right now.”

### 15.6 Handling Pricing Requests

For any pricing request:
- Provide only generic information about factors influencing price (gold rate, weight, making charges, design complexity).[^28][^6]
- Refrain from quoting actual rupee amounts.
- Offer to connect to the nearest showroom or online sales team.

### 15.7 Handling Complaints and Refunds

When customers complain about products or demand refunds:
- Listen, acknowledge the issue and collect details.
- Explain that refund and return policies differ by product and region and require human review.[^17]
- Create a complaint ticket and escalate to appropriate support teams.
