What a platform gives you

What you get
out of the box.

Enterprise conversational-AI platforms — the category Kore.ai, Cognigy and Yellow.ai compete in — sell a genuinely useful thing: a configurable builder you don't have to write from scratch.

01 / SPEED

A faster start.

Pre-built flows, templates and connectors mean a standard use case can be configured rather than built, which gets something live faster than a ground-up project.

02 / MAINTAINED PRODUCT

Someone else runs the underlying platform.

Model upgrades, uptime and the core product roadmap are the vendor's problem, not yours. You configure; they maintain the engine underneath.

03 / STANDARD CONNECTORS

Built-in integrations for common systems.

Standard CRM, helpdesk and telephony connectors cover a lot of ground for a business running mainstream systems in a fairly standard configuration.

All three vendors position themselves this way on their own sites.
Checked directly against each vendor's own homepage (2026-10-05): kore.ai describes itself as an "Enterprise Agentic AI Platform"; cognigy.com (now NiCE Cognigy) as powering "enterprise customer service with generative and conversational AI agents"; yellow.ai as "AI Agents for CX and EX Automation."

What custom buys you

What a custom build
actually gives you.

The two things a platform's generic connector most often can't reach: your exact system, and your exact voice.

01 / DEEP ERP/SAP INTEGRATION

Built for your instance, not a generic one.

A non-standard module, a custom approval chain, or write access gated on rules a generic connector wasn't designed to express — a custom build is scoped directly against your system as it actually runs.

02 / BRAND-TRAINED VOICE

Trained on your tone, not a configured persona.

Where the voice itself is part of the product — a premium brand, a regulated industry with specific phrasing rules — training directly on your own examples goes further than configuring a platform's persona settings.

03 / NO PLATFORM CEILING

Escalation and logic shaped to your process.

Exception handling, multi-step approval and unusual escalation paths can be built exactly as your business runs them, rather than adapted to fit a platform's workflow builder.

Where a platform is genuinely the better call

When we'd tell you
to buy the platform instead.

We build the custom side of this comparison, which is a reason to be specific about when that's the wrong answer.

  1. 01

    Your use case fits a standard template.

    If a platform's pre-built flow already covers what you need, configuring it is faster and cheaper than commissioning a custom build for the same outcome.

  2. 02

    You need to be live in days, not weeks.

    A platform's speed advantage is real for a time-boxed launch where "good enough, now" beats "exactly right, later."

  3. 03

    No one owns an ongoing custom integration.

    A custom build needs a vendor relationship that continues after launch. If that ownership isn't in place, a maintained platform product is the safer bet.

Before you choose

The practical
questions.

Is a custom-built AI agent always better than a platform?

No. A platform is genuinely the better call when your use case is close to its standard templates, you need to be live in days rather than weeks, and no one on your team can own an ongoing integration. A custom build earns its cost when the integration or the brand voice is the actual product, not an afterthought.

What's the real difference between buying a platform and building custom?

A platform gives you a configurable builder, pre-built connectors and a support team that maintains the underlying product. A custom build gives you an agent shaped around your specific ERP or SAP data, your own escalation rules and a voice trained on your brand, with your vendor responsible for the parts a platform's generic connector doesn't reach.

Can a platform like Kore.ai, Cognigy or Yellow.ai do deep ERP integration too?

Platforms in this category do offer integration tooling and connectors, and for standard systems that can be enough. The gap shows up with a non-standard ERP configuration, a workflow specific to your business, or write access with approval rules a generic connector wasn't built to express — that's where a custom-built integration tends to take over.

Why not just use a platform and customise it heavily?

You can, up to a point. Heavy customisation inside a platform still runs on top of someone else's architecture and release cycle, so deep changes can be constrained by what the platform exposes. A custom build has no such ceiling, at the cost of being the one responsible for maintaining it.

How does VoxDonna fit into this comparison?

VoxDonna builds the custom side of this comparison: agents trained on a specific brand voice, wired into a specific ERP or SAP instance, with escalation rules set by you rather than a platform default. For a use case that's closer to a standard template, we'll say so and point you at a platform instead.

What does a custom AI agent cost compared to a platform subscription?

A platform is typically a recurring licence plus configuration time. A custom build is a build cost plus ongoing running cost, scoped to your integrations and volume. Which is cheaper depends entirely on how close your use case is to the platform's standard templates — we'll tell you honestly if a platform is the cheaper fit.
