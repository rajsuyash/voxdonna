The aftersales desk problem

Your best product person repeats the catalogue fifty times a month.

None of this work is hard. All of it is relentless, and it lands on the four people who were doing something else.

🗓️

The season is the business, and it is eight weeks long

Enquiries stack up between March and June and again before the holidays, and the people who can answer them are the same people packing orders. Hiring for eight weeks and training for four is not a plan, it is a tax.

🔥

Pre-sales questions are technical, and nobody junior can answer them

Surface size against party size, kW against cooking zones, butane against propane, chrome-plated against enamelled against stainless. The answers never change, and your best product person repeats them fifty times a month.

⚠️

"Is it rated for indoors?" is a compliance question in a sales question's coat

Gas models need ventilation an indoor kitchen cannot give them, and electrical models need the right supply. Get it wrong on the phone and you have a liability, not a sale.

🔩

Spare parts calls are small, urgent and relentless

A burner kit, an ignition module, a drip tray, a replacement grate. Each one is a two-minute conversation and a serial number, and every one of them interrupts somebody who was doing something else.

🧾

Warranty starts with a registration nobody completes

Registration windows lapse, serial numbers go unrecorded, and the first time you hear about a unit is when it fails. Then the claim arrives with no proof of purchase and a customer who is already annoyed.

🌍

Export markets call in languages your desk does not staff

The UK, Italy and Spain buy, and the support desk sits in one country speaking one language. Callers who cannot be served in their own language do not leave a voicemail. They buy somebody else's grill.

What the AI employee does

From the first ring to the shipped burner kit.

Three chains it owns end to end, on the systems you already run. Each one ends in a record, not a callback.

Chain 01

Pre-sales model selection to order, in the caller's language

  1. Inbound call or WhatsApp lands on the brand number. The agent detects EN, FR or IT from the first few seconds of speech and stays in that language.
  2. Qualifies usage: indoor or outdoor, party size, fuel available, balcony or garden or restaurant.
  3. Retrieves the matching models from the product knowledge base and gives surface size, zones, power and fuel.
  4. Checks live availability against the ERP or the ecommerce stock feed.
  5. Flags the compliance rule out loud when it applies: gas models outdoor only, this model indoor-rated, this one needs a conversion kit.
  6. Takes the order details, or books a call with the sales team in the shared calendar.
  7. Writes the enquiry, the model shortlist and the language preference into the CRM.
  8. Sends the spec sheet and the care guide by WhatsApp or email, in the same language.
Chain 02

Spare part identified, priced, shipped

  1. Caller gives the model and, where they have it, the serial number.
  2. The agent identifies the unit, or walks the caller through where to find the serial on the chassis.
  3. Cross-references the part against the parts catalogue and confirms fitment across the model range.
  4. Checks stock and lead time in the ERP or the warehouse system.
  5. Quotes the part, the fitting time, and whether it is a do-it-yourself job or needs a technician.
  6. Takes payment or raises the order in the ecommerce backend, shipping to the address on file after confirming it.
  7. Opens a ticket in the helpdesk with the call transcript attached.
  8. Sends tracking by WhatsApp when the carrier updates, without anybody asking.
Chain 03

Warranty registration and claim intake

  1. After delivery, an outbound WhatsApp or call asks the customer to register: serial number, purchase date, dealer.
  2. Validates the serial against the production or ERP record and confirms the cover.
  3. Writes the registration to the warranty register and confirms it back in writing.
  4. On a later failure call, retrieves that registration instead of asking for a receipt.
  5. Captures the symptom, the model, the fault description and photos over WhatsApp.
  6. Pre-codes the claim against the fault categories your back office already uses.
  7. Routes it: in-warranty and clear-cut goes to the claims queue with everything attached, disputed or out-of-warranty or a safety concern goes straight to a named human.
  8. Books the technician or dispatches the part, and updates the CRM and the helpdesk record.
  9. Follows up seven days later to confirm the unit is working.

Adjudication stays with a person. The agent assembles the claim. It does not approve money and it does not accept liability, because those are not decisions you hand to software. WhatsApp agents for delivery and claim photos carry the picture half of that chain.

Questions we get

Questions cooking appliance manufacturers ask first.

Q

Can an AI agent handle the call spike during barbecue season without extra staff?

Yes, and that is the point. Volume in March through June is the same four questions at five times the frequency. The agent takes the repeat tier, which is most of it, and the calls that need a product expert reach one faster because the queue in front of them is gone. You staff for your normal month, not your worst week.

Q

Can it answer technical pre-sales questions about gas versus electric models?

It answers what your documentation says. Surface size against party size, kW against cooking zones, butane against propane, which models are rated for indoor use and which need a conversion kit. It works from your catalogue, spec sheets and care guides, so the answer on the phone matches the answer on the box.

Q

Will it tell a customer something unsafe about gas or electrical installation?

It is built not to improvise on that. Ventilation, fuel conversion and electrical supply answers come from your own documentation, and anything outside it, or anything that reads like a safety concern, goes to a named person immediately. Safety questions are an escalation rule, not a confidence score.

Q

Can it take a warranty claim over the phone and file it correctly?

It takes the claim and assembles it: model, serial, purchase date, dealer of record, symptom description and photos over WhatsApp, pre-coded against your fault categories. Your back office gets a complete file instead of a voicemail. Adjudication stays with a human, because approving money and accepting liability are not decisions to automate.

Q

How do we support customers in France, the UK and Italy from one support desk?

One agent, one knowledge base, three languages. The caller's language is detected from the first seconds of speech and the agent stays in it, switching mid-call if the customer asks. You maintain your documentation once, in your primary language, and the agent answers from it in the others. There is a live demo of exactly this on the site.

Q

Can it handle spare parts orders for models we stopped making years ago?

If the part is in your catalogue, yes. The agent identifies the unit from the serial number or walks the caller through finding it, confirms fitment across the model range, checks stock and lead time in your ERP, and raises the order. Discontinued units are usually the easiest, because the fitment table already exists.

Q

How long does it take to get one live on our number?

Two weeks from kickoff, which is the standard stated on every demo we publish. Most of that is your content, not our build: the catalogue, the care guides, the warranty terms and an export of your top fifty support questions. Pricing is per minute used, so a quiet February costs what a quiet February should.
