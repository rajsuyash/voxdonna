Natural, modern spoken Telugu for a voice agent should follow everyday “vyavahārika Telugu” patterns, with short, warm sentences, selective English mixing, neutral pan-regional vocabulary, and మీరు-based respectful speech rather than literary or over-formal Telugu.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-telangana-and-andhra-dialects-of-telugu/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]

## **1\. Spoken vs written Telugu**

Spoken Telugu used in daily conversation (“vyavahārika Telugu”) is significantly simpler than literary or formal “granthikam Telugu,” which follows stricter morphology and more Sanskritized vocabulary. Classical and formal writing tends to use standardized Telugu, while popular media and everyday speech favor regional and colloquial forms with contractions and relaxed grammar.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]\[[academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/book/32293/chapter/268506537)\]\[[conservancy.umn](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9192c46f-29a7-4ed6-8191-49125ccaecaa/content)\]

A key distinction: spoken Telugu drops case markers, uses short verbs, and mixes English naturally, whereas written Telugu keeps full verb forms and pure vocabulary.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]

### **Formal → Spoken → English examples**

| మీరు ఎక్కడ నివసిస్తున్నారు? | మీరు ఎక్కడ ఉంటారు? | Where do you stay/live? |
| మీకు ఎలా సహాయం చేయగలను? | నేను ఎలా help చేయగలను? / మీకు ఎలా help చెయ్యాలి? | How can I help you? |

## **2\. Core sentence structure in speech**

Spoken Telugu is usually SOV (subject–object–verb), but subjects and pronouns are frequently dropped when obvious, and auxiliary verbs are shortened. Questions often end in \-ా? or rising intonation rather than full question forms.\[[en.sorumatik](https://en.sorumatik.co/t/what-are-you-doing-in-telugu/71368)\]

Common patterns:

* Statement: Subject (often dropped) \+ object \+ verb  
  * మీ order ready అయింది. – Your order is ready.  
* Question: Object/topic \+ verb \+ question ending  
  * payment అయిందా? – Has the payment gone through?  
* Yes/no confirmation: clause \+ కదా / గదా / కదండి  
  * Vijayawada నుండి shipment కదా? – It’s shipment from Vijayawada, right?

---

## **3\. Contractions, dropped words, natural word order**

Spoken Telugu heavily uses contractions and omission:

* Pronouns often dropped:  
  * (మీరు) ఏ branch లో ఉన్నారు? → ఏ branch లో ఉన్నారు? – Which branch are you in?  
* Auxiliary shortening: చేస్తున్నారు → చేస్తున్నారు → చేస్తున్నారు (spoken) or చేస్తున్నరు (Telangana) in fast speech.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-telangana-and-andhra-dialects-of-telugu/)\]  
* Question lighteners: కదా, గదా, అంటారా, చెప్పగలరా, చెప్తారా.

Natural word order prefers topic-first:

* Phone number అడగడం:  
  * మీ phone number చెప్తారా? (topic “phone number” first, then verb “tell”).  
* Clarifying:  
  * delivery date గురించే కదా? – It’s about the delivery date, right?

## **4\. How questions are phrased**

Spoken questions favor short, direct forms, often with మీరు / నువ్వు choice depending on politeness.\[[blimto](https://blimto.com/learn/learn-telugu-language/learn-telugu-language-telugu-pronouns)\]\[[en.sorumatik](https://en.sorumatik.co/t/what-are-you-doing-in-telugu/71368)\]

Patterns:

* “What” questions: ఏమి / ఏమిటి / ఏంటి  
  * మీకు ఏ problem? – What’s the problem?  
* “Which” questions: ఏ / ఏది  
  * ఏ product గురించి మాట్లాడుతున్నారు? – Which product are you talking about?  
* “How” questions: ఎలా  
  * నేను ఎలా help చేయాలి? – How should I help?

Softening questions:

* చెప్తారా?, చెప్పగలరా?, అడగచ్చా?, చెప్పండి ఓసారి  
  * మీ address ఓసారి చెప్తారా? – Could you tell me your address once?

## **5\. Requests, agreement, disagreement, acknowledgements**

Requests are softened with చెప్తారా, వేచి ఉండండి, కొంచెం, ఒక minute, ప్లీజ్, sir/madam in customer-service contexts. Agreement uses అవును / అవునండి / అవును sir; disagreement uses కాదు, ఇంకా కాదు, కుదరదు, మా side నుంచి కుదరదు.\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]

Typical acknowledgement patterns:

* హం, అవును, సరే, okay, right, బాగుంది – listener is following.  
* అర్థమైంది, అర్థమైంది sir – “Got it.”  
* అవునండి, మీరు చెప్పింది correct – “Yes, what you said is correct.”

Natural sentence endings:

* కదా / గదా – seeking confirmation.  
* అండి /ండి – polite softener (esp. Andhra, neutral).  
* రా /రే – informal, avoid for customer-agent.

## **6\. Tone: familiarity vs respect**

Telugu uses pronouns and verb endings to mark respect: నువ్వు (informal), మీరు (respect), with plural/respect verbs like చేస్తున్నారు, వచ్చారా, ఇస్తారా. Telangana speech also relaxes endings and uses more contractions, but మీరు-based polite speech remains standard in service contexts.\[[en.sorumatik](https://en.sorumatik.co/t/what-are-you-doing-in-telugu/71368)\]\[[blimto](https://blimto.com/learn/learn-telugu-language/learn-telugu-language-telugu-pronouns)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-telangana-and-andhra-dialects-of-telugu/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]

For a neutral customer-service agent:

* Default to మీరు, not నువ్వు.  
* Use polite verbs: చెప్తారా, ఇస్తారా, చూస్తారా, వేచిచూడగలరా.  
* Use sir/madam sparingly; use గారు after names or “customer గారు” when appropriate, not in every sentence.

## **8\. Natural fillers and conversation markers**

Spoken Telugu uses a rich set of fillers that signal listening, hesitation, surprise, or empathy. Telangana speech especially uses more relaxed, melodic intonation and colloquial fillers, but a neutral agent should keep them sparse and non-mechanical.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-telangana-and-andhra-dialects-of-telugu/)\]

### **Key fillers**

| Expression | Meaning / function | Usage & emotional tone | Register | When an AI should avoid |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| హం / హ్మ్ | Hmm, I see | Light acknowledgement while listening | Neutral | Avoid repeating every turn |
| అా / అబ్బా | Ah / oh | Mild surprise or realization | Casual | Avoid “అబ్బా” in serious contexts |
| అయ్యో | Oh no / oh dear | Empathy, mild shock | Casual-warm | Avoid for very formal or serious info (e.g., bank disclosures) |
| సరే | Okay | Agreement / transition | Neutral | Don’t use as first word in every reply |
| అవును | Yes | Agreement | Neutral | None, but don’t chain (“అవును, సరే, బాగుంది”) too often |
| అవునా? | Oh really? / is it so? | Surprise \+ confirmation | Neutral | Avoid sounding judgmental |
| అలాగా? | Is that so? / really? | Mild curiosity/surprise | Neutral | Avoid after very bad news (sounds too casual) |
| బాగుంది | Good / okay | Positive assessment | Warm | Avoid if customer clearly upset |
| అర్ధమైంది | Got it | Cognitive acknowledgement | Neutral | Fine; don’t overuse |
| చూద్దాం | Let’s see | Hesitation, checking | Neutral | Avoid if you already have clear info |
| ఒక నిమిషం | One moment | Short pause marker | Neutral | Fine, just don’t repeat immediately |
| మరి | So / then | Discourse marker, moving topic | Neutral–slightly informal | Don’t use excessively; one “మరి…” per topic shift is enough |
| అయితే | In that case / then | Contrast / consequence marker | Neutral | Avoid starting every sentence with “అయితే” |
| actually | Actually | Correction / clarification | English mixing | Fine but not every turn |
| right | Right | Agreement | English | Use occasionally after explanation |

#### **Usage notes for specific markers**

* సరే / sare  
  * Used to accept or acknowledge: “సరే, నేను check చేస్తాను.”  
  * Also used to close a topic: “సరే, అది complete అయింది.”  
* అవును / avunu  
  * Simple yes; extended to అవునండి for politeness.  
  * As a stand-alone answer, use with short follow-up: “అవును sir, కనిపిస్తోంది.”  
* అవునా? / avunaa?  
  * Surprise \+ confirmation: “అవునా? delivery miss అయిందా?”  
* అలాగా? / alagaa?  
  * Soft surprise: “అలాగా? address change అయ్యిందా?”  
* అయ్యో / ayyo  
  * Empathetic reaction: “అయ్యో, మీ parcel damage అయిందా?”  
  * Use sparingly in customer-service, mostly when showing empathy.  
* అయితే / ayithē  
  * Logical connector: “అయితే, కొత్త order place చేస్తామా?”  
* మరి / mari  
  * Topic shift: “మరి, payment గురించి చెప్తారా?”

Hesitation patterns:

* “హం… ఒక్క నిమిషం… చూసి చెప్తాను.”  
* “ఒక్కసారి చూస్తాను… హ్మ్… మీ number అక్కడ లేదు.”

Use such patterns occasionally, not in every response, and keep them short.

## **9\. Telugu–English code switching in urban AP & Telangana**

Modern Telugu in cities like Hyderabad, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam, etc., keeps many business and tech terms in English, embedding them into Telugu grammar. English terms for “order,” “payment,” “address,” “message,” “website,” “WhatsApp,” etc., are routinely used inside Telugu sentences, especially in customer service and phone conversations.\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]

### **Typical English words kept in English**

Appointment, booking, order, payment, delivery, address, phone number, customer care, website, WhatsApp, email, store, product, offer, discount, date, time, confirmation, cancel, reschedule, refund, exchange, online, location, message, call, account, OTP, ID.\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]

### **Natural code-switching patterns**

Patterns similar to the following are widely attested in contemporary speech:\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]

* booking చేసారా? – Have you done the booking?  
* payment అయిందా? – Has the payment gone through?  
* order place చేశారా? – Have you placed the order?  
* message పంపండి / message పంపించండి – Please send a message.  
* appointment confirm చేస్తాను – I’ll confirm the appointment.  
* delivery status check చేస్తాను – I’ll check the delivery status.  
* refund process చేస్తాము – We’ll process the refund.

These follow Telugu verb-light \+ English noun/verb “native” patterns (light verb like చేయడం/ చేసుకోవడం around English root).

## **11\. Politeness and respect (నువ్వు vs మీరు, గారు, sir/madam)**

Telugu pronouns and verb forms encode respect: నువ్వు is informal singular “you,” మీరు is plural/respect “you” with polite verbs like చేస్తున్నారు, వస్తారా, చెప్తారా. In customer-facing contexts, మీరు is the safe default for adults, regardless of age, unless the customer clearly uses very informal language and would expect matching casual tone.\[[blimto](https://blimto.com/learn/learn-telugu-language/learn-telugu-language-telugu-pronouns)\]\[[en.sorumatik](https://en.sorumatik.co/t/what-are-you-doing-in-telugu/71368)\]\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]

### **Practical politeness rules**

* Default pronoun: మీరు.  
  * మీరు ఎలా ఉన్నారు? – polite.  
* Avoid నువ్వు unless: peer-level friend talk, or when user clearly speaks that way and expects it.  
* Use గారు after names or roles sparingly:  
  * Ramesh గారు, మీ order dispatch అయింది.  
  * Customer గారు, మీ complaint register చేశాం.  
* Don’t say గారు in every sentence; it sounds artificial and obsequious.  
* sir/madam:  
  * Common in CS; “sir” more frequent than “గారు” in urban calls.  
  * Use “sir / madam” once in a turn, not after every word.

Respectful verb forms:

* చెప్తారా, ఇస్తారా, అడగచ్చా, రాగలరా, వేచి ఉండగలరా – respectful, natural.  
* Avoid ultra-formal: “తెలియజేయగలరా,” “నిర్ధారించగలరా” unless in financial/legal context.

Default style for your agent:

* Adult voice, warm but professional.  
* మీరు \+ polite verbs.  
* Mix English where natural (order, payment, delivery).  
* Use sir/madam or గారు occasionally, not mechanically.

## **12\. Andhra vs Telangana Telugu (for a neutral agent)**

Research shows notable differences between Andhra and Telangana dialects in pronunciation, vocabulary, and informal grammar. Telangana Telugu tends to be softer, more melodic, and incorporates more Urdu/Hindi words like nakko (no/don’t want), hau (yes), baasha (language), while coastal Andhra leans on different borrowings and slightly crisper pronunciation.\[[academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/book/32293/chapter/268506537)\]\[[srikaram](https://srikaram.org/differences-between-andhra-telugu-and-telangana-telugu/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-telangana-and-andhra-dialects-of-telugu/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]

Key distinctions relevant to a voice agent:

* Pronunciation: Telangana often softens ch/j (చ / జ) and uses more nasal intonation; Andhra is crisper.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-telangana-and-andhra-dialects-of-telugu/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]  
* Vocabulary: Telangana: nakko (నక్కో – don’t want), baasha, hau; Andhra: vēḍu, bhāṣa, avunu.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]  
* Sentence endings: Telangana more likely to use “రా / రే” in informal, and colloquial particles; Andhra more “అండి /ండి” in polite casual.

For a pan-Telugu agent:

* Prefer neutral forms: అవును, లేదు, పర్లేదు, సరే, బాగుంది.  
* Avoid strong slang like “nakko,” “em ayithindi ra,” “baap re,” “asalu tension nakko” unless the customer uses them first.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]  
* Prefer “అండి /ండి” endings over “రా /రే” in CS.

Labeling:

* Neutral Telugu: అవును, సరే, బాగుంది, పర్లేదు, ఎలా ఉన్నారు?  
* Andhra-leaning: బాగున్నారా?, ఏమంటారు అండి?, “అబ్బా” in some contexts.  
* Telangana-specific: nakko, hau, baasha, “em ayindi ra?” – avoid in neutral agent.  
* Hyderabad-specific: mixed Hindi/Urdu (“accha,” “theek hai,” “haan,” “chalo”) – use only if caller strongly using them.

## **13\. Hyderabad & Telangana mixing (Telugu \+ English \+ Hindi/Urdu)**

Hyderabad and Telangana dialects historically absorbed many Urdu and Hindi elements due to Nizam-era and Deccan contacts. Everyday speech may include accha, haan, nahi, theek hai, chalo, bas, problem, tension, confirm, call, message, especially in casual contexts and younger speakers.\[[academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/book/32293/chapter/268506537)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]

Examples of mixed speech:

* accha, payment ho gaya? – mix Hindi with English verb.  
* haan, problem ledu. – mixture of Hindi “haan” and Telugu “leḍu.”  
* theek hai, message chestha. – Hindi “theek hai” plus Telugu “cheyadam” on English “message.”

For a neutral agent:

* Widely understood: accha, theek hai, haan, chalo, problem, tension – many Telugu speakers understand them.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]  
* Appropriate for neutral AI: Use English “okay,” “problem,” but avoid Hindi fillers unless the customer uses them heavily.  
* Better avoided: heavy Hindi/Urdu mixing (accha, theek hai, nakko, hau) unless you detect similar style in user.

## **14\. Pronunciation & speech guidance**

Research on Telugu phonology indicates initial-syllable stress and importance of vowel length, retroflex consonants, and optional vowel lengthening in coordination. In practice, native-sounding speech for a voice agent should focus on:\[[arts.osmania.ac](http://arts.osmania.ac.in/Journals/Linguistics/Volume%2019.pdf)\]\[[archive](https://archive.org/stream/dli.language.1602/dli.language.1602_djvu.txt)\]

### **Vowels (length & clarity)**

* Distinguish long vs short: అ / ఆ, ఇ / ఈ, ఉ / ఊ, ఎ / ఏ, ఒ / ఓ.  
  * Example: పడి (fell) vs పాడి (sang) – mis-length changes meaning.  
* Keep vowels clear, not reduced as in English (no schwa-like reductions).

### **Consonants**

* Retroflex: ట, డ, ణ, ఱ – pronounced with tongue curled back; avoid replacing with dental t/d (like Hindi or English).  
* Aspirated vs non-aspirated: Telugu has fewer aspirated stops; don’t over-aspirate k, t, p like in English.  
* Double consonants: stress (కక్కర, అక్క, బొమ్మ) – keep gemination audible.

### **Rhythm & intonation**

* Word-initial syllable often stressed; overall rhythm is syllable-timed rather than stress-timed.\[[archive](https://archive.org/stream/dli.language.1602/dli.language.1602_djvu.txt)\]  
* Sentence-final intonation:  
  * Statements: falling tone.  
  * Yes/no questions: slight rising tone with \-ా? or \-కదా?  
  * Respectful questions: polite rise with మీరు-forms.

### **Common non-native artifacts**

* Hindi speaker speaking Telugu:  
  * Overuse of Hindi aspirated consonants, Hindi fillers (achha, theek hai) without Telugu verbs.  
* English speaker reading Telugu:  
  * Flat intonation, reading every word fully without contractions, mis-lengthening vowels.  
* Newsreader style:  
  * Over-clear segmentation, no reductions, formal vocabulary.  
* TTS-like:  
  * Consistent pitch and timing, no micro-pauses or natural hesitations.

Guidance for TTS / prompting:

* Encourage slight reductions: “చేస్తున్నారు” → “చేస్తున్నారు/చేస్తున్నారు” in speech.  
* Use short chunks: 6–10 syllables per breath for CS lines.  
* Insert micro-pauses before key info, e.g., “కొంచెం wait చేయండి… మీ order status చూస్తున్నాను.”

## **15\. Conversational patterns (mini-dialogues: robotic vs natural)**

Here are sample mini-dialogues for key scenarios. Bad versions are overly formal or literal; preferred versions are natural, few-shot ready.

### **15.1 Greeting**

Bad:

* Agent: “శుభోదయం గారు, మీకు సేవ చేయుటకు సిద్ధంగా ఉన్నాను.”

Natural:

* Agent: “హలో, \[Company\] customer care నుండి మాట్లాడుతున్నాను. నేను ఎలా help చేయగలను మీకు?”  
* Customer: “నాకు delivery గురించి doubt ఉంది.”

### **15.2 Asking how you can help**

Bad:

* Agent: “మీరు ఏ విధమైన సమస్యను ఎదుర్కొంటున్నారో వివరిస్తే, నేను తగిన చర్యలు తీసుకుంటాను.”

Natural:

* Agent: “ఏ problem ఉందో కొంచెం చెప్తారా? నేను చూసి help చేస్తాను.”  
* Customer: “order status తెలుసుకోవాలి.”

### **15.3 Product enquiry**

Bad:

* Agent: “మీ ఆదేశిత ఉత్పత్తి లభ్యతను పరిశీలిస్తున్నాను.”

Natural:

* Agent: “మీరు ఏ product గురించి అడుగుతున్నారు? పేరు చెప్తారా?”  
* Customer: “\[Product\] గూర్చి.”

### **15.4 Appointment booking**

Bad:

* Agent: “మీ అపాయింట్‌మెంట్ వివరాలను వ్యవస్థలో నమోదు చేయుచున్నాను.”

Natural:

* Agent: “మీకు ఏ date, ఏ time లో appointment కావాలి? చెప్తారా?”  
* Customer: “రేపు సాయం కాలం.”

### **15.5 Order-status enquiry**

Bad:

* Agent: “మీ ఆర్డరు పంపిణీ స్థితినిపై సమాచారం అందజేయుచున్నాను.”

* Agent: “మీ order number చెప్తారా? చూసి status చెప్తాను.”  
* Customer: “\[Order number\].”

### **15.6 Delivery issue**

Bad:

* Agent: “డెలివరీలో లోపం సంభవించినట్లు కనిపిస్తోంది.”

* Agent: “అయ్యో, delivery లో issue వచ్చిందా? ఏమైంది exactly చెప్తారా?”

### **15.7 Payment issue**

### **15.8 Refund enquiry**

Bad:

* Agent: “మీ రీఫండ్ ప్రక్రియ కొనసాగుతోంది, దయచేసి ఓపిక వహించండి.”

Natural:

* Agent: “మీ refund process లో ఉంది, 3–5 working days పడుతుంది. update రావగానే మీకు message వస్తుంది.”

### **15.9 Complaint**

Bad:

* Agent: “మీ ఫిర్యాదును నమోదు చేసి సంబంధిత విభాగానికి పంపించుచున్నాము.”

Natural:

* Agent: “మీ complaint register చేశాం, related team కి forward చేస్తాం. result వచ్చిన వెంటనే మీకు call / message వస్తుంది.”

### **15.10 Customer doesn’t understand / agent doesn’t understand**

Bad:

* Agent: “మీకు వివరణ స్పష్టంగా లేకపోతే, దయచేసి మళ్లీ చదవండి.”

Natural:

* Agent: “అర్థం కాలేదా? పర్లేదు, నేను ఇంకోసారి simple గా explain చేస్తాను.”

Bad:

* Agent: “మీరు చెప్పినది నాకు గ్రహించబడలేదు.”

Natural:

* Agent: “నాకు పూర్తిగా అర్థం కాలేదు, మీరు చివరి part మళ్లీ చెప్తారా?”

## **16\. Turn-taking & voice conversation behaviour**

Turn-taking in spoken Telugu uses short acknowledgements before answers, clear signals while checking, and polite interruption markers.\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]

### **Key behaviours**

* Acknowledge before answering:  
  * “అవునండి… మీ order గురించి కదా?”  
  * “హం… అర్థమైంది, నేను check చేస్తాను.”  
* While checking info:  
  * “కొంచెం wait చేయండి, system open అవుతోంది.”  
  * “ఒక్క నిమిషం… details చూస్తున్నాను.”  
* Interrupt politely:  
  * “ఒకసారి… మీ phone number చివరి digits మళ్లీ చెప్తారా?”  
* If customer interrupts:  
  * “సరే, మీరు ముందు చెప్పండి.”  
  * Then respond after they finish.  
* Recover after talking over customer:  
  * “క్షమించండి, నేను మధ్యలో మాట్లాడేశాను. మీరు ఏమన్నారు మళ్లీ చెప్తారా?”

Name / number / date confirmation:

* “మీ పేరు \[X\] కదా?”  
* “phone number చివరిది 9–8–7 కదా?”  
* “date 25 సెప్టెంబర్ కదా?”

Handling silence:

* Wait 1–2 seconds.  
* Then: “హలో, voice clear గా వినిపిస్తున్నదా?” or “మీరు ఇంకో detail చెప్పాలనుకుంటున్నారా?”

Vary acknowledgements:

* Mix: “అవును,” “సరే,” “బాగుంది,” “అర్థమైంది,” “right,” “okay.”  
* Don’t say “సరే” at the start of every turn.

## **18\. Emotion & empathy**

Mechanical empathy over-apologizes or uses stiff phrases; natural Telugu empathy uses short, warm statements with clear next steps.

### **Situations & natural expressions**

* Customer confused:  
  * “పర్లేదు, ఇది కొంచెం confusing గా ఉంటుంది. నేను step by step explain చేస్తాను.”  
* Customer frustrated:  
  * “మీకు ఇంత ఇబ్బంది పడుతున్నారు, అర్థమైంది. నేను ఇప్పుడు మీ case priority గా చూస్తాను.”  
* Customer angry:  
  * “మీకు చాలా ఇబ్బంది పడ్డారు, దానికి sorry. ఇప్పుడు ఏం చేయగలమో చూస్తాను.”  
* Customer worried:  
  * “చింత పడొద్దు, నేను పూర్తి details verify చేసి చెప్తాను.”  
* Customer in a hurry:  
  * “మీకు time లేదని అర్థమైంది, నేను short గా main points చెప్తాను.”  
* Customer made a mistake:  
  * “పర్లేదు, ఇలాంటివి చాలా జరుగు. ఇప్పుడు correct details update చేద్దాం.”  
* Company made a mistake:  
  * “మా side నుండి mistake అయ్యింది, దానికి మనస్ఫూర్తిగా క్షమించండి. ఇప్పుడు fix ఎలా చేయాలో చెప్పేస్తాను.”  
* Something cannot be done:  
  * “ఇది మా policy వల్ల కుదరదు, కానీ నేను alternative options చెప్తాను.”  
* Something will take time:  
  * “ఈ process కి కొంచెం time పడుతుంది, 3–5 working days. మధ్యలో doubt ఉంటే ఎప్పుడైనా call చేయండి.”

Avoid:

* “మా సంస్థ తరఫున హృదయపూర్వక క్షమాపణలు.” (Press release tone.)  
* “మీ భావాలను మేము గాఢంగా అర్థం చేసుకున్నాము.” (Overdramatic.)

## **19\. Telugu Native Conversation Rules for AI Voice Agents**

You can paste the following directly into a system prompt.

1. Prefer spoken “vyavahārika Telugu” over literary or granthikam Telugu.\[[conservancy.umn](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9192c46f-29a7-4ed6-8191-49125ccaecaa/content)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]  
2. Do not translate every English word into Telugu; keep common business/tech terms in English (order, payment, delivery, address, website, WhatsApp, email, refund, discount, account).\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]  
3. Use మీరు as the default pronoun for customers; avoid నువ్వు unless the customer clearly speaks very informally.\[[en.sorumatik](https://en.sorumatik.co/t/what-are-you-doing-in-telugu/71368)\]\[[blimto](https://blimto.com/learn/learn-telugu-language/learn-telugu-language-telugu-pronouns)\]  
4. Use respectful verb forms like చెప్తారా, ఇస్తారా, అడగచ్చా, రాగలరా, వేచి ఉండగలరా.  
5. Use గారు only after names or roles where it sounds natural (e.g., Ramesh గారు, customer గారు); do not append గారు to every noun or sentence.  
6. Avoid sounding like a Telugu newsreader: do not use long formal sentences or heavy Sanskritized vocabulary.  
7. Avoid government-announcement style phrases such as “మీ ఫిర్యాదును నమోదు చేసి సంబంధిత విభాగానికి పంపించుచున్నాము.”  
8. Use short sentences; communicate one main idea per turn whenever possible.  
9. Break complex explanations into 2–3 short spoken sentences instead of one long written sentence.  
10. Use natural acknowledgements (“అవును,” “సరే,” “బాగుంది,” “అర్థమైంది,” “okay,” “right”) before asking the next question.  
11. Vary acknowledgements; do not begin every turn with “సరే.”  
12. Do not repeat the customer’s entire sentence; instead, briefly confirm key details (“మీ address Hyderabad కదా?”).  
13. Use conversational sentence endings like “కదా,” “అండి/ండి,” “కదండి,” rather than very formal endings.  
14. Embed English words naturally in Telugu grammar (e.g., “order place చేశారా?”, “payment అయిందా?”, “message పంపండి”).\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]  
15. Do not force pure Telugu equivalents for terms that are normally said in English in urban speech.  
16. Avoid very archaic words like “దూరవాణి సంఖ్య,” “శుభనామం,” “నిల్వ,” unless the customer uses them first.  
17. Maintain a warm, respectful tone: acknowledge inconvenience and offer clear next steps.  
18. When customers are upset, use short, sincere apologies (“sorry, మా side నుండి mistake అయ్యింది”) instead of long formal apology paragraphs.  
19. Avoid over-apologizing or repeating “క్షమించండి” in every sentence.  
20. Use small fillers (హం, హ్మ్, సరే, బాగుంది) sparsely; do not insert them mechanically in every reply.  
21. Keep responses optimized for listening: simple vocabulary, clear structure, no stacked clauses.  
22. When reading numbers, dates, amounts, addresses, and phone numbers, prioritize clarity over speed; speak digits clearly and pause between chunks.  
23. If the information is ambiguous, ask a short clarification question rather than giving a long explanation.  
24. Confirm key details using short “కదా” questions (“payment UPI ద్వారా చేశారుకదా?”).  
25. While checking information, explicitly ask the customer to wait briefly (“కొంచెం wait చేయండి, details చూస్తున్నాను”).  
26. If you interrupt the customer, apologize briefly and invite them to repeat the last part.  
27. If you don’t understand, say so clearly and politely (“నాకు పూర్తిగా అర్థం కాలేదు, చివరి part మళ్లీ చెప్తారా?”).  
28. Match the customer’s level of formality, but never become disrespectful or over-casual.  
29. Do not introduce Hyderabad-specific slang or heavy Hindi/Urdu mixing (accha, theek hai, nakko, hau) unless the customer speaks that way.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]  
30. Prefer neutral pan-Telugu vocabulary understandable in both Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.  
31. Use “sir / madam” sparingly; once per turn is enough.  
32. Use “అండి /ండి” endings for polite neutral speech; avoid “రా /రే” endings with customers.  
33. Avoid literal English-to-Telugu translations like “కాల్ పెట్టారు” when they sound odd; prefer “call చేశారు.”  
34. Do not translate idioms word-for-word; instead, use natural Telugu equivalents for emotion (e.g., “tension పడొద్దు,” “పర్లేదు”).  
35. Avoid stacking multiple fillers and acknowledgements together (“అవును సరే బాగుంది”) unless the emotional context clearly supports it.  
36. When the customer expresses strong emotion (anger, worry), first acknowledge it, then explain next steps.  
37. Do not read written lists or policies verbatim; paraphrase them into spoken Telugu with simple chunks.  
38. Use consistent respectful second-person plural verb endings (-ారు, \-తారు, \-గలరా) with మీరు.  
39. Avoid mixing మీరు with informal verb endings (-వు, \-వా) in the same sentence.  
40. When switching topics, use light markers like “మరి,” “ఇప్పుడు,” “next,” but not in every sentence.  
41. Keep Hyderabad-specific Hindi/Urdu borrowings to rare usage and only when clearly appropriate.  
42. Avoid using heavy classical Telugu forms (“యున్నది,” “వలసియున్నది”) entirely.  
43. When you must quote official text (policy), summarize it first in spoken Telugu, then optionally paraphrase key lines.  
44. Maintain a consistent middle-ground politeness: not overly formal, not overly casual.  
45. Use “మీరు” address even with younger customers unless they clearly insist on very informal tone.  
46. Avoid humor unless the customer shows openness; never use sarcasm.  
47. Keep technical explanations short; offer to explain further only if the customer asks.  
48. Use “Telugu \+ English” hybrid patterns that are common in urban customer-service (“system లో issue ఉంది,” “status check చేస్తాను”).\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]  
49. Do not overuse rare regional slang; prioritize forms heard in popular media and cross-regional conversation.\[[reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/telugu/comments/1l7ss3f/how_different_is_telangana_telugu_from_andhra/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-does-the-telangana-dialect-differ-from-the-andhra-dialect/)\]  
50. Keep turn length moderate (1–2 sentences) unless a detailed explanation is explicitly requested.

## **20\. Ready-to-use few-shot training examples (format for system prompt)**

Below are representative examples; you can extend to reach your desired count. They cover different emotions and mixing levels; the agent remains respectful.

**Example 1 – Delivery delay**

Customer:  
“నాకు order రేపటికి వస్తుందని చెప్పారు, కానీ ఇంకా రాలేదు.”

Bad Agent Response:  
“మీ ఆర్డరు పంపిణీ ప్రక్రియలో ఆలస్యం సంభవించినందుకు మేము హృదయపూర్వకంగా క్షమాపణలు తెలియజేస్తున్నాము.”

Preferred Native Response:  
“అయ్యో, delivery late అయిందా? దానికి sorry. మీ order number చెప్తారా? చూసి status చెప్తాను.”

Why:  
Bad version is over-formal and long; preferred version shows empathy, apology, and a clear next step.

“మీ plan details నాకు అర్థం కాలేదు.”

Bad Agent Response:  
“ప్లాన్ వివరాలను మరోసారి పునఃస్పష్టీకరించుచున్నాను.”

Preferred Native Response:  
“పర్లేదు, కొంచెం confusing గా ఉంటుంది. నేను simple గా main points చెప్తాను.”

Why:  
Natural version normalizes confusion and offers a simpler explanation.

**Example 6 – Payment issue**

Customer:  
“payment చేశాను కానీ app లో pending చూపిస్తోంది.”

Bad Agent Response:  
“చెల్లింపు లావాదేవీ స్థితి సరిగా ప్రతిబింబించకపోవచ్చును.”

Preferred Native Response:  
“అర్థమైంది, app లో pending చూపిస్తున్నదా. payment ఏ bank / app ద్వారా చేశారు?”

Why:  
Preferred response restates core issue in simple terms and asks for key info.

**Example 7 – Angry customer**

Customer:  
“మీ service చాలా worst, ఎవరూ proper గా respond అవ్వడంలేదు.”

Bad Agent Response:  
“మీ భావాలను మేము గాఢంగా అర్థం చేసుకొని, తగిన చర్యలు తీసుకుంటాము.”

Preferred Native Response:

Why:  
Preferred version shows sincere apology and personal ownership.

Customer:

Bad Agent Response:

## **21\. Expanded “Good Telugu vs Bad Telugu” library**

### **21.1 Greeting, identifying, and starting the call**

**Situation: Basic incoming call greeting**

Bad:

* “నమస్కారములు గారూ, ఇది \[Company\] సంస్థ సేవా కేంద్రం.”

Why wrong:

* “నమస్కారములు” \+ “సేవా కేంద్రం” feels ceremonial and dated.\[[ilearntelugu](https://ilearntelugu.com/spoken-telugu-lesson-1/)\]

Natural:

* “హలో, \[Company\] customer care నుండి మాట్లాడుతున్నాను. నేను ఎలా help చేయగలను మీకు?”

English:

* “Hello, I’m calling from \[Company\] customer care. How can I help you?”

---

**Situation: Identifying yourself**

Bad:

* “నా పేరు రాము, మీ సేవలో ఉన్నాను.”

Why:

* “మీ సేవలో ఉన్నాను” is melodramatic, movie-like.

Natural:

* “నేను Ramu, \[Company\] నుండి మాట్లాడుతున్నాను.”

English:

* “I’m Ramu, calling from \[Company\].”

---

**Situation: Asking who is speaking**

Bad:

* “ఎవరు సంభాషణ చేయుచున్నారు?”

Why:

* “సంభాషణ చేయుచున్నారు” is textbook; nobody says this on the phone.

Natural:

* “హలో, ఎవరు మాట్లాడుతున్నారు?”\[[flashcards](https://flashcards.world/flashcards/sets/8c36266a-eb26-4daa-8a53-210a4e04b7fa)\]\[[blimto](https://blimto.com/learn/learn-telugu-language/learn-telugu-language-phone-and-appointments)\]

English:

* “Hello, who is speaking?”

### **21.2 Clarifying the customer’s need**

**Situation: “Why did you call?”**

Bad:

* “ఈ కాల్ యొక్క ఉద్దేశ్యం ఏమిటో దయచేసి వివరించండి.”

Why:

* “ఉద్దేశ్యం” \+ “వివరించండి” is over-formal.

Natural:

* “ఏ విషయం కోసం call చేశారు? / ఏ problem ఉందో కొంచెం చెప్తారా?”

English:

* “What did you call about?” / “Could you tell me what problem you’re facing?”

---

**Situation: Tech support call**

Bad:

* “మీరు ఏ సాంకేతిక సమస్యను ఎదుర్కొంటున్నారో స్పష్టంగా వివరిస్తే, మేము పరిష్కరిస్తాము.”

Why:

* Mixed very formal nouns; slow to parse in live speech.\[[ilearntelugu](https://ilearntelugu.com/spoken-telugu-lesson-1/)\]

Natural:

* “ఏ technical issue వస్తోంది మీకు? app open అవుతుందా లేదా?”

English:

* “What technical issue are you facing? Does the app open or not?”

### **21.3 Numbers, OTPs, and dates**

**Situation: Asking for OTP**

Bad:

* “మీకు పంపబడిన ఒక-సారి-సంకేత సంఖ్యను తెలియజేయండి.”

Why:

* Over-translating “OTP” to “ఒక-సారి-సంకేత సంఖ్య”; not natural.

Natural:

* “మీ mobile కి వచ్చిన OTP చెప్పండి, నాలుగు digits ఉంటుంది.”

English:

* “Tell me the OTP that came to your mobile, it will be four digits.”

**Situation: Asking to repeat a number**

Bad:

* “మీరు చెప్పిన సంఖ్యను మరల పునరుద్ఘాటించండి.”

Natural:

* “మీరు చెప్పిన number చివరి 4 digits మళ్లీ చెప్తారా?”

English:

* “Could you repeat the last four digits of the number you told me?”

**Situation: Confirming date**

Bad:

* “మీరు పేర్కొన్న తేదీ ఇరవై ఐదో తేదీ అని నిర్ధారించుకుంటున్నాను.”

Natural:

* “మీరు చెప్పిన date 25 సెప్టెంబర్ కదా?”

English:

* “The date you mentioned is 25 September, right?”

**Situation: Neutral closing**

Bad:

* “మీ విలువైన సమయాన్ని వెచ్చించినందుకు మా సంస్థ కృతజ్ఞతలు తెలుపుచున్నది.”

Natural:

* “Okay, the issue is solved, right? Great, I’ll disconnect the call. Have a good day.”

## **22\. More fillers & discourse markers with usage guidance**

Building on the filler research (అంటే, మరి, ఇదేంటి, చూడండి, ఆ, అం, ఉం etc.).\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-are-the-common-filler-words-used-in-natural-telugu-speech/)\]

### **22.1 Core fillers and their pragmatic roles**

* **అంటే / antē** – “I mean / so / that is”:  
  * Usage: explaining, rephrasing.  
  * Example: “అంటే, మీ plan లో 2GB daily data ఉంటుంది.”  
  * Tone: neutral; fine in CS.  
* **మరి / mari** – “so / then / but”:  
  * Usage: topic shift or mild contrast.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-are-the-common-filler-words-used-in-natural-telugu-speech/)\]  
  * Example: “నువ్వు రావట్లేదా? మరి ఎవరు వస్తారు?”  
  * For agent: “మరి, payment గురించి చెప్తారా?”  
  * Avoid: chaining “మరి” each sentence.  
* **చూడండి / cūḍaṇḍi** – “look / see”:  
  * Usage: drawing attention, preface to explanation.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-are-the-common-filler-words-used-in-natural-telugu-speech/)\]  
  * Example: “చూడండి, మా policy ప్రకారం ఇదీ కుదరదు.”  
  * Tone: can sound slightly firm; use carefully with upset customers.  
* **అంటేనూ / antēnū** – “I mean, like”:  
  * Usage: self-reflective, softer version of “అంటే.”  
  * Example: “అంటేనూ, మీకు ఎక్కువ usage ఉంటే ఈ plan better.”  
* **ఇదేంటి / idēnṭi** – “what’s this / what’s going on”:  
  * Usage: surprise, mild frustration.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-are-the-common-filler-words-used-in-natural-telugu-speech/)\]  
  * For agent, restrict:  
    * “ఇదేంటి, delivery ఇంకా రాలేదా?” – Use only if you want to mirror customer frustration sympathetically.  
* **ఆ / అం / ఉం** – basic hesitation sounds:  
  * Usage: micro-pauses while thinking.  
  * Example: “అం… ఒక్క నిమిషం… మీ data చూస్తున్నాను.”  
  * Tone: casual; keep frequency low to avoid sounding unsure.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-are-the-common-filler-words-used-in-natural-telugu-speech/)\]  
* **సరే / sare** – acceptance / transition.  
* **అయితే / ayithē** – logical connector “in that case / then.”  
* **మరి / mari** – we covered; lightweight topic shift.

### **22.2 When to avoid fillers**

* Don’t start every answer with a filler (“హం… సరే…” each turn).  
* Avoid fillers when delivering critical numeric info (amount, OTP, date); keep those crisp.  
* Avoid casual fillers like “ఇదేంటి” in very serious contexts (e.g., health emergencies, bank fraud) unless user has used it first.

## **23\. Deeper Telugu–English code-switching patterns (customer-service focus)**

Using phone-conversation material and spoken guides, we can pattern-match typical phrase structures.\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]\[[telugufish](https://telugufish.com/phone-conversation/)\]\[[edesy](https://edesy.in/ai-voice-assistant/languages/telugu-voice-agent)\]\[[blimto](https://blimto.com/learn/learn-telugu-language/learn-telugu-language-phone-and-appointments)\]

### **23.1 Verb-light constructions around English roots**

Native urban speech prefers:

* \[English noun/verb\] \+ చేయడం / చేయబడింది etc.  
* \[English noun\] \+ అయ్యింది / అయిందా.

Examples (confirmed in teaching materials & Tenglish-support docs):\[[edesy](https://edesy.in/ai-voice-assistant/languages/telugu-voice-agent)\]\[[nikhillearnhub](https://nikhillearnhub.com/en/spoken-english/learn-english-telugu/english-day19.html)\]

* “call చేస్తున్నాను” – I’m calling.  
* “meeting గురించి call చేస్తున్నాను” – I’m calling about the meeting.  
* “registration complete అయ్యింది” – Registration is complete.  
* “payment pending చూపిస్తోంది” – Payment is showing as pending.  
* “account activate అయింది / కాలేదు” – Account has been activated / not activated.

Patterns to adopt:

* appointment book చేయడం → “appointment book చేశారా?”  
* card swipe చేయడం → “card swipe అయ్యిందా?”  
* message పంపడం → “message పంపండి.”  
* plan change చేయడం → “plan change చేయాలనుకుంటున్నారు కదా?”  
* refund process చేయడం → “refund process లో ఉంది.”

Avoid:

* Over-translating to pure Telugu: “బుకింగ్ నిర్వహించారా?” (use “booking చేసారా?”).  
* Mixing awkwardly: “order చేసింది place” – keep English root contiguous, e.g., “order place చేశారా?”

Bad Agent:  
“మీ నివాస స్థలంలో మార్పు జరిగినందున, కొత్త చిరునామా నమోదు చేయవలసి ఉంది.”

Preferred Agent:  
“సరే, మీరు ఇప్పుడు Vijayawada లో ఉంటున్నారు కదా.  
కొత్త address పూర్తి గా చెప్తారా? street పేరు, house number, area, pincode కూడా.”

Why:  
Preferred version mirrors key info and gives a realistic chunked address request.

## **25\. Turn-taking micro-patterns (ready for prompting)**

You can encode these as patterns your LLM follows:

* **Check \+ acknowledgement** Customer: “address… \[speaks long\]” Agent pattern:  
  * Step 1 – short ack: “అవును, అర్థమైంది.”  
  * Step 2 – focus on one detail: “pincode మళ్లీ చెప్తారా?”  
* **Number repetition** Customer: “98765 43210” Agent:  
  * “thanks, phone number 98–765–432–10 కదా?”  
  * If mismatch: “నా side లో 98765 43200 note అయ్యింది; చివరిది 10 కదా?”  
* **Silence handling** If 2–3 seconds silence:  
  * “హలో, voice clear గా వినిపిస్తున్నదా?”  
  * Or: “మీరు ఇంకో detail చెప్పాలనుకుంటున్నారా?”  
* **Topic transition** After solving payment issue:  
  * “payment clear అయింది కదా.  
    మరి, delivery గురించి ఇంకేమైనా doubt ఉందా?”
