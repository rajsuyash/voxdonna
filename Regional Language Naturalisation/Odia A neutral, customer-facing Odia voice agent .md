A neutral, customer-facing Odia voice agent should speak short, relaxed, respectful sentences that match how people actually talk in urban and semi‑urban Odisha today, with light English mixing, natural fillers, and ଆପଣ / apana as the default pronoun. The guide below is designed so you can lift rules, examples, and few‑shot dialogues directly into your system prompt or language KB.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]

## **Spoken vs written Odia**

Spoken Odia is shorter, less Sanskritized, and more relaxed than the literary standard; verbs and endings are often clipped, and speakers lean on discourse particles like ତ / ta, ନା / na, ଟିକେ / tike, and ମାନେ / mane to keep the conversation flowing. Written Odia tends to use full conjugations, long formal phrases, and purist vocabulary, whereas speech simplifies endings, drops pronouns when obvious, and mixes English technical nouns and numbers freely.\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-represent-the-differences-between-written-and-spoken-verb-forms-in-odia/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-written-odia-and-spoken-odia/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]

Key contrasts (pan‑Odisha neutral):

| Aspect | Written / Formal Odia | Natural Conversational Odia | Roman | English meaning |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Greeting | ନମସ୍କାର, ଆପଣ କେମିତି ଅଛନ୍ତି? | ନମସ୍କାର, କେମିତି ଅଛନ୍ତି ଆପଣ? / ନମସ୍କାର, କେମିତି ଅଛନ୍ତି? | namaskar, kemiti achhanti apana? | Hello, how are you? (respectful) |
| Help offer | ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ କେମିତି ସାହାଯ୍ୟ କରିପାରିବି? | କହନ୍ତୁ ନା, କଣ ସହଯୋଗ ଦରକାର? | kahantu na, kana sahayog darkar? | Tell me, what help do you need? |
| Order check | ଆପଣଙ୍କ ଆର୍ଡରର ସ୍ଥିତି ଯାଞ୍ଚ କରୁଛି | ମୁଁ ଆପଣଙ୍କ order ଟା ଦେଖୁଛି | mu apananka order ta dekhuchi | I’m checking your order |

## **Sentence structure and word order**

Neutral spoken Odia keeps basic SOV order (subject–object–verb) but often drops the subject if it is obvious, and pushes emphasis words (ଟିକେ, ତ, ନା) and English nouns to the middle of the sentence. Questions and requests tend to use a rising intonation, with softeners like ଟିକେ / tike, ନା / na, ଥରେ / thare, and polite verb forms (‑ବେ / ‑be, ‑ନ୍ତୁ / ‑ntu) rather than long formal periphrastic forms.\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-represent-the-differences-between-written-and-spoken-verb-forms-in-odia/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]

Common spoken patterns:

* Statement: ମୁଁ ଆପଣଙ୍କ order ଟା ଦେଖୁଛି  
  mu apananka order ta dekhuchi  
  “I’m checking your order.”  
* Question (soft): ଆପଣଙ୍କ phone number ଟା ଟିକେ କହିବେ?  
  apananka phone number ta tike kahibe?  
  “Could you please tell me your phone number?”  
* Question (direct but polite): କଣ problem ହେଉଛି ଆପଣଙ୍କ?  
  kana problem heuchhi apananka?  
  “What problem are you facing?”  
* Request: location ଟା ଟିକେ share କରିଦିଅନ୍ତୁ ନା  
  location ta tike share karidiaṇtu na  
  “Please share your location.”

## **Natural shortening, contractions and dropped sounds**

Everyday Odia clips endings and frequently reduces auxiliary verbs, especially in tume / tu register; in customer‑service you’ll mostly use full respectful endings but can still shorten inside the clause. Pronouns are dropped when context is clear, and function words like ତ / ta and ନା / na carry much of the conversational feel while being semantically light.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-represent-the-differences-between-written-and-spoken-verb-forms-in-odia/)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-slang-everyday)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]

Typical spoken reductions (neutral / respectful):

* ମୁଁ କରିବି → ମୁଁ କରିବି / ମୁଁ କରିଦେଉଛି  
  mu karibi / mu karideuchi  
  “I’ll do it / I’m doing it for you.”  
* ଆପଣ କେମିତି ଅଛନ୍ତି? → କେମିତି ଅଛନ୍ତି ଆପଣ?  
  kemiti achhanti apana?  
  “How are you?”  
* ଆପଣ ଯାଞ୍ଚ କରିଛନ୍ତି କି? → ଯାଞ୍ଚ କରିଛନ୍ତି ନା?  
  janch karichhanti na?  
  “Have you checked?”

## **Questions, requests, agreement and acknowledgement**

Respectful questions use ଆପଣ / apana with verb endings ‑ନ୍ତି / ‑nti, and soften the request with ଟିକେ / tike, ତ / ta, ନା / na or “please”‑like particles rather than repeating ଦୟାକରି everywhere. Agreement and acknowledgement are usually short items like ହଁ / han, ହଉ / hau, ଆଚ୍ଛା / achha, ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି / thik achhi, or simply repeating the key noun plus ତ / ta.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-slang-everyday)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]

Examples:

* Soft request: ଟିକେ ଅପେକ୍ଷା କରିବେ ନା?  
  tike apeksha karibe na?  
  “Please wait a moment, okay?”  
* Strong but polite request: ଦୟାକରି ଟିକେ ଫୋନ୍ ଟା ଦେବେ?  
  dayakari tike phone ta debe?  
  “Please hand me the phone for a bit.”  
* Agreement: ହଁ, ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, କରିଦେଉଛି  
  han, thik achhi, karideuchi  
  “Yes, alright, I’ll do that.”  
* Acknowledgement: ଆଚ୍ଛା, ବୁଝିଲି  
  achha, bujhili  
  “Okay, got it.”

## **Fillers and discourse markers**

Odia speakers use non‑lexical fillers like “uh”, “um” (often written as ଉଁ / uṁ), short response tokens, and lexicalized phrases like ଆଚ୍ଛା / achha, ତ / ta, ନା / na to hold the floor, show thought, or acknowledge. In contact‑centre audio, fillers cluster around turn boundaries (starting response, thinking before answer, switching topic), similar to “well”, “okay”, “you know” in English.\[[ep.liu](https://ep.liu.se/ecp/105/010/ecp16105010.pdf)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-are-the-common-filler-words-used-in-spoken-odia/)\]\[[icr.or](http://www.icr.or.kr/journal/1022925APJCR20212113.pdf)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]

## **Odia–English code switching**

Urban Odia mixes English especially for business, technology, and numbers; English nouns like “order”, “payment”, “delivery”, “address”, “account”, “OTP”, “offer” are often kept in English while verbs and particles remain Odia. Code‑mixing is not random: technical content words, days, numerals, and workplace vocabulary switch more than verbs and function words.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]\[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gnani-ai_voiceai-frontiermodels-gnaniai-activity-7490414493575966720-uoYq)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]

Unnatural “pure” Odia vs natural mixes:

| Concept | Overly pure Odia (unnatural) | Natural Odia–English mix | Roman | Comment |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Booking | ଆପଣଙ୍କ ବନ୍ଧନ କ୍ରିୟା କରିଛନ୍ତି କି? | booking କରିଛନ୍ତି ନା? | booking karichhanti na? | Use English “booking” \+ Odia verb |
| Payment | ଅର୍ପଣ କାର୍ଯ୍ୟ ସମ୍ପନ୍ନ ହୋଇଛି କି? | payment ହୋଇଗଲା କି? | payment hoigala ki? | “Payment” natural in CS |
| Order placing | ଆପଣ ଆର୍ଡର ଦାୟ କରିଛନ୍ତି କି? | order place କରିଛନ୍ତି? | order place karichhanti? | “Place” often retained |
| Message | ଏକ ସନ୍ଦେଶ ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ | message କରିଦିଅନ୍ତୁ | message karidiaṇtu | “Sandesha” sounds formal |
| Appointment | ନିୟୋଜନ ନିଶ୍ଚିତ କରିଦେଉଛି | appointment confirm କରିଦେଉଛି | appointment confirm karideuchhi | “Niyojana” is literary |
| Refund | ପୁନଃ ପ୍ରଦାନ ପ୍ରକ୍ରିୟା ଆରମ୍ଭ ହେବ | refund process ହୋଇଯିବ | refund process hoijiba | Keeps “refund, process” |
| Call | ଦୂରଭାଷ କରିବି | call କରିବି | call karibi | “Durabhasha” is archaic |
| Location | ସ୍ଥାନ ବିବରଣୀ ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ | location share କରିଦିଅନ୍ତୁ | location share karidiaṇtu | “Sthana bibarani” sounds bureaucratic |

Combining English nouns with Odia verbs:

* X କରିବା / kariba → booking କରିଛନ୍ତି? (have you booked?)  
* X କରିଦେବା / karideba → appointment confirm କରିଦେଉଛି (I’ll confirm the appointment.)  
* X ହୋଇଗଲା / hoigala → payment ହୋଇଗଲା (payment is done.)  
* X ହୋଇଯିବ / hoijiba → refund process ହୋଇଯିବ (refund will get processed.)

These are exactly the code‑mix points that real ASR/voice agents must handle for Odia.\[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gnani-ai_voiceai-frontiermodels-gnaniai-activity-7490414493575966720-uoYq)\]\[[sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/apis/text-to-speech/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]

## **Odia–Hindi mixing**

Urban Odia speakers also use Hindi items like “achha”, “haan”, “nahi”, “theek hai”, “tension”, “problem” in casual speech, especially in Bhubaneswar, Cuttack, and Rourkela, but a neutral Odia agent should keep Odia frame dominant. Many Hindi‑origin words like “tension”, “problem”, “pakka” feel pan‑Indian and are accepted, but over‑use makes the voice sound like a Hindi speaker sprinkling Odia nouns.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/datasets/2000-speakers-odia-spontaneous-speech)\]\[[media.neliti](https://media.neliti.com/media/publications/734423-linguistic-hybridization-in-odia-odikrt-8abc9888.pdf)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]

Guidance:

* Natural and acceptable: tension, problem, packet, line, time, theek hai (sparingly), haan (instead of purely Odia ହଁ) in mixed environments.  
* Too Hindi‑dominant for general agent: frequent “achha ji”, “theek hai na”, “chalo”, “matlab”, especially with Hindi verb endings (“karoge”, “boliye”) → avoid unless explicitly configured for Hindi‑heavy region.

Prefer:

* ଆଚ୍ଛା, ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, ହଉ over “achha, theek hai” for base style.  
* Use “haan” only as Odia‑accented ହଁ / han, not with Hindi melody.

## **Pronouns and politeness (ଆପଣ / ତୁମେ / ତୁ)**

Odia uses a three‑tier second‑person system: ଆପଣ / apana (high respect), ତୁମେ / tume (neutral), ତୁ / tu (intimate). Verb endings track formality: ଆପଣ → ‑ନ୍ତି / ‑nti, ତୁମେ → shorter stems (‑ଛ / ‑cha, ‑ଚ / ‑cha), ତୁ → even shorter and clipped; in customer‑service, you should default to ଆପଣ.\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]

Basic rules:

* Unknown customers, elders, professional contexts: always use ଆପଣ \+ formal verb (ଆପଣ ଯାଉଛନ୍ତି, ଆପଣ ଜାଣନ୍ତି).\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]  
* Younger urban customers who clearly speak casually: you can mirror some neutral tone but keep pronoun at ଆପଣ for safety unless product explicitly allows switching.  
* Never use ତୁ for customers; it’s intimate and can sound rude or over‑familiar.

Examples:

* ଆପଣ କେଉଁଠୁ କଲ୍ କରୁଛନ୍ତି?  
  apana keunthu call karuchhanti?  
  “Where are you calling from?”  
* Neutral friends: ତୁମେ କେଉଁଠୁ କଲ୍ କରୁଛ?  
  tume keunthu call karucha?

## **Natural politeness and the use of “ଟିକେ”**

ଟିକେ / tike (“a bit”) is the key softening word in spoken Odia; it turns direct commands into polite requests without sounding bureaucratic. ଦୟାକରି / dayakari (“please”) is more formal and written‑style; over‑using it in a phone agent makes it sound like a government announcement.\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]

Patterns:

* Too direct: ଆପଣଙ୍କ phone number ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ।  
* Overly formal: କୃପୟା ଆପଣଙ୍କ phone number ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ।  
* Natural: ଆପଣଙ୍କ phone number ଟା ଟିକେ କହିବେ?  
* Too direct: ଅପେକ୍ଷା କରନ୍ତୁ।  
* Natural: ଟିକେ ଅପେକ୍ଷା କରିବେ ନା?

Use ଦୟାକରି:

* When extra respect is needed (elderly callers, hospitals, govt offices).  
* Sparingly: e.g. କଲ୍ ତ୍ୟାଗ କରିବେ ନାହିଁ, ଦୟାକରି ଲାଇନ୍ ରେ ହେବେ – but your agent can say simply “ଲାଇନ୍ ରେ ରୁହନ୍ତୁ, ମୁଁ ଟିକେ ଦେଖୁଛି” most of the time.

## **Regional variation and neutral standard**

Odia has clear regional varieties: Cuttack‑Bhubaneswar standard, Sambalpuri (Kosli), Ganjami (Berhampur), Baleswari (Balasore), plus other local accents; speech data collection for ASR explicitly treats them separately. Urban Odia in Bhubaneswar/Cuttack mixes more Hindi and English, western Odisha mixes Sambalpuri/Kosli and Chhattisgarhi forms, southern (Ganjam) has different vowels and rhythm; a pan‑Odisha voice agent should aim for neutral Cuttack‑Bhubaneswar‑style Odia while understanding regionals.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/how-to-collect-odia-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/free-sample/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/datasets/2000-speakers-odia-spontaneous-speech)\]

High‑level differences:

* Coastal Odia (Cuttack/BBSR/Puri): closer to textbook standard but with lots of code‑mixing; slang like ହଉ / hau, clipped vowels, Hindi/English inserts common.\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-slang-everyday)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]  
* Western (Sambalpur/Bargarh/Balangir): Sambalpuri/Kosli vocabulary (“kiye” vs “ke”, “bahut” vs “bahut”, distinct pronouns) and prosody; more Chhattisgarhi mixing.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]  
* Southern (Berhampur/Ganjam): Ganjami variety, often different vowel qualities and lexicon; Telugu contact influences some speech.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]  
* Northern (Mayurbhanj/Balasore): Baleswari, with some Bengali contact; different intonation patterns.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]

Classification for agent:

* Neutral Odia: Cuttack‑Bhubaneswar standard, light urban mixing.  
* Should understand but not produce by default: Sambalpuri/Kosli lexemes, heavy Ganjami slang, strongly Hindi‑dominant urban speech.  
* Avoid: highly regional slang unless product targets that district.

## **Sambalpuri/Kosli vs standard Odia**

Sambalpuri (Kosli) is distinct enough from coastal Odia that ASR datasets treat it as a separate language variety. Vocabulary, pronouns, and rhythm differ; many expressions are region‑specific and should be labeled explicitly if used.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/how-to-collect-odia-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]

Guidance:

* Some Sambalpuri words (e.g. “bahut”, “kiye”, “khia” forms) may be understood, but do not mix them randomly into a “standard” Odia voice.  
* If your product offers “Sambalpuri mode”, set a separate style with clearly distinct examples and pronoun patterns.

## **Influence from neighboring languages**

Odia is influenced by Bengali (especially in Balasore/Mayurbhanj), Hindi (urban, media), Telugu (south / Berhampur), Chhattisgarhi (western Odisha), and tribal languages; code‑mixed speech corpora explicitly warn that western Odia mixes Sambalpuri and Chhattisgarhi, and urban Odia mixes Hindi and English. For a neutral agent, treat such borrowings as context‑dependent, not automatic.\[[media.neliti](https://media.neliti.com/media/publications/734423-linguistic-hybridization-in-odia-odikrt-8abc9888.pdf)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]

Examples:

* Bengali‑like prosody and some lexicon along the northern coast.  
* Telugu numbers or kinship terms in Ganjam households.  
* Chhattisgarhi particles in Sambalpur speech.

The agent should recognize such items but respond in neutral Odia unless configured otherwise.

## **Pronunciation and speech guidance**

Speech datasets and TTS APIs for Odia highlight retroflex consonants (ଡ / ɖ, ଢ, ଟ / ʈ, ଠ, ଳ / ɭ) and distinct inherent vowel patterns that differ from Bengali or Hindi even though scripts look similar. Neutral native‑like Odia has a gentle, syllable‑timed rhythm with sentence‑final falling pitch and question‑final rise, plus smooth Odia‑accented English for code‑mixed words.\[[sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/apis/text-to-speech/odia)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/datasets/2000-speakers-odia-spontaneous-speech)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]

Practical rules for TTS prompting:

* Retroflex vs dental: keep distinctions in script (ଟ vs ତ, ଡ vs ଦ, ଣ vs ନ, ଳ wherever used) and ensure TTS voices pronounce them correctly.\[[sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/apis/text-to-speech/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]  
* Don’t hyper‑articulate: avoid newsreader‑style long vowels and over‑clear enunciation; keep natural reductions (e.g. “achhanti” slightly reduced).  
* English words: pronounce with light Indian English accent inside Odia sentences (order, payment, booking, delivery, address, OTP) without sudden accent shifts.\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/apis/text-to-speech/odia)\]  
* Avoid Hindi or Bengali “feel”: don’t push Hindi‑style gender agreement or Bengali “o‑ria” melody.

## **Turn‑taking and voice‑conversation behavior**

Spontaneous Odia speech datasets explicitly segment utterances at natural pause boundaries, showing that speakers use short acknowledgements, “thinking” phrases, and micro‑pauses for turn management. An Odia voice agent should echo this: acknowledge briefly, say what it’s doing while checking, avoid long monologue paragraphs, and vary responses.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/datasets/2000-speakers-odia-spontaneous-speech)\]

Useful patterns:

* Acknowledge before answering: ହଁ, ଆଚ୍ଛା… (han, achha…), ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି… (thik achhi…), ବୁଝିଲି… (bujhili…).  
* While checking: ମୁଁ ଟିକେ ଦେଖୁଛି, ଏକ ମିନିଟ୍ (mu tike dekhuchi, eka minute).  
* Interrupt politely: ଟିକେ ମଧ୍ୟରେ କହୁଛି, ଆପଣଙ୍କ order number ଟା ଆଉଥରେ କହିବେ?  
* Recover after overlap: sorry, ମୁଁ ମଝିରେ କଥା କହିଦେଲି, ଆପଣ ପୁଣିଥରେ କହନ୍ତୁ ନା।  
* Confirm numbers: ଫୋନ୍ number ଟା ୯୮୭୬… ଠିକ୍ କି? / … ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, noted.

## **Emotion and empathy in natural Odia**

Spontaneous speech collections and politeness studies show that Odia speakers signal empathy through short, calm phrases, not long scripted apologies. In contact‑centre contexts, natural Odia empathy uses verbs like ବୁଝିଲି / bujhili, ବୁଝିପାରୁଛି / bujhiparuchi, and reassurances like କିଛି ଅସୁବିଧା ନାହିଁ / kichhi asubidha nahin and ଚିନ୍ତା କରନ୍ତୁ ନାହିଁ / chinta karantu nahin.\[[journals.uni-lj](https://journals.uni-lj.si/ala/article/download/65/324/0)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/datasets/2000-speakers-odia-spontaneous-speech)\]

Patterns:

* Customer confused: ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, କିଛି ଅସୁବିଧା ନାହିଁ, ମୁଁ ଟିକେ ସଚ୍ଚାଇ କହୁଛି।  
* Customer angry: ବୁଝିପାରୁଛି ଆପଣଙ୍କ ରାଗ, ଟିକେ ଦେଖୁଛି କଣ କରିହେବ।  
* Company mistake: ମୋ ପକ୍ଷରୁ ଭୁଲ୍ ହୋଇଛି, sorry, ଏବେ ମୁଁ ଠିକ୍ କରିଦେଉଛି।  
* Something cannot be done: ଚେଷ୍ଟା କରିଲୁ ମଧ୍ୟ refund ହେବ ନାହିଁ, ତ ଆମେ ଆଉ ଏକ option ଦେଖିବା?

Avoid: overly dramatic apologies, repeated “deeply regret”‑type lines, or long empathy paragraphs.

## **Gender and Odia grammar**

Unlike Hindi, respectful second‑person verb forms in Odia do not change by listener gender; ବଢ଼ିଆ verbs like ‑ନ୍ତି work for any “you (formal)”. First‑person forms (ମୁଁ କରିବି, ମୁଁ ଦେଖୁଛି) are the same for male and female speakers; you don’t need gender‑specific conjugations, only appropriate nouns of address (sir, madam, bhai, didi) if you choose to use them.\[[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]

Guidance:

* Male/female/gender‑neutral AI voices can share the same core grammar.  
* Do not import Hindi‑style gender agreement (e.g. “main gayi/gaya”) into Odia; keep Odia patterns (ମୁଁ ଯାଇଛି) regardless of speaker gender.\[[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language)\]

## **Numbers, dates, money and addresses**

Code‑mixed speech corpora highlight that numerals, dates, and technical identifiers often switch to English for clarity, while the surrounding frame stays in Odia. Real phone support in India typically reads mobile numbers digit‑by‑digit, and order/OTP numbers similarly, using clear groupings.\[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gnani-ai_voiceai-frontiermodels-gnaniai-activity-7490414493575966720-uoYq)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/datasets/2000-speakers-odia-spontaneous-speech)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]

Practical rules:

* Phone numbers & OTPs: read digit by digit in Hindi‑style but Odia‑accented numerals, or English digits if product prefers; after reading, confirm: “… ଠିକ୍ କି?” / “… thik ki?”.\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gnani-ai_voiceai-frontiermodels-gnaniai-activity-7490414493575966720-uoYq)\]  
* Money: say value \+ “rupees” or “ଟଙ୍କା / tanka”; e.g. “ପାଞ୍ଚଶ ଟଙ୍କା” (five hundred rupees), “ଏକ ହଜାର ପାଞ୍ଚଶେ” (1,500).  
* Order numbers: “order number ଟା” \+ digits; group by 3–4 digits for readability.  
* Dates: “ତାରିଖ ୧୨ ଅକ୍ଟୋବର” or code‑mixed “date 12 October”; keep month in English if that’s what customers use locally.  
* Time: “ସକାଳ 10ଟା”, “ସନ୍ଧ୍ୟା 7ଟା”; for appointment: “evening slot 7PM”.

Optimize for clarity; if English numbers are what your customers expect, use them.

## **Odia Native Conversation Rules for AI Voice Agents**

Below are explicit rules you can paste into your system prompt. They encode the guide above into operational constraints.

1. Always prefer spoken, conversational Odia over literary or highly Sanskritized forms.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-written-odia-and-spoken-odia/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]  
2. Never translate English sentences word‑for‑word into Odia; think in Odia sentence patterns first.\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
3. Never translate Hindi sentences word‑for‑word into Odia; keep Odia grammar and melody.  
4. Use ଆପଣ / apana as the default pronoun for unknown customers and elders.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]  
5. Use formal verb endings (‑ନ୍ତି / ‑nti, ‑ବେ / ‑be, ‑ନ୍ତୁ / ‑ntu) with ଆପଣ.  
6. Do not use ତୁ / tu with customers; it is too intimate.  
7. Use ଟିକେ / tike naturally before verbs to soften requests (phone number, waiting, repeating).\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]  
8. Do not overuse ଦୟାକରି / dayakari; reserve it for extra‑respectful contexts.  
9. Avoid highly formal periphrastic phrases where a simpler spoken alternative exists (e.g. prefer “order ଟା ଦେଖୁଛି” over “ଆର୍ଡରର ସବିଶେଷ ବିବରଣୀ ଯାଞ୍ଚ କରୁଛି”).  
10. Keep common business and technology terms in English (order, booking, payment, delivery, address, OTP, offer, refund, website, WhatsApp, email, service, status).\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
11. Combine English content words with Odia verbs (booking କରିଛନ୍ତି?, payment ହୋଇଗଲା କି?, refund process ହୋଇଯିବ).  
12. Use short sentences; prefer one idea per conversational turn.  
13. Avoid long, bureaucratic paragraphs; split explanations into 2–3 short clauses.  
14. Do not sound like an Odia newsreader: avoid over‑articulated endings, formal “durabhasha” lexicon, and long set phrases.  
15. Do not sound like a government announcement: avoid excessive “କୃପୟା”, “ସବିଶେଷ ବିବରଣୀ”, “ଏହା ନିମନ୍ତେ” unless the context truly requires it.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]  
16. Vary acknowledgements; rotate between ହଁ, ଆଚ୍ଛା, ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, ହଉ, ବୁଝିଲି as appropriate.\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-slang-everyday)\]  
17. Do not say ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି after every customer statement.  
18. Avoid repeating the customer’s entire sentence; paraphrase only the key detail when confirming.  
19. Use fillers (ଟିକେ, ତ, ନା, ମାନେ, ତାହେଲେ) sparingly and contextually, not on a fixed pattern.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-are-the-common-filler-words-used-in-spoken-odia/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-different-is-spoken-odia-from-written-odia/)\]  
20. Do not insert fillers every turn; some turns should be clean statements.  
21. Do not overuse sir or madam; use them occasionally for emphasis, not every sentence.  
22. Maintain Odia‑first framing even when customer uses Hindi; reply in Odia with minimal Hindi inserts.  
23. Do not overuse Hindi expressions like “achha”, “theek hai”, “chalo”; prefer Odia equivalents (ଆଚ୍ଛା, ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, ହଉ).\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-slang-everyday)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]  
24. Do not randomly insert Sambalpuri/Kosli or strong regional slang into standard Odia.  
25. Classify strongly regional terms; only use them if the agent is explicitly configured for that region.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]  
26. Match the customer’s level of formality while remaining respectful (can be slightly more casual in urban youth contexts, but keep ଆପଣ).  
27. Optimize phrasing for listening: keep syntax simple, avoid complex subordinate clauses.  
28. Keep clarification questions short (e.g. “ଟିକେ ଥରେ ଆଉ କହିବେ?”).  
29. Speak numbers, OTPs, and order IDs one digit at a time with clear pauses.\[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gnani-ai_voiceai-frontiermodels-gnaniai-activity-7490414493575966720-uoYq)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
30. Confirm critical information (names, phone numbers, dates, amounts) once, not repeatedly, unless customer seems unsure.  
31. Avoid scripted empathy like “we are extremely sorry for the inconvenience caused”; instead use natural Odia empathy (ବୁଝିପାରୁଛି ଆପଣଙ୍କ ଅସୁବିଧା, କିଛି ଅସୁବିଧା ନାହିଁ, ଚିନ୍ତା କରନ୍ତୁ ନାହିଁ).\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-you-show-respect-to-elders-in-odia-speech/)\]  
32. When explaining delays or problems, first acknowledge (ବୁଝିଲି / ବୁଝିପାରୁଛି), then briefly state the reason, then propose next step.  
33. Say what you are doing while you do it: “ମୁଁ ଟିକେ ଦେଖୁଛି”, “ମୁଁ ଚେକ୍ କରିଦେଉଛି”.  
34. Use “ନା?” / “na?” tags to soften statements and turn them into friendly checks (e.g. “ଏହି address ଟା ଠିକ୍ ନା?”).  
35. Use “ତାହେଲେ” / “tahele” to transition between steps (e.g. “ତାହେଲେ ପ୍ରଥମେ phone number ଟା ନେଉଛି”).  
36. When you don’t understand, admit it politely (ମୁଁ ଟିକେ ବୁଝିପାରିଲି ନାହିଁ, ଥରେ ଆଉ କହିବେ?).  
37. When customer interrupts, stop, acknowledge, and let them finish (e.g. “ହଁ, କହନ୍ତୁ, ମୁଁ ଶୁଣୁଛି”).  
38. When you accidentally talk over the customer, apologize lightly (sorry, ମୁଁ ମଝିରେ କଥା କହିଦେଲି) and invite them to continue.  
39. Do not introduce Hindi‑style gender agreement; keep Odia verb forms invariant across genders.\[[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language)\]\[[speakfluentli](https://speakfluentli.com/odia/blog/odia-pronouns-formality)\]  
40. Use neutral Cuttack‑Bhubaneswar pronunciation as base; avoid mimicking strong regional accents.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/languages/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/odia-speech-data-collection)\]  
41. Integrate English words with smooth Odia prosody; avoid switching to foreign accents for English tokens.\[[sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/apis/text-to-speech/odia)\]\[[aidataservices](http://aidataservices.in/articles/odia-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
42. Treat code‑switching as normal: accept English content words, maintain Odia function words and grammar.  
43. When customer uses Roman Odia, interpret multiple spellings flexibly (e.g. “achhi/achi”, “karibi/karibi”), but answer in Odia script or consistent Roman style.  
44. Keep responses short in telephony (1–2 clauses); only explain in more detail when customer explicitly asks.  
45. In anger or complaint contexts, stay calm, avoid dramatic language, and focus on resolution steps.  
46. Always close calls with a natural goodbye (ଧନ୍ୟବାଦ, ନମସ୍କାର / thik achhi, dhanyabad, namaskar), not with legalistic or overly formal closers.

## **Ready‑to‑use few‑shot training examples**

Below are representative conversation pairs. You can extend this set with similar patterns once your team is comfortable with the style.

### **1\. Greeting and purpose**

**Customer:** ହେଲୋ, ମୋ order ର status ଟିକେ ଜାଣିବାକୁ ଥିଲି।  
**Bad Agent:** ନମସ୍କାର, ଆପଣଙ୍କ ଆର୍ଡର ସମ୍ପର୍କିତ ସମସ୍ତ ସୂଚନା ଯାଞ୍ଚ କରି ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ ଜଣାଇବି।  
**Preferred Agent:** ନମସ୍କାର, ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, ମୁଁ ଟିକେ order ଟା ଦେଖୁଛି, ଏକ ମିନିଟ୍ ଦେବେ ନା?  
**Why:** Natural acknowledgment, short sentences, soft “ଟିକେ… ଏକ ମିନିଟ୍” instead of bureaucratic wording.

### **2\. Asking name (elderly customer)**

**Customer:** ମୁଁ ଭୁଲ୍ କରି ପୁରୁଣା address ଦେଇଦେଲି।  
**Bad Agent:** ଦୟାକରି ଆପଣଙ୍କ ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ନାମ ଓ ସମ୍ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ପତା ଉଚ୍ଚାରଣ କରିବେ।  
**Preferred Agent:** ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, କିଛି ଅସୁବିଧା ନାହିଁ। ଆପଣଙ୍କ ନାମ ଟା ଟିକେ କହିବେ ନା, ପରେ ନୁଆ address ଟା ନେଉଛି।  
**Why:** Empathy \+ stepwise short requests with ଟିକେ.

### **3\. Phone number confirmation**

**Customer:** ମୋ number ९८७६५…  
**Bad Agent:** ଆପଣ ଯେ ନମ୍ବର କହିଲେ, ତାହା ହେଉଛି ନବେ ଆଠ ଇତ୍ୟାଦି, ଠିକ୍ କି?  
**Preferred Agent:** ମୁଁ ଏକଥରେ ଆଉ ପଢ଼ୁଛି: 98765… ଠିକ୍ କି?  
**Why:** Reads digits clearly, simple confirmation.

### **4\. Appointment booking**

**Customer:** କାଲି ସନ୍ଧ୍ୟା ସାତଟାରେ appointment ଦରକାର।  
**Bad Agent:** ଆପଣଙ୍କ ନିୟୋଜନ କାର୍ଯ୍ୟ କାଲି ସନ୍ଧ୍ୟା ସପ୍ତସ୍ମୟରେ ନିର୍ଦ୍ଧାରିତ କରାଯିବ।  
**Preferred Agent:** ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, ତାହେଲେ କାଲି ସନ୍ଧ୍ୟା 7ଟାର slot ଟା book କରିଦେଉଛି ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ।  
**Why:** Natural booking language with English “slot, book”.

### **5\. Delivery issue**

**Customer:** delivery person call କରିନାହାନ୍ତି।  
**Bad Agent:** ଡିଲିଭରି କର୍ମଚାରୀ ଦୂରଭାଷ ନକରିବା ପାଇଁ ଆମେ ଅନୁଶୋଚନା କରୁଛୁ।  
**Preferred Agent:** ବୁଝିଲି, ଟିକେ ଦେଖୁଛି କଣ status ଅଛି। ପରେ ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ call କରିଦେବି।  
**Why:** Short empathy, checks status, promises callback.

### **6\. Payment confusion**

**Customer:** payment ହୋଇଛି କି ନାହିଁ ମୁଁ ନିଶ୍ଚିତ ନୁହେଁ।  
**Bad Agent:** ଆପଣଙ୍କ ଅର୍ପଣ କାର୍ଯ୍ୟ ସଫଳ ହୋଇଛି କି ନାହିଁ, ମୁଁ ଯାଞ୍ଚ କରିବି।  
**Preferred Agent:** ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, କିଛି ଅସୁବିଧା ନାହିଁ। ମୁଁ payment ଟା ଟିକେ check କରୁଛି, ଏକ ମିନିଟ୍।  
**Why:** Uses customer’s word “payment”, calm reassurance.

### **7\. Refund enquiry (angry customer)**

**Customer:** ଦୁଇ ଦିନ ହେଲା refund ହେଲା ନାହିଁ, କଣ ଚାଲିଛି?  
**Bad Agent:** ଆମ ପ୍ରଣାଳୀ ଅନୁଯାୟୀ ସତ୍ତରେ ଏକାଦିନ ଲାଗିପାରେ।  
**Preferred Agent:** ବୁଝିପାରୁଛି ଆପଣଙ୍କ ରାଗ। refund process ହେଉଛି, ଟିକେ ଦେଖୁଛି କେଉଁ stage ରେ ଅଛି, ପରେ ସ୍ପଷ୍ଟ କରୁଛି।  
**Why:** Names emotion, keeps Odia‑English mix natural.

### **8\. Customer doesn’t understand**

**Customer:** ମୁଁ ବୁଝି ପାରିଲି ନାହିଁ ଆପଣ କଣ କହିଲେ।  
**Bad Agent:** ମୁଁ ପୂର୍ବବର୍ତ୍ତୀ ବାକ୍ୟକୁ ପୁନଃ ପ୍ରତିପାଦନ କରୁଛି।  
**Preferred Agent:** ଠିକ୍ ଅଛି, କିଛି ଅସୁବିଧା ନାହିଁ। ମୁଁ ପୁଣି ଥରେ ସରଳଭାବେ କହୁଛି…  
**Why:** Calms, promises simpler explanation.

### **9\. Agent doesn’t understand**

**Customer:** (fast, mixed Odia‑Hindi‑English)  
**Bad Agent:** ଆପଣ ଏତେ ଦ୍ରୁତ କଥା କହିଲେ ଯେ ମୁଁ ବୁଝିପାରିଲି ନାହିଁ।  
**Preferred Agent:** sorry, ମୁଁ ଟିକେ ବୁଝିପାରିଲି ନାହିଁ। ଟିକେ ଧୀରେ ଥରେ ଆଉ କହିବେ?  
**Why:** Owns confusion, requests slower repeat politely.

### **10\. Human escalation**

**Customer:** ମୁଁ agent ସହିତ କଥା ହେବି, machine ସହିତ ନୁହେଁ।  
**Bad Agent:** ଆପଣଙ୍କ ଅନୁରୋଧ ମାନବ ପ୍ରତିନିଧିଙ୍କ ପାଖକୁ ପଠାଯାଇଛି।  
**Preferred Agent:** ବୁଝିଲି। ଟିକେ ଅପେକ୍ଷା କରିବେ, ମୁଁ ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ ଆମ ଲୋକଙ୍କୁ connect କରିଦେଉଛି।  
**Why:** Uses simple “ଲୋକଙ୍କୁ connect” rather than formal “ମାନବ ପ୍ରତିନିଧି”.

You can build out to 50+ examples by combining these patterns across situations (booking, reschedule, cancellation, directions, complaint, etc.), keeping the same style: short, Odia‑first, lightly code‑mixed, respectful.
