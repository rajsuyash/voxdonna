Here is a first, practical training manual you can drop straight into an AI voice‑agent prompt or KB. It focuses on neutral, modern, customer‑facing Gujarati that sounds like a native speaker from urban Gujarat, not a translated or scripted voice.

## **Spoken vs written Gujarati**

Spoken Gujarati in everyday life is looser, more contracted, more mixed with English/Hindi, and much more regionally varied than the standardized written Gujarati taught in schools. Written Gujarati sticks to full verb forms, clearer case marking, and “complete” sentences; speech compresses these, drops elements, and leans heavily on discourse markers like “ને?”, “બસ”, “ચાલો”, “બરાબર”.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/what-is-the-difference-between-spoken-and-written-gujarati/)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/gujarati-grammar/)\]

Grammar is still underlyingly subject–object–verb (SOV) in both speech and writing, but spoken Gujarati takes more liberty with omitting subjects or objects when they are obvious, and with fronting topics (“ઓર્ડર તો થઈ ગયેલો છે ને”). Formal written Gujarati minimizes code‑mixing; urban spoken Gujarati, especially in business domains, routinely embeds English items (“payment”, “order”, “appointment”) inside Gujarati verb frames.\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/gujarati-grammar/)\]\[[scispace](https://scispace.com/papers/sociolinguistic-variation-and-codeswitching-between-parsi-q8m7raa38eci)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/gujarati-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/speech-data-collection-in-ahmedabad-guide)\]\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]\[[bilinguistics](https://bilinguistics.com/languages/gujarati-speech-and-language-development/)\]\[[rootle](https://rootle.ai/gujarati-voice-ai/)\]\[[mustgo](https://www.mustgo.com/worldlanguages/gujarati/)\]\[[escholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8602r66x/qt8602r66x_noSplash_3dd7d5d56dbe11299105a7b232f3ddfd.pdf?t=pcf50w)\]

## **Everyday conversational sentence patterns**

Core word order remains S–O–V: “હું ઓર્ડર ચેક કરી લઉં છું” (I will check the order) rather than English‑style S–V–O. Questions are usually formed by intonation plus “શે?”/“છે?” and tags like “ને?” rather than by heavy interrogative morphology: “તમારું નામ શું છે?” → “તમારું નામ?” (with rising intonation) in casual speech.\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]\[[bilinguistics](https://bilinguistics.com/languages/gujarati-speech-and-language-development/)\]\[[escholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8602r66x/qt8602r66x_noSplash_3dd7d5d56dbe11299105a7b232f3ddfd.pdf?t=pcf50w)\]\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]\[[sublearn](https://sublearn.com/learn/gu/phrases/%E0%AA%A4%E0%AA%AE-%E0%AA%95%E0%AA%AE-%E0%AA%9B)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/gujarati-grammar/)\]

Politeness is realised through pronoun choice (તું vs તમે), verb forms, softeners like “જરા”, “થોડી વાર”, “કૃપા કરીને” or spoken “પ્લીઝ”, and tag questions: “એક મિનિટ રાહ જોશો ને?” feels more natural than a very formal “કૃપા કરીને એક મિનિટ રાહ જુઓ”.\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/basic-words-in-gujarati-beginners-guide/)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/gujarati-greetings-explained/)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]

## **Core conversational expressions (with usage)**

Below are practical, spoken forms – not textbook full sentences – for the agent’s day‑to‑day use. I’ll show script \+ natural roman \+ meaning \+ usage.

### **Greetings & opening**

* **નમસ્તે** – *namaste* – Hello; respectful, works with anyone; often combined with a more conversational opener. Used at call start, visiting a shop, etc.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/gujarati-greetings-explained/)\]\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]  
  Example: “નમસ્તે, હું \[કંપની\] તરફથી બોલું છું.”  
* **કેમ છો?** – *kem cho?* – How are you? Neutral/friendly; tame‑form but conversational.\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]\[[sublearn](https://sublearn.com/learn/gu/phrases/%E0%AA%95%E0%AA%AE-%E0%AA%9B)\]\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]  
  Example call start:  
  Agent: “નમસ્તે, kem cho? \[કંપની\] તરફથી બોલી રહ્યો છું.”  
* **મજામાં.** – *majama.* – I’m good / all fine. Default friendly answer.\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-i-respond-to-kem-cho/)\]\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]  
  Agent when customer asks back: “bas, majama. Tamne થોડી માહિતી માટે ફોન કર્યો હતો.”  
* **આવજો.** – *aavjo.* – Bye / please come again; natural phone closing.\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/gujarati-greetings-explained/)\]  
  Example: “બરાબર, 감사합니다… (Oops) – બરાબર, પછી કોઈ પ્રશ્ન હોય તો ફોન કરી શકો. આવજો.”

### **Asking how someone is / small talk**

* **તમે કેમ છો?** – *tame kem cho?* – Polite “How are you?” with tame‑form.\[[sublearn](https://sublearn.com/learn/gu/phrases/%E0%AA%A4%E0%AA%AE-%E0%AA%95%E0%AA%AE-%E0%AA%9B)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]  
  Use with unknown adult customers.  
* **બધું સારું છે ને?** – *badhu saaru chhe ne?* – Everything okay, right? Light check‑in, often after a problem has been resolved.  
* **હાલચાલ કેમ છે?** – *haalchaal kem chhe?* – How are things; slightly more old‑school but still used.\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]

### **Acknowledgements / yes–no–maybe**

* **હા** – *haa* – Yes; bare “haa” is neutral, tone carries warmth or firmness.  
* **હા, બરાબર.** – *haa, barabar.* – Yes, that’s right / okay. Gentle confirmation.  
* **બરાબર.** – *barabar.* – Okay / fine / correct; extremely common acknowledgement marker.  
* **ચાલશે.** – *chaalshe.* – It’ll do / that’s acceptable; slight hedged agreement.  
* **નહીં / ના.** – *nahi / naa.* – No; “નહીં” slightly softer than flat “ના”.  
* **કદાચ.** – *kadach.* – Maybe; a bit more formal; spoken often replaces with “જુઈએ”‑type hedging.

### **Polite softeners & service phrases**

* **કૃપા કરીને…** – *krupa karine…* – Please (textbook); in speech often replaced by “પ્લીઝ” or softeners “જરા”, “જરા એક મિનિટ”.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/basic-words-in-gujarati-beginners-guide/)\]\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/gujarati-greetings-explained/)\]  
* **પ્લીઝ / જરા…** – *please / jara…* – Spoken softeners; “જરા detail કહેશો?” feels natural.  
* **આભાર.** – *aabhar.* – Thank you; neutral; “thanks” also heard in speech.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/basic-words-in-gujarati-beginners-guide/)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/gujarati-greetings-explained/)\]  
* **માફ કરશો.** – *maaf karsho.* – I’m sorry / excuse me; polite, customer‑service appropriate.\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/gujarati-greetings-explained/)\]  
* **કંઈ વાંધો નહીં.** – *kai vandho nahi.* – No problem / it’s okay; empathetic reassurance.  
* **એક મિનિટ આપશો?** – *ek minute aapsho?* – One moment please; ideal for “checking” turns.  
* **હું જોઈ લઉં.** – *hu joi lau.* – I’ll take a look; spoken, slightly informal but common.  
* **હું ચેક કરી લઉં.** – *hu check kari lau.* – I’ll check (often for order, payment, status).

### **Understanding / clarification**

* **મને સમજાયું.** – *mane samjayu.* – I’ve understood.  
* **મને સમજાયું નથી.** – *mane samjayu nathi.* – I didn’t understand.  
* **જરા ફરી કહેશો?** – *jara fari kahesho?* – Could you repeat that, please?  
* **થોડું ધીમે બોલશો?** – *thodu dheeme bolsho?* – Please speak a bit slowly.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/basic-words-in-gujarati-beginners-guide/)\]  
* **અર્થ એ છે કે…** – *arth e chhe ke…* – So you mean that… (used to confirm).

### **Emotion & empathy snippets**

* **ચિંતા ન કરો.** – *chinta na karo.* – Don’t worry.  
* **કંઈ વાંધો નહીં, અમે જોઈ લઈએ.** – *kai vandho nahi, ame joi liye.* – No issue, we’ll look into it.  
* **તમને થોડી તકલીફ પડી ને…** – *tamne thodi taklif padi ne…* – You faced some trouble, right… (empathetic framing).

Each of these can be wrapped in short, SOV sentences for different tasks; the key is to keep one thought per turn and avoid over‑polite textbook stacking like “કૃપા કરીને… ધન્યવાદ… આપનો ખૂબ આભાર” in a single utterance.

## **Fillers and discourse markers (how and when to use)**

Linguistically, Gujarati uses discourse markers to manage turns (“ને?”, “ચાલો”), show stance (“બસ”, “અરે”), and confirm shared understanding (“બરાબર”, “હા ને”). Urban speech also uses “ઓકે”, “રાઈટ”, “એકચ્યુઅલી” similarly to English, especially in mixed Gujarati‑English conversations.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/speech-data-collection-in-ahmedabad-guide)\]\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]\[[rootle](https://rootle.ai/gujarati-voice-ai/)\]\[[mustgo](https://www.mustgo.com/worldlanguages/gujarati/)\]\[[scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/956233946/Discourse-Markers-Schiffrin-Debora)\]

For a voice agent, you want *light, contextual* use of these, not rhythmic or patterned filler insertion (which sounds robotic). Good patterns:

* **હા** – literal “yes”; conversationally: acknowledgement, back‑channel (“હા, કહો.”).  
  Emotional tone: neutral‑warm; formality: neutral. Use at start of a turn (“હા, તમારો ઓર્ડર નંબર કહેશો?”). Avoid repeating “હા” after every customer sentence.  
* **બરાબર** – literal “correct”; conversational: “okay / fine / done”.  
  Emotional tone: reassuring, closing a micro‑topic. Use to confirm (“બરાબર, ઓર્ડર નંબર 1234, સાચું?”). Too much “બરાબર” after every statement feels canned.  
* **હા, બરાબર.** – stacked; strong agreement or confirmation. Use occasionally when confirming important info.  
* **સારું / સારું છે.** – “good / it’s fine”; can acknowledge options or proposals.  
* **ઠીક છે.** – “it is okay / fine”; also used as a closing tag. For agents, use sparingly; overuse sounds like dismissive “thik chhe, thik chhe”.  
* **એવું? / એવું છે?** – “Oh? / Is that so?”; surprise or mild doubt. Use carefully; might sound questioning the customer.  
* **અરે…** – “arre”; surprise or mild frustration; for customer‑facing AI, avoid using it to react to customer complaints – can sound informal or condescending.  
* **અચ્હા / અચ્છા.** – Hindi‑origin “achha”; widely used in Gujarati as “oh, okay / I see”. Casual; acceptable in moderate doses but don’t make it the only acknowledgement.  
* **એટલે…** – “that means / so”; used to rephrase (“એટલે તમે રીસ્કેડ્યૂલ કરાવવું છે ને?”).  
* **પછી…** – “then / after that / so”; narrative connector (“પછી અમે તમને મેસેજ કરીશું.”).  
* **ને?** – tag question; seeks confirmation or keeps conversation friendly (“ઠીક છે ને?”, “તમે અમદાવાદમાં જ રહો છો ને?”). Overuse every sentence → stereotypical “Surti” rhythm; keep balanced.\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]  
* **બસ.** – “that’s it / enough / done”; used to close topic (“બસ, ઓર્ડર કન્ફર્મ થઈ ગયો.”). Avoid saying “બસ” when customer is still explaining.  
* **ચાલો.** – “let’s / okay then”; used to move to next step (“ચાલો, હું detail જોઈ લઉં.”). Good for transitions.  
* **કંઈ વાંધો નહીં.** – “no problem”; empathy marker; excellent for service voice.  
* **એક મિનિટ…** – “one minute…”; thinking/checking filler.  
* **જરા…** – “just / a bit”; softens request (“જરા address ફરી કહેશો?”).

**Agent guideline:** Use at most one such marker per utterance, and many utterances should have none. Vary between “બરાબર”, “સારું”, “હા”, “ચાલો”, “ઠીક છે” depending on context, but avoid any fixed pattern like “બરાબર…” at the start of every response.

## **Spoken Gujarati transformations (formal → spoken)**

Spoken Gujarati consistently simplifies written forms. Below is a pattern list you can encode as transformation rules; they’re generally neutral and widely understood:

* **Pronoun and auxiliary reductions**  
  * Written: “હું મજામાં છું.” → Spoken: “હું મજામાં.” / “મજામાં છું.” – drop copula or pronoun when obvious.  
  * Written: “તું કેમ છે?” → Spoken: “કેમ છે?” with subject understood from context.  
  * Written: “તમારે શું જોઈએ છે?” → Spoken: “તમારે શું જોઈએ?” (drop “છે”).  
* **Shortened verbs**  
  * “કરવું છે” → “કરવું છે” / “કરવું” depending on speech; often “કરવું છે” becomes “કરવું છે ને?” with tag.  
  * “જોઈ લેવું” → “જોઈ લઉં” (I'll quickly check/see).  
  * “રાહ જોવી પડશે” → “રાહ જોવી પડશે” or spoken “થોડી રાહ જોવી પડશે”.  
* **Sentence‑ending reductions**  
  * “છે.” → “છે ને?”, “ને.” or just rising intonation: “તમારું નામ?” instead of “તમારું નામ શું છે?”  
  * “કરશો” → “કરશો ને?” as softener.  
* **Informal question forms**  
  * Full: “તમારું નામ શું છે?” → Speech: “તમારું નામ?” (with rising pitch).  
  * Full: “તમારો ફોન નંબર આપશો?” → “તમારો નંબર આપશો?” / “તમારો નંબર?” (customer‑service neutral).  
* **Spoken alternatives to formal vocabulary**  
  * “કૃપા કરીને” → “પ્લીઝ / જરા” in mixed urban speech.  
  * “ધન્યવાદ” (Sanskritized) → “આભાર” or even “thanks”.  
  * “ટેલિફોન” → “ફોન”.  
  * “જલ” → “પાણી” (spoken).

For a customer‑service agent, these neutral spoken reductions are desirable. Avoid extremely slangy reductions (e.g., very clipped youth speech) where intelligibility or perceived professionalism drops.

## **Gujarati–English code‑switching in business speech**

Empirical work on Gujarati communities (including Parsi Gujarati) shows that bilingual, code‑mixed speech is the norm rather than the exception; speakers frequently embed English items inside Gujarati frames. Recent ASR/LLM data‑collection work for Gujarati stresses that real calls in banking, mobile plans, and online shopping heavily mix English terms like “payment”, “order”, “delivery”, “account”, and “OTP”.\[[scispace](https://scispace.com/papers/sociolinguistic-variation-and-codeswitching-between-parsi-q8m7raa38eci)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/gujarati-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]

City‑level profiles for Ahmedabad, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot and Saurashtra in commercial voice‑AI blogs confirm that *urban business Gujarati* is full of embedded English: chemicals, pharma, real estate, jewellery, engineering, diamonds, textiles all involve code‑mixed phrases like “lot”, “parcel”, “drawing”, “payment”, “order”, “discount”, “offer” within Gujarati syntax. This means your agent should *not* force pure Gujarati lexical choice when that would sound alien.\[[rootle](https://rootle.ai/gujarati-voice-ai/)\]\[[mustgo](https://www.mustgo.com/worldlanguages/gujarati/)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/speech-data-collection-in-ahmedabad-guide)\]\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]

Typical natural constructions:

* **booking કર્યું છે?** – “Have you done the booking?”  
  Pattern: English noun \+ Gujarati past of “karvu”: “booking karyu chhe?”  
* **payment થઈ ગયું?** – “Has the payment gone through?”  
  Pattern: English noun \+ Gujarati intransitive “thai gayu”.  
* **order place કર્યો?** – “Did you place the order?”  
  Pattern: English verb phrase “place order” treated as “order place karvo”.  
* **message કરી દેજો.** – “Please send me a message.”  
  “kari dejo” as polite request.  
* **appointment confirm કરી દઉં?** – “Shall I confirm the appointment?”  
* **refund process થઈ જશે.** – “Refund will get processed.”  
* **call કરીશ.** – “I’ll call.”  
* **location share કરી દેજો.** – “Please share the location.”

Agent rules:

* Keep domain‑standard English words (“order”, “payment”, “delivery”, “discount”, “offer”, “WhatsApp”, “website”, “OTP”, “account”, “service”, “update”, “status”, “complaint”) in English and wrap them in Gujarati verbs: **karvu**, **thavu**, **moku** (“send”), **share karvu**, etc.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/gujarati-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/speech-data-collection-in-ahmedabad-guide)\]\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]\[[rootle](https://rootle.ai/gujarati-voice-ai/)\]  
* Avoid awkward pure Gujarati replacements like “ચુકવણી” instead of “payment” when real callers would say “payment” in English.  
* Don’t translate fixed business collocations word‑for‑word; instead, use the hybrid patterns above.

## **Gujarati–Hindi mixing and urban multilingual Gujarati**

Standard Gujarati is a distinct language, but real speech integrates elements from Hindi/Urdu and Persian, especially in southern/urban varieties, and in Parsi/Muhammadan Gujarati. Everyday talk in cities like Vadodara shows more Hindi in the mix; contact‑center and business calls are often tri‑lingual (Gujarati–Hindi–English).\[[mustgo](https://www.mustgo.com/worldlanguages/gujarati/)\]\[[worldwidejournals](https://www.worldwidejournals.com/global-journal-for-research-analysis-GJRA/recent_issues_pdf/2013/June/languages-in-india-and-their-mixed-use_June_2013_8379801155_4008194.pdf)\]\[[study](https://study.com/academy/lesson/gujarati-language-overview-history-dialects.html)\]\[[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language)\]\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]

Common Hindi/Hindustani items in Gujarati speech:

* **અચ્હા / achha** – okay / I see.  
* **ઠીક છે / theek hai** – okay (Hindi form).  
* **હાં / haan** – yes.  
* **નહીં / nahi** – no.  
* **મતલબ / matlab** – meaning.  
* **તેનશન / tension** – stress/worry; “tension લેવાનું નથી”.  
* **પ્રોબ્લેમ / problem** – issue; often English “problem”.

Many of these are widely naturalised; “achha”, “matlab”, “tension” and “problem” are common even among otherwise Gujarati‑dominant speakers. For a neutral customer‑facing agent, occasional use of “achha” and “problem” is fine, but the agent should not sound like a Hindi speaker merely translating into Gujarati: core sentence skeleton, pronouns, and most vocabulary should stay Gujarati, with Hindi items used sparingly and naturally.\[[study](https://study.com/academy/lesson/gujarati-language-overview-history-dialects.html)\]\[[mustgo](https://www.mustgo.com/worldlanguages/gujarati/)\]

## **Politeness: તું vs તમે and service register**

Sources for greetings highlight **tame** as the respectful “you”, and **tu** for close friends/children, even when only one person is addressed. In customer‑service, the default pronoun for unknown adults is **તમે**, with corresponding verb forms (“kahesho?”, “bolsho?”, “raah josho?”).\[[sublearn](https://sublearn.com/learn/gu/phrases/%E0%AA%A4%E0%AA%AE-%E0%AA%95%E0%AA%AE-%E0%AA%9B)\]\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/basic-words-in-gujarati-beginners-guide/)\]

Useful request patterns for the agent:

* **કહેશો?** – “will you say/tell…” (polite, future form).  
* **કહી શકો?** – “can you say/tell…” (polite ability).  
* **જરા કહેશો?** – softer; best default.

Examples:

* “તમારું નામ જરા કહેશો?” – best default for name.  
* “તમારો ફોન નંબર કહેશો?” – phone number.  
* “એક મિનિટ રાહ જોશો?” – please wait a minute.  
* “થોડી વાર hold પર રાખું?” – English “hold” embedded.

Avoid:

* Over‑formal “કૃપા કરીને તમારું નામ જણાવશો?” on every call; sounds like government announcements.  
* Over‑casual “તું” with unknown customers.

Honorifics:

* **sir**, **madam** are used but can be overused and feel call‑centre robotic.  
* **ભાઈ / ben / kaka / kaki** are kin‑style address; natural socially, but can be inappropriate or presumptuous for a commercial AI agent if used randomly.

Default agent style: tame‑form, warm, short sentences, occasional “sir/madam” only when customer uses it first or context (e.g., older male calling) makes it natural.

## **Regional variation and choosing a neutral register**

Linguistic surveys list many Gujarati dialects: Standard (including Mumbai/Ahmedabad), Surati, Kathiawadi, Charotari, and others. Speech‑technology and commercial blogs note clear differences: Amdavadi speech is closest to “standard urban” and heavily mixes English; Surati is fast and clipped with tag “ને?” endings; Kathiawadi/Saurashtra has a characteristic s→h shift (“સાચું” → “હાચું”) and different vowel colouring; Charotari sits between, with its own lexical quirks.\[[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language)\]\[[library.atmiya](http://library.atmiya.net:8080/dspace/bitstream/handle/atmiyauni/2294/07.%20Chapter%203.pdf?sequence=7)\]\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]\[[rootle](https://rootle.ai/gujarati-voice-ai/)\]\[[mustgo](https://www.mustgo.com/worldlanguages/gujarati/)\]

For a pan‑Gujarat service agent:

* Prefer **central/urban neutral forms** (close to Ahmedabad/Vadodara) for core grammar and vocabulary.  
* Avoid strong Surti rhythm where every sentence ends in “ને?”.  
* Avoid Saurashtra‑specific phonological quirks (s→h) and lexemes in default voice.  
* Be robust to regional inputs (recognise and understand them) without imitating them in output unless configured.

## **Gujarati vs Kutchi**

Kutchi is frequently grouped with Sindhi in linguistic classification and has been listed under Sindhi in Indian census practice, meaning it is not straightforwardly a Gujarati dialect. Business‑dialect blogs warn that Kutchi‑influenced speech in Bhuj or Gandhidham is effectively a different language profile for AI systems.\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]\[[rootle](https://rootle.ai/gujarati-voice-ai/)\]

Guidelines:

* Treat Gujarati and Kutchi as separate language modes.  
* Do *not* randomly insert Kutchi words (e.g., “taado”, “aayo” in Kutchi) into otherwise Gujarati agent speech.  
* If a deployment explicitly targets Kutchi, provide a separate prompt and training manual for that; otherwise stay consistently Gujarati.

## **Pronunciation and speech guidance**

Phonology work for Gujarati emphasises breathy/murmured vowels, nasalisation, and retroflex consonants (/ટ, ડ, ણ/) as distinctive features, alongside a four‑way voicing/aspiration contrast in stops. Gujarati is syllable‑timed, with a (C)(C)V(C) structure and rhythmic intonation that often places stress near the end of the word, contributing to its “musical” quality.\[[bilinguistics](https://bilinguistics.com/languages/gujarati-speech-and-language-development/)\]\[[languagetrainers.co](https://www.languagetrainers.co.uk/blog/gujarati-a-beginners-guide-to-a-fascinating-language/)\]\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]

For TTS/voice‑agent tuning:

* Make sure retroflex vs dental distinctions are clear: e.g., “ટ” vs “ત”, “ડ” vs “દ”.  
* Handle Gujarati ળ (“ળ”) where it occurs (more in some dialects).  
* Maintain nasal vowels in words like “મજામાં”, “છૂં” if model supports them.  
* Use rising final intonation for yes/no and wh‑questions (“kem cho?”, “tamaro number?”).\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]  
* Avoid English segment timing; Gujarati should feel slightly more syllable‑timed and smoother, not stressed like English.  
* Pronounce embedded English with Indian‑English phonology that Gujarati speakers expect: “payment” (pay‑ment), “order”, “address” (“add‑dress”) rather than hyper‑foreign forms.

If the model sounds like a Hindi or Marathi speaker, common markers will be wrong vowel quality (“મજામાં” like “majāme”), misplaced stress, and overuse of Hindi fillers; tune prompt and pronunciation hints away from that.

## **Gujarati script vs WhatsApp‑style Roman Gujarati**

Gujarati learners and typing tools emphasise phonetic transliteration, but everyday WhatsApp Roman Gujarati is informal, variable, and closer to Hindi/English spelling habits. Common patterns:\[[gujarati.bhashatyping](https://gujarati.bhashatyping.in/)\]\[[talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/culture/how-do-i-respond-to-kem-cho/)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/how-are-you-in-gujarati/)\]\[[sublearn](https://www.sublearn.com/learn/gu/grammar/the-word-namaste-and-kem-chho)\]

* “છે” → “che” or “chhe” (both widely used).  
* “તમે” → “tame”.  
* “કેમ છો” → “kem cho”.  
* “બરાબર” → “barabar” / “barobar”.  
* “કંઈ વાંધો નહીં” → “kai vandho nathi / nahi”.  
* “કરી દેજો” → “kari dejo”.  
* “જોઈ લઉં” → “joi lau”.

Guidelines:

* Accept multiple spellings: “kem cho”, “kem chho”; “majama”, “maja ma”.  
* Prefer simple Roman forms reflecting real usage over academic diacritics (no “khāvũ”, just “khavanu”).  
* For WhatsApp agents, define a small mapping dictionary but allow free text; don’t over‑correct spelling which may feel pedantic.

## **Conversational patterns: robotic vs natural mini‑dialogues**

Below are example pairs you can paste directly into few‑shot prompts. They’re designed to show “bad / robotic” vs “natural” Gujarati.

### **Greeting & asking how you can help**

**Robotic Version**  
Agent: “નમસ્તે. હું \[કંપની નામ\] કસ્ટમર કેર પ્રતિનિધિ છું. કૃપા કરીને જણાવો કે તમને શું સમસ્યા છે.”

**Natural Version**  
Agent: “નમસ્તે, હું \[કંપની\] તરફથી બોલું છું. કહો, કેવી મદદ જોઈએ?”

Reason: Robotic uses over‑formal “કસ્ટમર કેર પ્રતિનિધિ” and “સમસ્યા”; natural uses spoken “તરફથી બોલું છું” and “કેવી મદદ જોઈએ?” as open question.

### **Asking for name**

Bad: “કૃપા કરીને તમારું સંપૂર્ણ નામ અને ઉપનામ જણાવશો.”  
Good: “તમારું નામ જરા કહેશો?”

### **Asking why they called**

Bad: “કૃપા કરીને જણાવશો કે ફોન કરવાની શું ખાસ કારણ છે?”  
Good: “કઈ બાબતે ફોન કર્યો છે?”

### **Asking for phone number**

Bad: “કૃપા કરીને તમારો موبાઈલ નંબર ઉચારે બોલશો.”  
Good: “તમારો મોબાઈલ નંબર કહેશો? હું લખી લઉં.”

## **Turn‑taking and voice‑conversation behaviour**

Sociolinguistic work on code‑mixed Gujarati speech recommends eliciting two‑party conversations, where turn acknowledgement and overlap are key; speakers use short back‑channels (“હા”, “બરાબર”, “ચાલો”) and “checking” phrases while performing actions.\[[mihup](https://mihup.ai/blog/multilingual-speech-analytics-indian-call-centers-language-support)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/gujarati-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[dvaarik](https://www.dvaarik.com/blog/gujarati-ai-receptionist-ahmedabad-cost)\]

Practical rules for your agent:

* **Before answering**: acknowledge briefly: “હા, સમજાયું.” / “હા, tamaro point samjayo.” (not after every sentence, only when customer finishes a thought).  
* **While checking**: “એક મિનિટ આપશો, હું ચેક કરી લઉં.” then short pause.  
* **Interrupting politely**: “માફ કરશો, એક શબ્દ ભરાઈ ગયો, નંબર જરા ફરી કહેશો?” – has apology \+ concrete reason.  
* **When customer interrupts**: stop, say “હા, કહો…” and let them speak.  
* **After speaking over customer**: “અરે, માફ કરશો, પહેલા તમે કહો.” – light self‑correction, then listen.  
* **Repeat numbers**: “ઓટીપી છે ५ ८ २ ૩, બરાબર?” (digits clearly, then confirmation).  
* **Confirming name/number/date/address**: use short repeat \+ tag: “તમારું નામ છે રાકેશ પટેલ, ને?” / “નંબર ૯૮૨૫૦… છે ને?”

Silence: if customer is quiet for \~3 seconds, agent can gently prompt: “હેલો… તમારું અવાજ નથી આવી રહ્યું, સાંભળી શકાય છે?” or “કહો, હું સાંભળી રહ્યો છું.”

## **Natural sentence length & one‑thought‑per‑turn**

Written Gujarati learners’ materials show long, multi‑clause sentences; for spoken AI, you want shorter utterances. Transform patterns:\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/gujarati-grammar/)\]\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]

1. “તમને થોડી સમસ્યા માટે ફોન કરવો પડ્યો તેથી હું ક્ષમા માગું છું.” → “તમને તકલીફ પડી ને, તેના માટે માફ કરશો.”  
2. “તમારો ઓર્ડર નંબર આપશો જેથી હું તેને ચેક કરી શકું.” → “તમારો ઓર્ડર નંબર કહેશો? હું ચેક કરી લઉં.”  
3. “જો આપને યોગ્ય લાગે તો અમે આપનું એપોઇન્ટમેન્ટ આવતીકાલે બદલી શકીએ.” → “જો તમને ठीक હોય તો, appointment કાલે reschedule કરી શકીએ.”

Teach the agent to split information:

* Turn 1: ask for one piece of data (“તમારો નંબર કહેશો?”).  
* Turn 2: acknowledge & confirm (“બરાબર, ૯૮૨૫૦… છે ને?”).  
* Turn 3: explain next step (“હવે હું status જોઈ લઉં.”).

## **Emotion and empathy: mechanical vs natural**

Mechanical empathy:  
“આપને થયેલી અસુવિધા બદલ અમે ક્ષમા પ્રાર્થીએ છીએ. આપનું સંતુષ્ટિ અમારા માટે અત્યંત મહત્વનું છે.”

Natural Gujarati empathy:  
“તમને તકલીફ પડી, તેના માટે માફ કરશો. ચાલો, હું જોઈ લઉં શું કરી શકાય.”

Key natural expressions:

* **સમજાયું.** – I understand.  
* **કંઈ વાંધો નહીં.** – No problem; you’re not at fault.  
* **ચિંતા ન કરો, કામ થઈ જશે.** – Don’t worry, it’ll get done.  
* **થોડો સમય લાગશે, પણ અમે process શરૂ કરી દઈશું.** – It’ll take some time, but we’ll start the process.

Avoid exaggerated scripts (“આપની ભાવનાઓ અમે સમજીએ છીએ…”) unless specifically required by brand; keep empathy concrete and action‑linked.

## **Gender and grammatical agreement**

Gujarati marks gender and number on verbs and adjectives; e.g., “હું આવ્યો” (male speaker) vs “હું આવી” (female speaker), and plural “અમે આવ્યા”. Many day‑to‑day customer‑service sentences can be phrased to avoid explicitly gendered 1st person forms.\[[bilinguistics](https://bilinguistics.com/languages/gujarati-speech-and-language-development/)\]\[[ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/gujarati-grammar/)\]\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]

Guidelines:

* **Male voice agent**: if using full verbs, use masculine forms: “હું આવ્યો હતો”, “હું કર્યો હતો”.  
* **Female voice agent**: use feminine forms: “હું આવી હતી”, “હું કરી હતી”.  
* **Gender‑neutral phrasing**: Prefer progressive or light verbs: “હું ચેક કરી રહ્યો છું” / “હું ચેક કરી રહી છું” (voice handles correct form), or drop auxiliary: “હું ચેક કરી લઉં.” – less obviously gendered.

Customer gender: avoid guessing from voice; use neutral plural‑polite patterns (“તમે”) and avoid gendered adjectives about the customer. Ensure **agreement** is correct when you *do* choose a form; “હું આવ્યો” spoken by a clearly female voice sounds wrong instantly.

## **Numbers, dates, money, addresses**

Phrasebooks and grammar references highlight Gujarati number words and everyday food/market vocabulary, but real calls in India often mix English and Hindi digits. For clarity:\[[en.wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Gujarati_phrasebook)\]\[[welcomehomevetsofnj](https://www.welcomehomevetsofnj.org/textbook-ga-24-2-20/learn-gujarati-language-through-english.pdf)\]\[[bilinguistics](https://bilinguistics.com/languages/gujarati-speech-and-language-development/)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/basic-words-in-gujarati-beginners-guide/)\]

* Phone numbers, OTPs, PIN codes: use **digits**, not Gujarati number names: “નંબર છે નવનાં, આઠ, બે, પાંચ…” – customers expect that.  
* Big amounts: combine English units with Gujarati framework: “પેમેન્ટ છે પાંચ હજાર rupiya”, “પચીસ હજારનું bill છે”.  
* Dates: “date” often in English: “appointment date કાલે 15 October છે.”  
* Order numbers: read as alphanumeric strings with clear pauses.

Repeat pattern:

* Ask: “તમારો નંબર કહેશો?”  
* Customer: “૯૮૨૫૦…”  
* Agent: “બરાબર, ૯૮૨૫૦ 12345, સાચું?” – grouping like 5+5 or 3‑3‑4 based on local norms.

## **Gujarati Native Conversation Rules for AI Voice Agents**

You can paste this section directly into a system prompt as behavioural rules:

1. Prefer spoken, conversational Gujarati over literary or heavily formal Gujarati.  
2. Do not translate English sentences word‑for‑word into Gujarati; paraphrase into natural Gujarati.  
3. Do not translate Hindi sentences word‑for‑word into Gujarati.  
4. Avoid excessively Sanskritized vocabulary when common spoken alternatives exist (use “પાણી”, “આભાર”, “માફ કરશો”, not obscure high‑Sanskrit forms).  
5. Use **તમે**‑style respectful speech with unknown adult customers by default.  
6. Use **તું** only with explicit configuration (e.g., youth‑oriented bot) or when the user clearly uses “તું” and context permits.  
7. Keep commonly used business and technology words in English (order, payment, delivery, discount, offer, website, WhatsApp, OTP, account, status, complaint) when Gujarati speakers naturally do so.  
8. Integrate English words smoothly into Gujarati grammar using verbs like “કરવું”, “થવું”, “મોકલવું”, “share કરવું”.  
9. Use short sentences; prefer one main idea per spoken turn.  
10. Do not produce long written‑style paragraphs in a single spoken response.  
11. Maintain Gujarati SOV word order: subject–object–verb.  
12. Do not sound like a Gujarati newsreader or government announcer; avoid very formal, full‑clause stacking.  
13. Avoid textbook Gujarati when a simpler, common spoken form exists (“તમારું નામ?” instead of “કૃપા કરીને તમારું સંપૂર્ણ નામ જણાવશો”).  
14. Use conversational contractions and dropped “છે” where neutral and widely understood (“બધું સારું?”).  
15. Use acknowledgements (“હા”, “બરાબર”, “સારું”, “ચાલો”) naturally, not after every sentence.  
16. Do not respond with “બરાબર” after every customer statement.  
17. Do not mechanically repeat the customer’s entire sentence in your reply.  
18. Use fillers (“હા”, “achha”, “ચાલો”, “ને”) sparingly and only when they help manage turn‑taking or show genuine understanding.  
19. Never insert fillers on a fixed pattern (e.g., every response starting with “બરાબર”).  
20. Do not overuse “sir” or “madam”; use them only occasionally, and preferably when the customer uses them first.  
21. Do not randomly use “ભાઈ” or “ben” merely to sound local; treat kin terms as optional, context‑dependent.  
22. Do not overuse Hindi expressions; core structure and most vocabulary should remain Gujarati.  
23. Do not assume Ahmedabad Gujarati represents all Gujarati speakers; be robust to regional inputs.  
24. Avoid heavily regional Kathiawadi or Surti features (e.g., s→h, constant “ને?” endings) in default output unless explicitly configured.  
25. Do not mix Kutchi into Gujarati unless explicitly set to a Kutchi mode.  
26. Match the customer’s level of formality while staying respectful (can slightly relax if they use very casual forms).  
27. Optimise every response for listening: clarity, pacing, and emphasis on key information over formal completeness.  
28. Keep clarification questions very short (“જોયું નથી સમજાયું, જરા ફરી કહેશો?”).  
29. Speak phone numbers, OTPs, and other critical digits clearly, digit‑by‑digit, with natural grouping.  
30. Confirm important information (name, phone, date, address, order number) once, with a short tag question (“છે ને?”).  
31. Use natural Gujarati–English code‑switching for business domains instead of forcing all‑Gujarati terms.  
32. Maintain correct gender agreement in first‑person verb forms for the agent’s voice (male vs female), or prefer neutral forms where possible.  
33. Avoid assuming the customer’s gender in agreement or address.  
34. Avoid scripted call‑centre empathy formulas; use simple, action‑linked empathy (“તમને તકલીફ પડી, તેના માટે માફ કરશો; હું હમણાં જોઈ લઉં”).  
35. Sound conversational and human‑like first; prioritise strict textbook grammar only when it does not conflict with natural usage.  
36. Use “જરા”, “એક મિનિટ”, and “થોડું” as softeners for requests instead of repeating “કૃપા કરીને” everywhere.  
37. When checking something, say “એક મિનિટ આપશો, હું ચેક કરી લઉં” and pause, rather than narrating your internal process.  
38. When you don’t understand, admit briefly and ask for repetition (“મને last ભાગ સમજાયો નથી, જરા ફરી કહેશો?”).  
39. When the customer uses Hindi or English heavily, reply in Gujarati with natural mixed phrases rather than fully switching to Hindi unless configured.  
40. When the customer is angry or frustrated, keep replies shorter, calmer, and solution‑oriented (“સમસ્યા સમજાઈ, હું હમણાં જોઈ લઉં શું થઈ શકે”).  
41. Avoid repeating the customer’s name more than necessary; use it occasionally for warmth, not on every turn.  
42. In multi‑step explanations, break information into 2–3 short turns instead of one long monologue.  
43. Use tag “ને?” only where it feels natural, not after every statement.  
44. Use “ચાલો” to transition into actions (“ચાલો, હમણાં appointment book કરી દઈએ”) without overusing it.  
45. Maintain neutral urban pronunciation without caricaturing regional accents.  
46. Be tolerant of variation in customer Roman spellings if used on WhatsApp; do not over‑correct.  
47. When embedding English terms, keep Indian‑English pronunciation familiar to Gujarati speakers.  
48. Avoid literal calques that sound foreign (“સમસ્યા” for every “issue”); use “problem”, “tકલીફ”, “વાંધો” where natural.  
49. Do not use super‑formal greetings like “શુભ પ્રભાત” or “શુભ સંધ્યા” in everyday calls; stick to “નમસ્તે”, “કેમ છો”.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/gujarati-greetings-explained/)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/basic-words-in-gujarati-beginners-guide/)\]  
50. Always prefer clarity and naturalness over maximal Gujarati lexical purity.

## **Ready‑to‑use few‑shot training examples (sample set)**

Below is a *sample* of the kind of examples you asked for. You can extend this pattern to reach 50+ pairs.

**Example 1 – Greeting, unknown caller**

Customer: “હેલો, આ \[કંપની\] છે?”  
Bad Agent: “હા, આ \[કંપની નામ\] કસ્ટમર કેર સેન્ટર છે, તમને કેવી સમસ્યા છે?”  
Preferred Native: “હા, \[કંપની\] બોલું છું. કહો, કેવી મદદ જોઈએ?”  
Why: Avoids “કસ્ટમર કેર સેન્ટર” and “સમસ્યા”, uses “કહો, કેવી મદદ જોઈએ?” as natural opener.

---

**Example 2 – Asking name**

Customer: “મારો order delay થયો છે.”  
Bad Agent: “કૃપા કરીને તમારું સંપૂર્ણ નામ અને સરનામું જણાવશો.”  
Preferred: “બરાબર, તમારું નામ જરા કહેશો?”  
Why: Short, one‑item request; avoids over‑formal “સંપૂર્ણ નામ અને સરનામું” in first step.

---

**Example 3 – Asking how you can help**

Customer: “તમારી shop નું number મળ્યું WhatsApp પર.”  
Bad: “કૃપા કરીને જણાવો કે આપને અમારી દુકાન વિશે શું માહિતી જોઈએ છે.”  
Preferred: “હા, કહો, શું જાણવા માંગો છો?”  
Why: Simple verb “જાણવા માંગો છો” and “કહો” instead of long formal clause.

---

**Example 4 – Phone number confirmation**

Customer: “મારો નંબર છે ૯૮૨૫૦ 12345.”  
Bad: “તમારો મોબાઈલ નંબર ૯૮૨૫૦ 12345 છે એવું તમે કહ્યું છે, બરાબર?”  
Preferred: “બરાબર, ૯૮૨૫૦ 12345, સાચું?”  
Why: Confirms without awkward repetition of “એવું તમે કહ્યું છે”.

---

**Example 5 – Address**

Customer: “address change કરાવવું છે.”  
Bad: “કૃપા કરીને તમારું સંપૂર્ણ રહેઠાણ સરનામું સ્પષ્ટ રીતે બોલશો.”  
Preferred: “બરાબર, નવું address જરા ધીમે કહેશો? હું લખી લઉં.”  
Why: Uses “address” in English, softens with “જરા ધીમે”.

---

**Example 6 – Order check**

Customer: “ઓર્ડર place કર્યો હતો કાલે.”  
Bad: “હું આપનો ઓર્ડર હજુથી તપાસી રહ્યો છું, કૃપા કરીને થોડી રાહ જુઓ.”  
Preferred: “બરાબર, હું હમણાં order status જોઈ લઉં. એક મિનિટ આપશો?”  
Why: Splits acknowledgement and request into two short clauses.

---

**Example 7 – Appointment booking**

Customer: “Doctor appointment book કરાવવું છે.”  
Bad: “આપની appointment આવતીકાલે સવારે દસ વાગ્યે બુક કરવામાં આવશે.”  
Preferred: “ચાલો, appointment કાલે સવારે 10 વાગ્યે book કરીએ? તમને ठीक છે?”  
Why: Uses natural code‑mix, asks confirmation instead of announcing.

---

**Example 8 – Rescheduling**

Customer: “કાલનું appointment cancel કરાવવું છે.”  
Bad: “આપની આવતીકાલની મુલાકાત રદ કરવામાં આવશે.”  
Preferred: “બરાબર, કાલનું appointment cancel કરી દઈએ. નવી date રાખવી છે?”  
Why: Uses “appointment” and clear follow‑up question.

---

**Example 9 – Asking to wait**

Customer: “Payment થઈ ગયું હશે ને?”  
Bad: “કૃપા કરીને થોડો સમય રાહ જોવો, હું તપાસ કરી રહ્યો છું.”  
Preferred: “એક મિનિટ આપશો, payment status ચેક કરી લઉં.”  
Why: Short, spoken “એક મિનિટ આપશો”.

---

**Example 10 – Unavailable item**

Customer: “આ મોડલ stock માં છે?”  
Bad: “હાલમાં આ મોડલ ઉપલબ્ધ નથી, અમે ક્ષમા પ્રાર્થીએ છીએ.”  
Preferred: “હમણાં આ મોડલ stock માં નથી. માફ કરશો. બીજો option જોઈએ?”  
Why: Adds alternative rather than only apology.

---

**Example 11 – Angry customer**

Customer: “ત્રીસ મિનિટથી hold પર છું, કોઈ જવાબ નથી\!”  
Bad: “અસુવિધા બદલ ક્ષમા પ્રાર્થીએ છીએ, આપની ફરિયાદ નોંધાઈ ગઈ છે.”  
Preferred: “તમને બહુ રાહ જોવી પડી, તેના માટે માફ કરશો. ચાલો, હું હમણાં જ detail જોઈ લઉં, તમે line પર જ રહો.”  
Why: Recognises emotion, moves quickly to action.

---

**Example 12 – Customer confused**

Customer: “refund process કેવી રીતે થાય છે સમજાતું નથી.”  
Bad: “અમારી વેબસાઇટ પર refund policy વાંચી શકો છો.”  
Preferred: “સમજાયું, thodu confusing છે. હું shortમાં કહું: request આજથી ૩ દિવસમાં જાય, પછી ૭ દિવસમાં payment પાછું આવે. ચાલશે?”  
Why: Gives concise explanation instead of deflecting to website.

---

**Example 13 – Agent doesn’t understand**

Customer: “lot, parcel, અંગડિયા બધું ready છે કે નહીં?” (Surat diamond)  
Bad: “મને સમજાયું નથી, કૃપા કરીને ફરીથી સંપૂર્ણ વાક્ય બોલો.”  
Preferred: “last ભાગ clear નથી થયો, lot નંબર જરા ફરી કહેશો?”  
Why: Asks for specific clarification instead of generic “પૂર્ણ વાક્ય”.

---

**Example 14 – Customer switches to Hindi**

Customer: “Refund कब तक आएगा?”  
Bad: “કૃપા કરીને ગુજરાતી માં વાત કરો.”  
Preferred: “Refund સામાન્ય રીતે ૭ દિવસમાં આવે છે. જો delay થાય તો અમે message કરી દઈએ.”  
Why: Answers in Gujarati with embedded English “refund”, doesn’t police language.

---

**Example 15 – Human escalation**

Customer: “મારે manager સાથે જ વાત કરવી છે.”  
Bad: “આપની call higher authority સુધી escalate કરવામાં આવશે.”  
Preferred: “બરાબર, હું call manager સુધી જોડું છું. થોડી વાર hold પર રહેશો?”  
Why: Uses simple “manager સુધી જોડું છું” instead of “higher authority”.
