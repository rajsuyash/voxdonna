A natural Malayalam voice agent should speak colloquial, everyday Malayalam (with Manglish where appropriate), not Granthika (literary) Malayalam or newsreader Malayalam. The guide below is structured so you can lift sections, tables, examples, and rules directly into your agent’s system prompt and knowledge base.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]

## **Spoken vs written Malayalam**

Malayalam is strongly diglossic: there is a highly codified literary (“Granthika”) variety used in writing, media, and formal speech, and a much more flexible colloquial variety used in everyday conversation. In cities like Kochi and Thiruvananthapuram, spoken Malayalam is fast, often slurred, rich in English loanwords, and shows regional colour, whereas formal writing suppresses English and prefers Sanskrit-derived terms. A voice agent for customer calls should target neutral colloquial spoken Malayalam, not Granthika: think WhatsApp and shop-talk, not government circulars.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]

### **Written vs spoken examples**

* Written: **എന്താണ് ചെയ്യുന്നത്?**  
  Spoken: **എന്താ ചെയ്യുന്നത്? / എന്താ cheyyunne?**  
  Meaning: “What are you doing?” — spoken form drops copula and compresses vowels.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]  
* Written: **എനിക്ക് മനസ്സിലാകുന്നില്ല.**  
  Spoken: **മനസ്സിലാകുന്നില്ല / മനസ്സിലയില്ല.**  
  Meaning: “I don’t understand.” — subject pronoun often dropped when context is clear.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
* Written: **നിങ്ങൾക്ക് വെള്ളം വേണമോ?**  
  Spoken: **വെള്ളം വേണമോ? / vellam venamo?**  
  Meaning: “Do you want water?” — polite pronoun omitted, verb carries politeness.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

Spoken Malayalam favours short clauses, dropped subjects, reduced auxiliaries, and conversational particles like **എന്താ (entha?)**, **ശരി (sheri)**, **പിന്നെ (pinne)**, **അയ്യോ (ayyo)**, which are largely absent from formal writing.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]

## **Everyday sentence patterns**

Spoken Malayalam is typically SOV (subject–object–verb), but subjects are very often omitted and word order flexes around focus and particles.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

* Neutral statement:  
  **Njaan veettil aanu.** / **ഞാൻ വീട്ടിൽ ആണ്.** — “I am at home.”\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]  
  Subject can drop if obvious: **Veedtil aanu.** (“(I’m) at home.”)  
* Topic–comment:  
  **Ithu enthaa?** — “What is this?” (topic **ithu** fronted).\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]  
* Short answers:  
  **Sugham\!** — “(I’m) fine.”\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]  
  **Ariyilla.** — “(I) don’t know.”\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

Questions in speech often use rising intonation and particles like **\-ആ? (aa?)**, **\-ആണോ? (aano?)**, or standalone **എന്താ? (entha?)** rather than full written forms with **ആണ്**. Requests and softening commonly rely on **ഒന്ന് (onnu)**, **പറയാമോ? (parayaamo?)**, **പറയൂ (parayoo)**, **ദയവായി (dayavaayi)**, making them sound less abrupt.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]

## **Fillers and markers**

Everyday Malayalam is full of discourse particles and reaction words which carry tone and stance more than literal meaning. A voice agent should know them, use them sparingly, and avoid sounding like a scripted call centre that says **ശരി** after every sentence.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]

### **Key markers**

| Expression | Literal | Conversational meaning | Tone & formality | Agent usage |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| ശരി (sheri) | correct | Okay, right, agreed | Neutral; widely used.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\] | Use as occasional acknowledgement; vary with others. |
| അതെ (athe) | it is so | Yes | Neutral; can be lengthened (**atheee**) for warmth.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Use for clear yes, especially after questions. |
| അല്ല (alla) | not so | No / that’s not correct | Neutral, can be soft if spoken gently.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Use to correct gently: **alla, order number maattiyirikkunnu.** |
| അല്ലേ? (alle?) | isn’t it? | Tag question / softener | Friendly; can sound coaxing or confirming.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\] | Use to confirm shared understanding: **ningal booking cheythittundu alle?** |
| ആണോ? (aano?) | is it? | Really? / Is that so? | Curious, mild surprise.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Use occasionally to show engagement: **athu aano? pinne nokkaam.** |
| അങ്ങനെയാണോ? (anganeyaano?) | is it like that? | Oh, is that how? | Empathetic curiosity; slightly longer.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Use in empathy flows: **anganeyaano? vishamikkenda, njan nokkaam.** |
| അയ്യോ (ayyo) | oh\! | Oh no / oops / sympathy | Strong emotional exclamation; informal.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\] | Use very sparingly for mild mishaps; avoid in very formal contexts. |
| പിന്നെ (pinne) | then | Then / of course / filler | Multipurpose; also used like “of course”.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\] | Use for transitions: **pinne, payment details parayoo.** |
| എന്താ? (entha?) | what? | What is it? / What’s up? | Casual; can sound abrupt if misused.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Avoid as stand-alone in service; use only inside full questions (e.g., **ഇതെന്താ?**). |
| ഓക്കെ (okay) | English “okay” | Okay | Urban, informal.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Acceptable mixed with Malayalam: **okay, pinne delivery date parayoo.** |
| നോക്കട്ടെ (nokkatte) | let (me) see | Let me check / I’ll have a look | Neutral polite.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\] | Excellent “thinking” phrase while querying systems. |
| ഒരു മിനിറ്റ് (oru minute) | one minute | One moment | Neutral.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Use before pause: **oru minute, nokkatte.** |
| പ്രശ്നമില്ല (prashnamilla) | no problem | No issue / it’s fine | Reassuring.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\] | Use when resolving or downplaying small issues. |

Fillers like **hmm**, **aa**, **oh** are usually realised prosodically and do not need explicit text; the agent should instead rely on brief markers plus short silence to sound natural rather than mechanical.

## **Spoken Malayalam transformations**

Spoken Malayalam systematically shortens, drops, and compresses written forms. The examples below are drawn from spoken-phrase resources and diglossia descriptions.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]

### **Typical reductions**

* Copula **ആണ് (aanu)** often dropped:  
  Written: **ഇത് നല്ലതാണ്.**  
  Spoken: **ഇത് നന്നാ. / ithu nalla.** — “This is good.”\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]  
* Pronoun drop:  
  Written: **എനിക്ക് വിശക്കുന്നു.**  
  Spoken: **വിശക്കുന്നു\! / vishakkunnu\!** — “I’m hungry\!”\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* Aux \+ negation compressed:  
  Written: **എനിക്ക് അറിയില്ല.**  
  Spoken: **അറിയില്ല / ariyilla.** — “I don’t know.”\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]  
* Polite question softened with **\-ഓ?**:  
  Written: **നിങ്ങൾക്ക് മനസ്സിലായോ?**  
  Spoken: **മനസ്സിലായോ?** — “Did you understand?”\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
* Adverbial **പതുക്കനെ (patukkanē)** vs **പതുക്കെ (patukke)** regional variant for “slowly”.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]

Rather than enumerating 100 separate lines here, you can treat the whole Starter Pack list as a transformations set: each written form (with full pronouns and auxiliaries) has a spoken equivalent in the PDF, and the agent should choose the spoken version wherever possible.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

## **Malayalam–English code switching**

Urban and professional Malayalam speech commonly mixes English nouns, technical terms, numerals, and dates into a Malayalam grammatical frame (Manglish). Code-mixing is especially strong around banking, mobile plans, medical appointments, online shopping, and work-related topics, where English content words carry clearer technical meaning.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]

### **When English tends to be kept**

Typical English words in customer-service contexts include: **appointment, booking, order, payment, delivery, address, phone number, customer care, website, WhatsApp, email, offer, discount, date, time, confirmation, cancel, reschedule, refund, exchange, online, location, message, call, account, OTP, application, service, update, problem, complaint, status**. Speakers usually keep these in English and add Malayalam inflection via verbs like **ചെയ്യുക (cheyyuka)** or aspect words like **ആയോ (aayo)**, rather than searching for pure Malayalam equivalents.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]

Examples (patterned on natural Manglish structures and your prompt):

* **booking ചെയ്തോ?** — “Have you done the booking?”  
* **payment ആയോ?** — “Has the payment gone through?”  
* **order place ചെയ്തോ?** — “Did you place the order?”  
* **message അയക്കൂ.** — “Send a message.”  
* **appointment confirm ചെയ്യാം.** — “We’ll confirm the appointment.”  
* **refund process ആവും.** — “The refund will be processed.”  
* **call ചെയ്യാം.** — “We’ll call (you).”  
* **location share ചെയ്യൂ.** — “Please share the location.”

In these constructions, Malayalam supplies word order, pronouns, question particles (**\-ഓ?**), and aspect/tense (e.g., **ചെയ്തോ? cheytho?**, **ചെയ്യാം cheyyaam**), while English contributes content nouns and sometimes verb stems. A neutral service agent should mirror this pattern, preserving English for business/tech terms and using Malayalam verbs and particles to glue them.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]

## **Politeness and respect**

Malayalam encodes respect both through pronouns (**നീ nee** informal singular vs **നിങ്ങൾ ningal** polite) and verb endings. Customer-service speech to unknown adults should default to **നിങ്ങൾ**\-style or drop pronouns entirely while keeping polite verb morphology (e.g., **parayamo?**, **cheyyamo?**, **kathirikkaamo?**).\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

### **Key polite forms**

* **പറയൂ (parayoo)** — “Please say / tell (me).” — Neutral imperative, polite but direct.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* **പറയാമോ? (parayaamo?)** — “Could you say (it)?” — Softer, more deferential.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* **ഒന്ന് പറയാമോ? (onnu parayaamo?)** — Literally “Can you say once?”; functions as “Could you please tell me?”.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* **നോക്കാം (nokkaam)** — “Let’s see / I’ll check.” — Polite, reassuring.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
* **ഒന്ന് നോക്കട്ടെ (onnu nokkatte)** — “Let me just check.” — Standard agent line while querying.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
* **കാത്തിരിക്കാമോ? (kaathirikkaamo?)** — “Could you wait?” — Gentle wait request.  
* **വേണമോ? / വേണോ? (venamo? / veno?)** — “Do you need/want (it)?” — Soft offer; tone matters.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
* **പ്രശ്നമില്ല (prashnamilla)** — “No problem.” — Warm reassurance.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]

Relationship address terms like **ചെട്ടാ (chetta)**, **ചേച്ചി (chechi)**, **ഇക്കാ (ikka)**, **മോനേ (mone)**, **മോളേ (mole)** are very common socially, but a general-purpose commercial agent should avoid them unless explicitly branded as “friendly local shop assistant” with tight persona control. “Sir” and “madam” are used, but overuse feels call-centre-like; sprinkling them only when the customer uses them is safer.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

## **Regional variation**

Kerala has broadly Southern (Travancore), Central (Cochin), and Northern (Malabar) dialect zones, with sub-areas tied to caste and community. Differences are strongest in pronunciation (vowel quality, intonation), certain vocabulary items (food, kinship terms), and some particles.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]

Examples:

* South Kerala / Trivandrum influences: more Tamil lexical items; local slang like **എന്തെരു (entheru)** for “what?” in Trivandrum.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
* Malabar / Kozhikode–Kannur: strong **v-b** alternation under Kannada influence, and Muslim speech with Arabic/Urdu loans like **വാപ്പ (vaappa)** for father.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
* Slang: **അടിപൊളി (adipoli)**, **കിടു (kidu)**, **മച്ചാൻ (machaan)**, **ബ്രോ (bro)** are widely understood but more Kochi/urban in flavour.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]

For a neutral agent:

* Prefer pan-Kerala expressions from phrasebooks: **namaskaaram, sughamaano, manassilaayi, kuzhappamilla, venam/venda**.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* Avoid region-specific slang in system prompts (**entheru**, hyper-local nicknames).  
* Recognise but don’t imitate strong regional pronunciation or heavy Gulf/urban slang unless mimicking the customer’s style in a controlled experiment.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]

## **Gulf influence**

Muslim dialects and Gulf-returnee speech incorporate Arabic-origin kinship terms and religious vocabulary (e.g., **വാപ്പ / ബാപ്പ (vaappa/bāppa)**, **ഉമ്മ (umma)**, **അല്ലാഹ് (allah)** exclamation), along with Gulf English accent features. These are widely understood in many communities but are still community- and region-specific, especially in North Kerala.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]

A neutral commercial agent:

* Should not use Arabic religious exclamations (**അല്ലാഹ്\!**) or strongly Muslim kinship terms unless built as an explicitly community-specific assistant.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
* Can safely understand them and respond with neutral Malayalam (**ശരി, ഞാന്‍ നോക്കാം.**, **പ്രശ്നമില്ല.**) without mirroring the religious register.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]

## **Pronunciation patterns**

Malayalam has a rich consonant inventory with important contrasts between dental vs retroflex, tapped vs rolled r, and the special letters **ള (ḷ)**, **ഴ (ḻ)**, **റ (ṟ)**. Native-like sound requires:\[[cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/malayalam-namboodiri-dialect/19CBF6E9E1CE65A84928F7C9C2286A9B)\]\[[thebinate](https://thebinate.in/blog/malayalam-voice-ai)\]

* Correct long/short vowels (e.g., **a** vs **aa**, **i** vs **ii**) and gemination (doubling) of consonants, as in **കുറച്ചു (kurachu)** vs **കുരച്ചു (kurachu)**.\[[cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/malayalam-namboodiri-dialect/19CBF6E9E1CE65A84928F7C9C2286A9B)\]  
* Retroflexes: **ട, ട്ട, ണ, ള, ഴ, റ** require tongue curled back; mispronouncing **ഴ (ḻ)** as **ജ (j)** or **സ (s)** instantly sounds non-native.\[[cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/malayalam-namboodiri-dialect/19CBF6E9E1CE65A84928F7C9C2286A9B)\]  
* Stress: typically falls on first syllable unless second has a long vowel; this shapes rhythm.\[[cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/malayalam-namboodiri-dialect/19CBF6E9E1CE65A84928F7C9C2286A9B)\]

For English words inside Malayalam:

* Most urban speakers use fairly Indian-English pronunciations (“payment”, “order”, “location”) with Malayalam-style intonation.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
* The agent’s TTS or prosody controller should avoid hyper-Anglicised or hyper-Malayalamised pronunciations (e.g., “pēymenṭṭu” in full Granthika style).

## **Mini-dialogues (robotic vs natural)**

Below, “bad” variants simulate written, stiff, or call-centre Malayalam; “natural” variants align with phrasebook-style colloquial speech.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

### **Greeting**

Bad:  
Agent: **നമസ്കാരം, നിങ്ങൾ XYZ കമ്പനിയുടെ ഉപഭോക്തൃ സേവന കേന്ദ്രത്തിൽ എത്തിയിരിക്കുന്നു.**  
Natural:  
Agent: **നമസ്കാരം, XYZ company-il ninnanu vilikkunne.**  
Meaning: “Hello, I’m calling from XYZ company.”

### **Asking how you can help**

Bad:  
Agent: **താങ്കൾക്ക് എന്ത് സഹായം വേണമെന്നു ദയവായി വ്യക്തമാക്കുക.**  
Natural:  
Agent: **എന്ത് help വേണം? / എങ്ങനെ സഹായിക്കാം?**  
Meaning: “What help do you need?” / “How can I help?”

### **Order-status enquiry**

Customer: **Ente order status entha?**  
Bad:  
Agent: **താങ്കളുടെ ഓർഡറിന്റെ നില സംബന്ധിച്ച വിവരങ്ങൾ ഞാൻ ഇപ്പോൾ പരിശോധിച്ചു കൊണ്ടിരിക്കുന്നു.**  
Natural:  
Agent: **ഒന്നു nokkatte… ശരി, order shipped aayi. നാളെ delivery varum.**  
Meaning: “Let me check… okay, the order has been shipped. Delivery will come tomorrow.”

You can generate many similar pairs by rewriting any long multi-clause sentence into 2–3 short spoken lines, each carrying one idea.

## **Turn taking and call flow**

Malayalam speakers often acknowledge before answering, and say something like **ഒന്ന് നോക്കട്ടെ**, **ഒരു മിനിറ്റ്**, or **നോക്കാം** when pausing to think or check. They also use short interjections like **ഹും**, **ശരി**, **അല്ലേ?** between turns, but not after every sentence.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]

For a voice agent:

* Acknowledge before answering: **ശരി, മനസ്സിലായി.** / **അവേ, manassilaayi.** — then answer.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* While checking: **ഒന്ന് nokkatte…** (brief pause) — then report result.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
* If interrupted: **ശരി, ആദ്യം നിങ്ങൾ പറയൂ.** — “Okay, you go ahead first.”  
* If talking over someone: **ക്ഷമിക്കണം, mid-il vannu. നീങ്ങൽ ആദ്യം finish cheyyu.** — short apology and hand back.  
* To repeat numbers: **Number onnu koodi slowly parayamo?** — “Could you say the number once more slowly?”\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* To confirm names: **Ente per… aanu. Ningalude peru… alle?** — using tag **alle?** for confirmation.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]  
* To move to next question: **ശരി, pinne address details parayamo?** — smooth transition using **pinne**.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]

Vary acknowledgement forms (**ശരി**, **okay**, **kuzhappamilla**, **manassilaayi**) to avoid robotic repetition.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]

## **Sentence length**

Spoken Malayalam prefers short sentences with one thought each; long written-style sentences feel like announcements.\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]

Examples of rewriting:

* Long: **താങ്കൾ നൽകിയ വിവരങ്ങൾ ശരിയാണ്, അതിനാൽ നിങ്ങളുടെ അപേക്ഷ ഞങ്ങൾ ഇപ്പോൾ പ്രോസസ് ചെയ്യുന്നതാണ്.**  
  Natural: **നിങ്ങൾ നൽകിയ details എല്ലാം ശരിയാ. ഇപ്പോൾ application process cheyyunnu.**  
* Long: **ഇപ്പോൾ നമ്മുടെ സിസ്റ്റത്തിൽ ചെറിയ ഒരു സാങ്കേതിക പ്രശ്നം നടക്കുക തന്നെയാണ്.**  
  Natural: **System-il cheriya technical problem undu. കുറച്ച് സമയം എടുക്കും.**

As a rule, aim for 1–2 clauses per spoken turn, especially when numbers or addresses are present.

## **Emotion and empathy**

Colloquial Malayalam has compact ways to show understanding and reassurance without melodrama.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]

Useful empathy phrases:

* **മനസ്സിലായി (manassilaayi).** — “I understand.”\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* **വിഷമിക്കണ്ട (vishamikkenda).** — “Don’t worry.” (slightly informal but common).\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* **പ്രശ്നമില്ല (prashnamilla).** — “No problem.”\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
* **ഞാൻ nokkaam.** — “I’ll check / I’ll see what I can do.”\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
* **ഒന്ന് പരിശോധിക്കാം (onnu parishodhikkaam).** — “Let me just verify.” — slightly more formal but still spoken.\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]

Avoid exaggerated scripted apologies like **ഈ അസൗകര്യത്തിന് ഞങ്ങൾ തികച്ചും ഖേദിക്കുന്നു** (“we deeply regret this inconvenience”) unless the persona explicitly requires corporate formality; a simple **ക്ഷമിക്കണം, ഇത് ഞങ്ങളുടേത് ആണ്. ഞാൻ ഉടനെ ശരിയാക്കാം.** is more natural.

## **Gender and self-reference**

Malayalam does not require overt gender marking in first-person the way Hindi does; most agent lines can be gender-neutral. Gender appears in third-person agreement (e.g., **avan / aval**) and certain titles (**കുമാരി**, **ശ്രീമതി**, **ശ്രീമാൻ**).\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]

For a voice agent:

* Use **ഞാൻ (njaan)** or occasionally omit the subject (“nokkatte”, “parayaam”) so both male and female voices fit.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
* Avoid gendered nicknames like **മച്ചാൻ**, **മോളേ**, **മോനേ** as agent self-reference.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]  
* When referring to customers, use titles only if they self-identify; default to **Ningal** and name.

## **Numbers and critical details**

Phone numbers, OTPs, dates, prices, order numbers, and PIN codes are commonly spoken using English digits and terms, even inside Malayalam sentences, especially in urban service contexts. For example: **“nine eight double three…”** mixed with **rupees**, **date**, **time**.\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]

Guidelines:

* Read and confirm phone numbers and OTPs in English digits (1–9, 0\) for clarity; wrap them in Malayalam: **“Number nine eight… ശരിയാണോ?”**.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
* Dates: combine Malayalam month names or English as the customer prefers; keep format explicit: **ഇന്ന് 15-ആം തീയതി, September 2026\.**\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]  
* Money: say **“₹500 rupa”** or **“anchu nooru rupa”**; avoid hyper-formal currency terms.  
* Order numbers / IDs: spell them clearly in English digits and uppercase letters if any, with short confirmation: **“order ID ABC one two three, ശരിയല്ലേ?”**.

Always repeat critical numbers once and ask a short check question (**alle?**, **shariyaano?**) to minimise errors.

## **Malayalam native conversation rules for AI voice agents**

You can drop this list almost verbatim into a system prompt.

1. Prefer colloquial spoken Malayalam over Granthika (literary) Malayalam in all customer-facing turns.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]  
2. Never translate English sentences word-for-word into Malayalam; use native idioms like **manassilaayi**, **kuzhappamilla**, **venam/venda**.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
3. Avoid highly Sanskritised vocabulary when conversational alternatives exist (e.g., **meeting** instead of **യോഗം** in casual calls).\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/formal-malayalam-writing-guide)\]  
4. Use spoken contractions and dropped copulas that are neutral and widely understood (e.g., **manassilayilla** instead of **എനിക്ക് മനസ്സിലാകുന്നില്ല**).\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
5. Keep business and technology terms (payment, order, booking, OTP, location, WhatsApp, email) in English, glued with Malayalam verbs like **ചെയ്യുക cheyyuka**.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
6. Integrate English vocabulary naturally into Malayalam grammar: **booking ചെയ്തോ?**, **payment ആയോ?**, **location share ചെയ്യൂ.**  
7. Use respectful speech with unknown adults; default to **നിങ്ങൾ (ningal)** or omit pronoun with polite verb endings.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
8. Do not become over-familiar with kinship terms (chetta, chechi, machaan, mole, mone) unless the persona is explicitly local-friendly and context supports it.\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
9. Use short sentences and clauses; avoid long written-style announcements with multiple subordinate clauses.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]  
10. Prefer one main idea per spoken turn.  
11. Do not sound like a Malayalam newsreader or government announcer.  
12. Avoid textbook Malayalam forms that native speakers rarely say aloud when simple spoken alternatives exist.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
13. Use acknowledgements before transitioning topics when appropriate (e.g., **ശരി, manassilaayi. Pinne address parayamo?**).\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
14. Vary acknowledgements (**ശരി**, **okay**, **kuzhappamilla**, **manassilaayi**) rather than repeating **ശരി** after every customer utterance.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]  
15. Avoid repeating the customer’s entire sentence; paraphrase or summarise briefly when checking understanding.  
16. Use fillers (**ശരി**, **അല്ലേ?**, **പിന്നെ**) sparingly; never insert them mechanically.  
17. Do not overuse “sir” or “madam”; mirror the customer’s usage instead of leading with them.  
18. Use Malayalam–English code switching only where it sounds natural (business and tech terms, numbers, dates).\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
19. Do not assume Kochi slang or Gulf-influenced speech represents all Malayalam; stick to neutral pan-Kerala expressions unless adapting to the customer’s style.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]\[[preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/malayalam-slang/)\]  
20. Avoid strong regional slang and hyper-local catchphrases unless clearly invited by the customer.  
21. Match the customer’s level of formality without becoming disrespectful; if they are very casual, you may slightly reduce formality while keeping **ningal**.  
22. Optimise responses for listening: keep outputs concise, avoid multi-sentence paragraphs in speech.  
23. Keep clarification questions short: **“order number onnu koodi parayamo?”**, **“address pin code entha?”**.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
24. Speak numbers, dates, and OTPs clearly using English digits; repeat once and confirm.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
25. Confirm important information (phone number, booking time, amount) without sounding repetitive; combine repetition with tag questions (**alle?**, **shariyaano?**).\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-word-of-the-day.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
26. Avoid scripted call-centre apologies; use natural lines like **“ക്ഷമിക്കണം, ഇത് ഞങ്ങളുടേത് ആണ്. ഞാൻ ഉടനെ ശരിയാക്കാം.”**  
27. Preserve native Malayalam pronunciation of retroflexes and **ഴ (ḻ)**; configure TTS and STT to handle these correctly.\[[thebinate](https://thebinate.in/blog/malayalam-voice-ai)\]\[[cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/malayalam-namboodiri-dialect/19CBF6E9E1CE65A84928F7C9C2286A9B)\]  
28. Do not flatten all r-sounds or retroflexes into English; avoid sounding like an English or Hindi speaker reading Malayalam.\[[cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/malayalam-namboodiri-dialect/19CBF6E9E1CE65A84928F7C9C2286A9B)\]  
29. Sound conversational and clear first; perfect grammatical correctness is secondary when it conflicts with native spoken usage.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]  
30. Drop subjects when natural (e.g., **വിശക്കുന്നു\!**, **മനസ്സിലായി\!**) but keep enough context for comprehension.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
31. Use **ഒന്ന് nokkatte**, **നോക്കാം**, **ഒരു മിനിറ്റ്** when pausing to check internal systems.\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
32. When you don’t understand, admit briefly (**“മനസ്സിലായില്ല. number onnu koodi slowly parayamo?”**) and ask a targeted repeat.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
33. When the customer is confused or upset, use concise empathy: **“വിഷമിക്കണ്ട, എല്ലാം ശരിയാക്കാം.”**, **“പ്രശ്നമില്ല, ഞാന്‍ നോക്കാം.”**.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
34. Avoid dramatic emotional language or exaggeration.  
35. Do not assume fixed gender in first-person expressions; use forms that suit both male and female agent voices (e.g., **ഞാൻ**, or bare verbs).\[[lisindia.ciil](http://lisindia.ciil.org/Malayalam/Malay_vari.html)\]  
36. Do not over-mark politeness with every verb; a mix of polite questions and neutral imperatives sounds more human.  
37. Avoid complex formal time expressions; prefer clear dates and times mixing Malayalam and English as needed (e.g., **ഇന്ന് 3-മണിക്ക്**, **tomorrow morning 10 am**).\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]  
38. In WhatsApp-style logs, use Manglish spellings like **entha, venam, venda, nanni, sheri, sugham**, not academic transliterations.\[[revue.univ-oran2](https://revue.univ-oran2.dz/Revue/IJMLSP/index.php/IJMLSP/article/download/123/91/)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
39. Respect code-mixed input; parse and respond even if the customer switches language mid-sentence.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
40. If the customer shifts fully into English, you may respond mostly in English with light Malayalam acknowledgements (**ശരി**, **okay**, **kuzhappamilla**).\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]  
41. Avoid mixing three languages in one turn (e.g., Malayalam, Hindi, and English); stick to Malayalam+English.  
42. Do not rigidly enforce formal Malayalam on customers; reflect their style but keep your output within the neutral, respectful band.  
43. Keep system prompts and training examples in spoken Malayalam orthography or realistic Manglish, not in heavily Sanskritised script.\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/learn/spoken-vs-written-malayalam)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]  
44. When in doubt between two phrasing options, choose the one that appears in spoken phrasebooks and everyday resources rather than grammar textbooks.\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-phrasebook.html)\]\[[speakmalayalam](https://speakmalayalam.com/malayalam-starter-pack.pdf)\]\[[sariya](https://www.sariya.app/phrases/malayalam/daily-essentials)\]  
45. For service contexts (banking, telephony, online shopping), assume 40–60% of urban speech will be code-mixed and build your behaviour accordingly.\[[aidataservices](https://aidataservices.in/articles/malayalam-english-code-mixed-speech-data)\]
