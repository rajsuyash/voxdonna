## The enquiry does not start on your website

Ask a jeweller in India where customer conversations begin and almost nobody says the website. It is WhatsApp, and it has been for years.

A photo of a necklace taken at a wedding. A screenshot from your Instagram post. A forwarded image of a competitor's piece with "do you have something like this". A voice note in Malayalam asking whether the Anna Nagar branch has it. "Kitna hua mera scheme." Store timings on a festival day.

They arrive all day, in no particular order, from a number your customers saved years ago. And they get answered by whoever on the sales team happens to have a free hand.

That last part is the problem. Not the volume — the **randomness of who answers and when.**

## Two minutes versus tomorrow

The economics of a WhatsApp jewellery enquiry are unusually simple.

Answered in two minutes, it is a live conversation with someone who is actively shopping. You can send pictures, ask what the occasion is, and offer a time to come in.

Answered tomorrow, it is an archaeology exercise. The customer has moved on, probably to whichever jeweller replied first, and your reply now reads as an interruption rather than a service.

Nothing about the reply changes. Only the delay does, and the delay is decided entirely by whether a salesperson was free at that moment — which is to say, by luck.

## What actually arrives, and what it needs

Before automating anything, it is worth sorting the inbox honestly. In a jewellery business the traffic separates cleanly into three kinds.

**Questions with exactly one right answer.** Today's rate. Store timings. Branch addresses. Exchange policy. Making charges by category. What hallmarking means. Scheme terms. These do not vary by customer, they do not need judgement, and they are the bulk of the volume.

**Questions that need your data but not your people.** Is this in stock at this branch. What is my scheme balance. Is my repair ready. Where is my bespoke order. The answer is specific to the customer but it is sitting in a system, not in someone's head.

**Conversations that need a person, eventually.** Bridal. A dispute. A valuation. A high-value bespoke commission. An unhappy customer.

The first two are roughly eighty percent of what lands on the number, and neither is a good use of a salesperson's afternoon. The third is where your advisors earn their keep — and it is exactly what they have least time for, because the first two are eating it.

## What an AI WhatsApp agent handles

An [AI WhatsApp agent for a jewellery store](/jewellers/whatsapp-automation.html) runs on the business number customers already have saved, and takes the first two categories outright.

**It reads what actually arrived.** Not just typed text — photos of a piece the customer wants something like, and voice notes, which are a large share of Indian WhatsApp traffic. A customer who sends a thirty-second voice note in Tamil is not going to retype it in English because your system prefers that.

**It sends pictures back.** Jewellery is bought with the eyes. "We have three in that style" is worth a fraction of three photographs from your actual catalogue, with the branch each one is at.

**It answers from your approved information.** Your rate sheet, your stock extract, your scheme ledger, your policy text. Not a general model's idea of what a making charge should be.

**It qualifies without interrogating.** Occasion, rough budget, preferred branch, the date it is needed for. Four things your advisor needs in order to prepare — asked the way a good salesperson asks them, spread through the conversation rather than as a form.

**It books the consultation** into the advisor's diary and confirms it in the same thread the customer started, so the confirmation lives where they will look for it.

## Gold savings schemes: the clearest win

If you run savings schemes, this is usually where automation pays for itself first, and it has nothing to do with being clever.

Roughly a quarter of Indian gold and jewellery sales run through scheme structures, and the enquiry volume follows: what is due, what have I paid, what happens if I miss this month, when does it mature. Every one of those answers is in your scheme ledger.

More importantly, the **outbound** side is pure repetition on a schedule — a reminder before the instalment is due, a follow-up after a missed one. Done by hand it falls to whoever remembers; done on a schedule it simply happens, and your team is left chasing only the people who did not respond.

## Bridal is a thread, not a message

A bridal enquiry is the most valuable thing that arrives on your WhatsApp and the one most likely to be lost, because it does not behave like the others.

A bridal order typically runs three to five showroom visits, with design revisions, an advance payment and a final fitting — playing out over weeks in a single thread. Industry accounts put roughly one in four bridal leads lost to a competitor, and the usual cause is not price. It is a week in which nobody replied.

The automation here is not answering the bridal enquiry. It is **keeping the thread alive between visits** — following up on the design that was promised, confirming the next appointment, nudging when it goes quiet — and handing to a human advisor the moment there is a decision to be made.

## The rules that keep the number safe

A jewellery business's WhatsApp number is a real asset, and getting it restricted would be a genuinely bad day. So the constraints matter as much as the capability.

- Outbound goes through **approved WhatsApp Business templates**, not bulk sends from a personal number.
- The **24-hour session window** is respected.
- **STOP is honoured permanently** on any thread, and recorded.
- Opt-in lists are the only lists that get proactive messages.

None of this is optional, and any approach that treats it as optional is trading a short-term campaign for your primary customer channel.

## Hinglish is a real language here

Customers type the way they talk. "Kitna hua mera scheme" is half Hindi and half English in one line, and the same customer may switch to full English two messages later. In the south, voice notes in Tamil, Telugu, Kannada or Malayalam are routine.

An agent that handles English only is an agent that handles a fraction of your inbox. One agent covering Hindi, English, Hinglish, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Gujarati and Bengali — in text *and* voice notes — means you are not staffing a language desk per region.

## Where it connects

The integration list is short, which is usually a surprise:

- **WhatsApp Business API** on your existing number
- **Your product catalogue and collection images** — so it sends your pieces
- **A branch stock extract** — so availability answers are true
- **Your rate sheet** — the same one your team publishes each morning
- **The scheme ledger** — balances and instalment dates
- **Your CRM or lead sheet** — where the qualified enquiry and the booked slot are written back

Nothing needs to be installed inside your billing software to start.

## A sensible first month

Do not automate the whole inbox. Pick the single highest-volume question — for most jewellers that is the rate enquiry or the scheme balance — and let the agent handle only that, with everything else routed to your team as it is today.

You will learn two things quickly: what proportion of your inbox is genuinely repetitive, and where your team's judgement is actually needed. Both are worth knowing before you expand the scope.
