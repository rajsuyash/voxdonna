<h3 id="blg-what-custom-adds">What “custom” adds</h3>
<p>A custom or tailored workflow follows your own rules and your own data rather than a template. A lead follow-up flow, for example, can start from an inbound message, collect the missing details, check or update a record in a connected system, trigger a follow-up and pass the conversation to a person when a condition is met. Vendor documentation for AI-agent workflows describes exactly these building blocks: <a href="https://crmsupport.freshworks.com/support/solutions/articles/50000011733-workflows-for-ai-agents">workflows that start from customer messages, use condition paths and APIs to update connected systems, capture lead information and hand conversations to human agents</a>. What makes it custom is mapping those blocks to your pricing rules, approval limits, product catalog and escalation policy.</p>

<h2 id="blg-when-to-bring-in-a-consultant-instead-of-building-in-house">When to bring in a consultant instead of building in-house</h2>
<p>In-house automation works well for contained tasks inside one system, owned by one team, with few exceptions. Outside help pays off under different conditions.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The process crosses systems.</strong> An order change that touches the CRM, the ERP, email and a customer channel needs integration work that most operations teams do not do daily.</li>
<li><strong>Exceptions are frequent.</strong> Refusals, escalations and partial approvals need designed rules, not just a happy path.</li>
<li><strong>Compliance or audit requirements apply.</strong> Every action needs a log that shows what was done, on what data and under which rule.</li>
<li><strong>Capacity is the constraint.</strong> The people who know the process best are also the people running it every day.</li>
<li><strong>Nobody owns the result after launch.</strong> Automations drift when data formats, products or policies change, and someone must monitor them.</li>
</ol>
<p>If your situation matches two or more of these, a scoped engagement is usually faster and cheaper than a long internal attempt. For sales processes in particular, see <a href="https://voxdonna.com/blog/en/ai-agents-for-sales-teams.html">AI agents for sales teams: tasks, controls and metrics</a> for how tasks and controls are divided between agent and staff.</p>
<h2 id="blg-how-to-tell-which-processes-are-ready-for-automation">How to tell which processes are ready for automation</h2>
<p>The assessment phase decides whether the engagement succeeds. A practical scoring approach rates each candidate on volume, repetition, data quality, rule clarity, business value, manageable exceptions and change risk, and records baseline time, volume, rework and delays. <a href="https://opsmith.ca/resources/how-to-identify-processes-to-automate">Bounded workflows with a clear trigger, output, owner and measurable problem</a> are the strongest candidates, and the score guides investigation rather than triggering automatic implementation.</p>
<p>The New South Wales government’s automation guide gives a complementary profile: <a href="https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/delivery/nsw-automation-guide/automation-lifecycle/stage-1-discover">processes suited to automation are manual, rule-based, stable, low-exception, repetitive, error-prone and measurable, with digitally readable inputs</a>. The same guide tells assessors to weigh data sensitivity, compliance, integration effort, scalability and impact on employees.</p>
<p>In practice, the following workflows score well in most mid-sized businesses:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lead response and follow-up.</strong> High volume, a clear trigger and a measurable delay.</li>
<li><strong>Repetitive customer inquiries.</strong> Order status, delivery questions and document requests with answers held in existing systems.</li>
<li><strong>Order change requests.</strong> Rule-bound, with status fields and delivery blocks that decide whether a change is allowed.</li>
<li><strong>Data entry into ERP or CRM.</strong> Extraction from emails, PDFs and messages into structured records.</li>
<li><strong>Recurring reporting.</strong> Scheduled compilation from several sources.</li>
</ul>
<p>Those that score poorly are rare, high-judgment tasks, processes about to be redesigned, and workflows whose inputs are inconsistent and undocumented.</p>
<h2 id="blg-what-a-custom-automation-engagement-includes-step-by-step">What a custom automation engagement includes, step by step</h2>
<p>The steps below describe a typical engagement. Names differ between firms, but the sequence holds.</p>
<h3 id="blg-discovery">1. Discovery</h3>
<p>The consultant interviews process owners, reads the current procedures and examines the systems involved. The output is a process map showing who does what, where data enters and where delays arise. The common failure here is relying on how people describe the work rather than how the system records it.</p>
<h3 id="blg-baseline">2. Baseline</h3>
<p>Before building, record how the process performs today. A useful guide on measuring automation advises examining <a href="https://www.cio.com/article/4213758/before-you-automate-anything-learn-to-measure-it.html">audit trails, system logs and ticket timestamps rather than self-reported estimates</a>, and keeping to a few measures: elapsed time from request to completion, active effort, error and rework rates, and consistency. Without this baseline, no result can be verified later.</p>
<h3 id="blg-prioritization">3. Prioritization</h3>
<p>Candidates are scored as described above and ranked. A good shortlist is short. It names the first workflow, the reason it comes first and the ones deliberately deferred.</p>
<h3 id="blg-design">4. Design</h3>
<p>The design document sets out the trigger, the inputs, the decision rules, the permitted actions and the outcomes. A well-specified order change workflow, for instance, defines three outcomes: executed, prepared as a draft for approval, or refused and escalated. It also specifies which ERP fields are read, which are written and which are never touched.</p>
<h3 id="blg-build-and-test">5. Build and test</h3>
<p>The agent is connected to ERP, CRM, email, WhatsApp or voice channels and tested on historical and simulated cases, including the awkward ones. Testing before the agent acts on live data is the control that protects your records. This is the principle behind <a href="https://voxdonna.com/ai-agent-development.html">our AI agent development approach</a>, where the agent is tested before it writes to your systems.</p>
<h3 id="blg-pilot">6. Pilot</h3>
<p>The workflow runs on a limited scope, such as one product line, one region or one inbound channel, with humans reviewing output. The pilot shows real exception rates and surfaces rules nobody wrote down.</p>
<h3 id="blg-rollout-and-operation">7. Rollout and operation</h3>
<p>Scope widens in phases. Logs, dashboards and an escalation path stay in place, and someone owns tuning. Results are compared with the baseline at fixed intervals, for example after 30, 60 and 90 days.</p>
<h2 id="blg-what-affects-cost-and-timeline">What affects cost and timeline</h2>
<p>Consulting and automation projects are usually priced by scope, not by a fixed catalog, because the same label can mean very different work. These factors move cost and duration most:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Number of workflows.</strong> One bounded workflow is a different project from five connected ones.</li>
<li><strong>Number and age of integrations.</strong> A modern CRM with an API is simpler than an ERP customized over many years.</li>
<li><strong>Exception complexity.</strong> Every additional rule, approval level or document type adds design and testing time.</li>
<li><strong>Channels.</strong> Adding voice, WhatsApp and email to one workflow multiplies testing, and languages add more.</li>
<li><strong>Data quality.</strong> Clean product, customer and price data shortens the build. Cleanup extends it.</li>
<li><strong>Volume and usage.</strong> Run costs follow message, call and transaction volume.</li>
<li><strong>Governance needs.</strong> Audit logs, approval chains and security reviews take time to specify and verify.</li>
</ul>
<p>Because of this, phased scoping is the sensible structure: assessment first, then a pilot with a defined success measure, then rollout. Each phase has its own price and its own decision point, so you commit budget as evidence arrives. For tiered pricing, our published plans and one-time setup fees are listed under <a href="https://voxdonna.com/#pricing">AI automation services pricing</a>.</p>
<h2 id="blg-how-to-measure-results-and-judge-a-recommendation">How to measure results and judge a recommendation</h2>
<p>A recommendation is realistic when it can be checked. Use these tests on any proposal.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Is there a baseline?</strong> The proposal should state current time, volume, error rate and rework for the target workflow, with the source of each number.</li>
<li><strong>Is the metric small and specific?</strong> Three or four measures tied to the process, such as time to first response, share of requests resolved without staff, and error rate, beat a long list of vanity figures.</li>
<li><strong>Is there a named owner on your side?</strong> Each workflow needs an accountable person who approves rules and reviews exceptions.</li>
<li><strong>Are exceptions designed?</strong> Ask what happens when the agent cannot decide. The answer should include human handoff with the context attached.</li>
<li><strong>Is there an audit trail?</strong> Every action should be logged with the rule and data used.</li>
<li><strong>Does the scope start narrow?</strong> Promises to automate everything at once usually hide missing assessment work.</li>
<li><strong>Who is accountable after launch?</strong> Monitoring, tuning and incident response should be assigned in writing.</li>
</ol>
<p>Track the results at fixed intervals and compare them with the original baseline, not with a revised one. The same CIO guidance warns that without measurement, <a href="https://www.cio.com/article/4213758/before-you-automate-anything-learn-to-measure-it.html">organizations cannot defend improvements when budgets tighten</a>, so the measurement plan belongs in the contract from the first day.</p>

<h2 id="blg-faq">FAQ</h2>
<h3 id="blg-what-do-business-process-automation-consultants-actually-deliver">What do business process automation consultants actually deliver?</h3>
<p>They deliver an assessed list of candidate processes, a prioritized plan, workflow designs and, in the stronger engagements, the built and deployed automation with monitoring. The deliverables should be named in the proposal, along with the baseline and the measures used to judge results.</p>
<h3 id="blg-how-long-does-an-automation-consulting-engagement-take">How long does an automation consulting engagement take?</h3>
<p>It depends on the number of workflows, the integrations and the exceptions involved. A common pattern is a short assessment, a bounded pilot, then phased rollout, with a decision point after each phase. Ask for a timeline per phase rather than one figure for the whole project.</p>
<h3 id="blg-can-small-and-mid-sized-businesses-use-automation-consulting">Can small and mid-sized businesses use automation consulting?</h3>
<p>Yes, when they have a repetitive, measurable workflow with real volume, such as lead follow-up or customer inquiries. Starting with one workflow and a pilot keeps the commitment proportionate. Our messaging plans begin from $20 per month, with setup fees listed on the offer pages.</p>
<h3 id="blg-what-is-a-process-automation-assessment">What is a process automation assessment?</h3>
<p>It is the first phase, in which each candidate process is scored on volume, repetition, data quality, rule clarity, value, exceptions and change risk, and measured against a baseline. The output is a ranked shortlist and a recommendation for the first workflow to build.</p>
<h3 id="blg-how-do-i-know-the-consultants-estimate-is-realistic">How do I know the consultant’s estimate is realistic?</h3>
<p>Check that it rests on your own logs and timestamps, names an owner for each workflow, designs for exceptions and includes an audit trail. A realistic plan also starts narrow and states who is accountable after launch.</p>
