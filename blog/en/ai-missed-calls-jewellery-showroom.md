## The hour you are least able to answer is the hour worth most

Walk into any busy jewellery showroom on a Saturday afternoon. Four advisors on the floor, each one deep in a consultation — a tray of bangles out, a family deciding, a mother holding a piece up to the light. This is exactly what good retail looks like.

Now listen to the phone.

It rings with a question that would take ninety seconds to answer. Today's 22 carat rate. Do you have this in a size 16. What time do you close on Sunday. Nobody is free, because everybody who could answer is doing the more valuable thing. So it rings out.

The caller does not leave a voicemail. They ring the next jeweller on the list.

This is the structural problem with the showroom phone line: **your capacity to answer it is lowest precisely when the demand for it is highest.** Saturday afternoon, the week before Dhanteras, the fortnight of a wedding season — these are the same hours in both directions. The busier your floor, the more calls you lose, and the calls you lose are the ones from people who were ready to buy.

## What is actually on the other end of those calls

It is worth being precise about this, because "we miss some calls" sounds like a small operational annoyance rather than a revenue line.

The calls a jewellery showroom misses are overwhelmingly **not** complex. In practice they cluster into a handful of repeated questions:

- **The rate call.** Today's gold rate, today's silver rate, usually 22 carat, sometimes 18. The single highest-volume inbound call in Indian jewellery retail.
- **Availability.** Is that necklace from the new collection at this branch. Do you have it in my size. Is the matching set in stock.
- **Timings and logistics.** Sunday hours, festival hours, parking, which branch is closer.
- **Exchange and making charges.** How old gold is valued, what the making charge is on a particular category, what hallmarking means for resale.
- **Scheme questions.** What is due on a gold savings scheme, what has been paid in, what happens after a missed instalment.
- **The bridal enquiry.** The one that is worth twenty times the others, and the one most likely to arrive on a Saturday.

Five of those six have exactly one correct answer, and it does not change from caller to caller. They do not need a trained advisor. They need *somebody to pick up*.

The sixth one — bridal — does need a person eventually. But it does not need a person *first*. It needs someone to establish the occasion, the rough budget and the preferred branch, and to get a consultation into the diary while the family is still interested.

A good share of those callers want to see the piece on themselves before they drive in, which is the question [virtual try-on](/virtual-try-on.html) answers.

## Why voicemail and callbacks do not solve it

Most showrooms have already tried the obvious fixes, and the obvious fixes underperform for reasons worth naming.

**Voicemail assumes the caller will wait.** They will not. A customer comparing three jewellers on a Saturday is not leaving a message and sitting by the phone. They are dialling the next number before your greeting finishes.

**A callback list assumes somebody works it.** In practice the list is worked when the floor goes quiet, which is Tuesday, which is three days after the decision was made somewhere else.

**A dedicated phone person is expensive and still single-threaded.** They handle one call at a time, take lunch, and go home at eight. Dhanteras does not.

**An IVR menu makes it worse.** "Press 1 for rates, press 2 for store timings" is a way of asking a customer to do clerical work on your behalf. Most hang up.

The common failure in all four is the same: they treat a missed call as a message to be retrieved later, when it is actually **an intent that expires**.

## What an AI voice agent does instead

An [AI voice agent for a jewellery showroom](/jewellers/ai-voice-agent.html) answers on the first ring, in the language the caller opened in, and handles the call to a conclusion rather than to a message.

A realistic version of the Saturday call:

> **Customer:** "Aaj ka gold rate kya hai? 22 carat."
>
> **Agent:** "Today's 22 carat rate is the one on our board — I'll send it on WhatsApp so you have it in writing. Are you looking for something particular?"
>
> **Customer:** "Bangles. Shaadi ke liye."
>
> **Agent:** "Our bridal advisor keeps Saturday and Sunday slots. Shall I hold one for you this weekend?"

Three things happened there that a voicemail cannot do.

**It answered the question that was asked**, from the rate your team approved that morning — not a number it inferred, and not a price it negotiated.

**It put the answer in writing.** The rate goes to WhatsApp, which means the customer now has your number in their thread and a reason to come back to it.

**It converted an information request into a booking.** The rate call was never really about the rate. It was a customer establishing whether you are worth the trip.

## The part that matters more than the answer: the refusal

There is a version of this that is worse than a missed call, and it is an agent that invents a making charge, quotes a price it was not given, or promises a piece is in stock when it is not.

A jewellery customer who drives across a city for a necklace that is at another branch does not forgive it, and they tell people.

So the design principle is the opposite of the usual one: **the refusal path is built before the answer path.** The agent answers from your rate sheet, your stock extract, your scheme terms and your published policy. A question outside that gets handed to a person with the context already captured — who called, what they asked, what they were told. An advisor picks up a complete note rather than a recording.

That is also what makes it safe to run unsupervised on a Saturday. It is not deciding anything. It is reading your own approved information out loud, quickly, to everyone who rings.

## After hours is not a smaller version of the same problem

It is a different problem, and often a larger one.

A wedding family making a decision on Sunday morning is not going to wait for Monday. Neither is the customer browsing your Instagram at 10pm who taps the number on your profile. The call at 9pm is frequently a *higher-intent* call than the one at 2pm, because nobody rings a jeweller at night casually.

Out-of-hours enquiries currently go to nothing at all. There is no floor to be busy — the shutters are down. Every one of them is a loss with no competing explanation.

This is usually where the arithmetic gets persuasive fastest, because there is no staffing alternative to compare against. You are not choosing between the agent and a person. You are choosing between the agent and silence.

## Festival peaks, where the gap is widest

Akshaya Tritiya, Dhanteras and the wedding season compress months of demand into days. Two things happen at once, and they compound.

Call volume goes up sharply. Floor availability goes down sharply, because every advisor is with a customer and the queue is real.

The result is that your worst answer rate lands on your highest-value days. Adding temporary staff helps a little, but a person hired for Dhanteras does not know your scheme terms, your branch stock or which advisor handles bridal — so they take messages too.

An agent absorbing the routine overflow during those days does something specific and useful: **it lets the queue standing in your showroom get your team's full attention**, which is where the margin actually is.

## What this looks like in practice

The sequence is unremarkable, which is the point:

1. The agent answers on the first ring, in Hindi, Tamil, Hinglish or whatever the caller opened with.
2. It works out the intent — rate, availability, timings, scheme, repair, bridal.
3. It answers from your approved information, or says plainly that it does not know.
4. It books the showroom visit against real advisor availability, and confirms it on WhatsApp.
5. It writes the record into your CRM: who called, what they asked, what was promised.
6. It escalates anything that needs judgement, with the context attached.

Nothing is installed in your billing system to start. A scheduled export of rates, stock and scheme data is enough for a first line. A live connection makes answers fresher and can come later.

## Where to start

Not with the whole phone line. Start with the single call type your team is most tired of taking — for most showrooms that is the rate call, because it is high volume and zero judgement — or with the after-hours window, because there is currently nothing there at all.

One call type, one branch, and a week of call logs to check it against. If it answers that one well, the rest is a configuration question rather than a decision.
