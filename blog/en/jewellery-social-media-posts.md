# Jewellery Social Media Posts: A Weekly System for Showrooms

## The Short Answer

Jewellery social media posts work when they follow a schedule, not a mood. Post three times a week on the Instagram feed or Reels, and use Stories and WhatsApp Status on the days you are in the showroom. Give each weekday one job. Shoot the simple posts on a phone. Hand the one expensive-looking post a month, the ad, to someone who makes ads.

That is the system. Most showrooms have one person who "does Instagram" between billing and customer calls. This page is written for that person. It tells you what to post on which day, what to shoot yourself, what to outsource, what to never post, and how to check at the end of the month which posts brought enquiries.

It is not a campaign calendar. For the year-long view, read the [jewellery shop advertisement plan](/blog/en/jewellery-shop-advertisement-plan.html). This is the weekly layer underneath it.

## How Many Jewellery Posts Is Realistic?

Fewer than you think, done on the same days every week.

A showroom that posts daily for two weeks and vanishes for a month does worse than one that posts three times a week for a year. Customers notice whether your page looks alive when they check it before walking in.

A realistic load for a non-creator:

- **Instagram:** 3 feed posts or Reels a week, plus Stories on the days you are in the showroom with a phone in your hand.
- **Facebook:** the same Reels and feed posts, reposted. No separate content.
- **WhatsApp Status:** 4 to 5 updates a week. This is the cheapest channel to feed, because it takes ten seconds.

Total new shooting: about 90 minutes once a week. Everything else is reuse.

## The Weekly Rhythm

Give each day one job. When the job is fixed, the "what do I post today" question disappears.

| Day | Post type | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | New arrival close-up | Instagram feed, Facebook, Status |
| Tuesday | Rate update (dated) | Status |
| Wednesday | Try-on or styling Reel | Instagram Reel, Facebook |
| Thursday | Behind the counter | Stories, Status |
| Friday | Customer pickup (with consent) | Instagram feed, Status |
| Saturday | Weekend offer or scheme reminder | Status, Instagram Story |
| Sunday | Rest, or reshare the best post of the week | Status |

Shoot Monday and Wednesday content in one sitting on Monday morning. Pieces are fresh, the showroom is quiet, the light through the front glass is usable. Everything else is a thirty-second job on its own day.

The ad is not on this table because it does not fit a weekday. It is the anchor. More on it below.

## Post Types: What to Shoot, What to Outsource

### New arrivals (shoot yourself)

A piece, a clean surface, good light, one steady clip or three photos. Name the piece, the occasion it suits, and say "ask for price" or state it. This is the workhorse of any jewellery instagram posts schedule. Do it every Monday and it becomes a catalogue customers can scroll.

### Product close-ups (shoot yourself, once you learn macro)

Close-ups are what people screenshot and forward to a sister or a mother. Details sell: the stone setting, the finish, the clasp. A phone in macro mode can do this well. The basics are further down.

### Try-on and styling (shoot yourself, with a willing staff member)

A hand wearing a bangle against a plain saree border. A neck with a necklace over a kurta. Use a staff member who is comfortable on camera. Keep the face out of frame if they are not. This format gives you most of your jewellery reels ideas for free, because the motion does the selling: a wrist turning, a jhumka swinging, a clasp closing.

### Rate updates (shoot yourself, always dated)

A plain card with today's date, the rate, and the showroom name. No design needed. Always put the date on it. Gold rates move through the day, and an undated screenshot gets forwarded for a week after the rate has changed. If you cannot date it, do not post it.

### Behind the counter (shoot yourself)

A craftsperson sizing a ring. A tray being sorted. The morning lamp being lit. This is trust-building content, and it costs nothing. 

### Customer pickups (shoot yourself, with consent)

A family collecting a wedding set is the strongest social proof you own, and needs the most care. See the consent section below.

### Scheme reminders (design once, reuse monthly)

If you run a gold savings scheme, post the instalment date, the benefit and the contact method on a simple card. Make one template and change the date. Status is the right home for this, because it reaches the customers already paying into the scheme. ## WhatsApp Status: The Channel That Reaches Existing Customers

Instagram finds strangers. WhatsApp Status reaches people who already saved your number: the family who bought a mangalsutra three years ago, the customer on your scheme, the relative who forwards your rate card.

These people are the cheapest sales you will ever make. They already trust you. They are one tap from replying. And a reply lands in the same chat where they would book an appointment.

What to post on WhatsApp Status for a jewellery shop:

1. **The dated rate card,** Tuesday and any day the rate moves sharply.
2. **A new arrival,** one photo, one line, "ask for price" or the price.
3. **A scheme reminder** a few days before the instalment date.
4. **The ad,** reposted from Reels, in vertical format. It already fits.
5. **A visit prompt:** "Open till 8 pm today. New bangles on the second tray."

Keep every Status post to one idea. Nobody reads a paragraph on a Status.

Saved numbers matter here. Only contacts who saved your number see your Status, so ask every customer at billing to save the showroom number. It costs five seconds and builds the audience this channel depends on.

## Phone Photo Basics for Jewellery

Bad input kills good posts. These habits fix most of it.

**Light.** Window light, not a ceiling tube. Place the piece near a window with daylight coming from the side, away from direct sun. Direct sun burns gold into a flat yellow smear. Showroom spotlights add hot reflections.

**Background.** One plain surface. A velvet tray in a single colour, a cloth, or a sheet of matte card. Remove every other object from the frame. Dark velvet suits silver and diamonds. A soft neutral suits gold.

**Scale reference.** A ring on its own looks the same size as a necklace. Put a hand in the frame, or a coin, or place a piece against a saree border. Customers judge size by what sits next to the piece.

**Macro.** Use the phone's macro or close-focus mode, tap on the stones to focus, and hold still. Brace your elbows on the counter. Two or three sharp close-ups beat ten blurred ones.

**Keep the originals.** Save unedited photos in a shared folder. They are also the input for your monthly ad: a video can only be as true to a piece as the photo it starts from.

## Caption Rules

Short wins. Four lines is a long caption.

1. **Line one names the piece and the occasion.** "Temple-style necklace set for a wedding season."
2. **Line two says one detail.** The stone, the finish, the weight if you want to state it.
3. **Price policy, decided once.** Either show the price or write "Ask for price." Pick one and use it on every post. Mixing them confuses customers.
4. **One call to action.** "Message us on WhatsApp to see this piece" or "Visit the showroom this weekend." Not both.

Tags go at the end, three or four, and only the ones people would type. Skip the paragraph about your story. Nobody reads it under a necklace.

## Consent and Privacy for Customer Photos

A customer collecting a piece makes a good post. It also puts their face and their purchase on a public page. Treat it with care.

- **Ask first, in plain words.** "Can we post a photo of the pickup?" Get a clear yes.
- **Prefer hands, not faces.** A hand holding a gift box, with no face, solves most of the problem.
- **Never show the amount, the address or the order sheet** in the frame.
- **Never post a minor's photo** without a parent's clear yes.
- **Keep the customer's "yes" on WhatsApp,** and take the post down the same day if asked.

If a customer hesitates, drop the post. One awkward conversation costs more than any post earns.

## What Not to Post

- **Unverified purity claims.** Do not write "100% pure" or "certified" unless you can show the hallmark or certificate for that exact piece. A claim you cannot back is a complaint waiting to happen.
- **Prices that move with the gold rate, without a date.** If the price depends on the rate, say so and date the post. Otherwise, write "ask for today's price."
- **Stock you do not have.** If a piece sold on Tuesday, remove Monday's post. A customer who walks in for it and finds it gone does not come back.
- **Stone counts or weights you have not checked** against the piece. In any AI-made ad, review every frame against your photos before you accept it.
- **Customer photos without consent,** as above.

## The Monthly Review: Which Posts Brought Enquiries

The review takes twenty minutes on the last day of the month.

Open the showroom's WhatsApp. For every new enquiry that month, ask one question: "Where did you see us?" Staff can ask it at the start of the reply. Write the answer in a simple sheet.

Then check:

1. **Which post type brought the most WhatsApp messages?** New arrival, try-on, ad, rate card?
2. **Which brought walk-ins?** Ask at billing. "Did you see something online?"
3. **Which got nothing?** Drop it or change it.

Keep what worked, drop what did not, and test one new thing next month. After three months, you will know your showroom's pattern, which no generic guide can tell you.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a jewellery shop post on Instagram?

Three posts a week on the feed or Reels, plus Stories on the days you are in the showroom. Consistency matters more than volume. A showroom that posts three times a week for a year builds a page customers trust. One that posts daily for two weeks and stops does not.

### What should a jewellery shop post on WhatsApp Status?

Dated rate cards, new arrivals, scheme reminders, the monthly video ad and a short visit prompt. Status reaches customers who already saved your number, so it suits repeat buyers and scheme members. Keep each update to one idea and ask customers at billing to save the showroom number.

### Should I show prices in jewellery posts?

Pick one policy and apply it to every post. Either show the price with the date, or write "ask for price." If the price moves with the gold rate, never post it undated. A price that was right on Monday and wrong on Thursday creates arguments at the counter.
