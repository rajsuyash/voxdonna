Before lunch, every day

Six things happening on your aftermarket line that nobody planned for.

Manufacturing inbound is not complicated. It is relentless, repetitive, and it lands on people whose actual job is somewhere else.

📦

"Where is my order?" is your largest call category

The distributor buyer calls, the inside-sales rep tabs to SAP, runs a VA03 lookup, opens the carrier page in a second window, comes back and reads a date. Forty-five seconds of value wrapped in eight minutes of hold, context switching and "let me check that for you."

🔩

The spare part nobody can name

The caller has a broken bearing, a machine serial and a competitor's SKU, not your part number. Finding the right material in the master takes a person who knows the catalogue, and that person is one of three, and two of them are on leave.

🧾

Claims that arrive half-filled

Warranty intake lands without the serial, without the hours, without the photos. First touch becomes three touches, the dealer escalates in between, and the claim is older than the fault by the time anyone qualified looks at it.

🌙

The line that stops at 9pm

Breakdowns do not respect the shift. The call goes to voicemail, the claim gets filed tomorrow, the escalation lands the day after, and the customer has already told three people what happened.

📄

Quotations and RFQs that go quiet

An RFQ arrives as an email with a PDF drawing. Somebody has to read it, check the catalogue, price it and reply. The follow-up two weeks later is the one that closes it, and the follow-up two weeks later is the one nobody made.

🗝️

A named user licence to read a date

Every casual user who only needs to look up an order status or a stock figure is a named-user seat in SAP, priced the same way as a power user, with support riding on it every year. Half your service desk is licensed to read.

Every one of these is a business problem wearing an ERP costume. If your team's first question is the mechanism, it is further down this page: how the SAP integration actually works, down to what is read, what is written and what is never installed.

End to end

AI agents for manufacturers, and the calls they own.

Not a menu tree. A call that starts with a ringing phone and ends with a date in writing, a coded claim, or an engineer already on the way.

Workflow A

Order status and ETA, off the inside-sales desk

  1. Dealer or distributor calls the customer-service number and the agent answers on the first ring.
  2. It identifies the caller against the customer master and takes an order number, a PO reference or a delivery number.
  3. It reads the current stage from the order and schedule-line data — an overnight VBAK/VBEP-style extract on day one, an OData or RFC service once basis allows.
  4. Where the consignment has shipped, it adds the carrier status so the caller gets one answer, not two calls.
  5. It states the stage and a realistic date, in the caller's language, switching mid-call if they do.
  6. It confirms on WhatsApp where that helps, so the date exists in writing.
  7. The call leaves a structured record: caller, order, intent, answer given, date promised, whether a human is needed.
  8. A delay that crosses a threshold is flagged to the account owner before the customer has to chase it.
  9. Nothing was written to the ERP, and nobody opened a screen.
Workflow B

Spare part identified, confirmed and ordered

  1. Aftermarket line rings: a dealer, a plant maintenance lead, or a machine operator.
  2. The agent takes whatever they have — a part number, a machine serial, a competitor SKU, or a description of the broken thing.
  3. It resolves that to a material in the part master, and reads it back for confirmation before going further.
  4. It checks availability and the stocking location from the stock extract.
  5. It captures quantity, delivery address and urgency, and takes the order.
  6. Anything that fails a validation — unknown serial, obsolete material, quantity outside the norm — is held for a person, not guessed.
  7. A dispatch confirmation goes out on WhatsApp with the reference.
  8. The order lands in the queue your team already works, complete, with no retyping.
  9. Where a live write path is granted, the sales order is created directly behind validation. That is the one write most teams want, and it stays behind the gate.
Workflow C

Warranty claim and breakdown, complete on first touch

  1. Claim or breakdown call arrives, in or out of shift.
  2. The agent captures serial or PIN, running hours, error codes, date of purchase, dealer of record, and the fault as the caller describes it.
  3. It requests the photos on the call and confirms they arrived on WhatsApp before ending it.
  4. It classifies the fault against your failure modes so the claim routes to the right queue, not the general one.
  5. Where it is a breakdown, it captures asset, symptom and site access requirements and routes to the right engineer with the context written down.
  6. Anything ambiguous escalates with the full file attached, so the engineer starts from a complete note instead of a callback.
  7. The customer gets a reference and the expected next step, on the call.
  8. Quality gets a usable structured report — batch, application, failure mode — instead of an angry summary.
  9. Status questions from then on are self-serve on the same number.

The roster

Five AI employees a manufacturer can put on the line.

Each one owns a call type end to end. You are not buying a channel. You are filling a seat that has been vacant since the last person who knew the catalogue retired.

The order desk

Answers "where is my order" and "when does it ship" on the first ring, from your order and schedule data, and states a realistic date rather than an optimistic one. Logs what it promised.

The parts counter

Resolves a broken thing into a material number, confirms stock, takes the order, and holds anything that fails validation instead of guessing. Works at 2am and during the shutdown week.

The claims clerk

Takes warranty claims complete on first touch — serial, hours, error codes, photos, dealer of record — and routes them coded to the right queue.

The breakdown dispatcher

Takes the call when the line stops, captures asset and symptom, checks site access, and puts the right engineer on it with the context already written down.

The quotation chaser

Reads the RFQ that arrived as an email and a PDF, captures the requirement structured, and makes the follow-up call two weeks later that a person meant to make. For the deals that get won in a room instead, it pairs with whatever you use to capture what was agreed in the plant visit.

How it connects to SAP

The integration question, answered honestly.

Most AI for SAP stalls in the same meeting: basis says no live connection this quarter. So the agents are built to earn their keep without one, and to get better when you grant one.

1
Files, to start

Scheduled exports into a folder. Nothing is installed in SAP, nothing changes, no transport is needed. Most pilots never go further than this.

2
A service, when you are ready

An OData or RFC endpoint, or whatever your middleware already exposes, gives the agent live status instead of this morning's picture.

3
Write access, only where you want it

Creating a sales order is the one write most teams want. It stays behind validation, and anything that fails is held for a person.

4
Read-only by default

Every query path is read-only, row-capped and killed on timeout. A badly phrased question cannot touch your data or your bill.

Questions we get

What plant and service heads ask first.

What does an AI agent for manufacturers actually handle?

Order status and delivery ETA, spare-parts identification and ordering, warranty claim intake, breakdown dispatch, and dealer and distributor enquiries. Those are the calls that arrive constantly, rarely need judgement, and always land on someone whose real job is elsewhere. Quotation and RFQ follow-up sits in the same family and is usually the second thing a manufacturer automates.

Does it need to be integrated with SAP first?

No. A scheduled export of open orders, schedule lines, part master and stock is enough to answer the questions on day one, and nothing is installed inside SAP to run a pilot. A live OData or RFC connection makes the answers fresher and can be added later. How that integration actually works is on the AI agents for SAP page.

Can it read order status from SAP without a named-user licence for every person who asks?

That is most of the point. The people who only ever look up a date or a stock figure are asking the agent instead of logging in. The agent reads through one access path your systems already allow — an export, middleware, or a service — rather than through a seat per person on the service desk.

What happens when it does not know the answer?

It says so, and hands off with the context captured: who called, which order or part, the fault as described, and what was promised. Your engineer starts from a complete note instead of a callback. An agent that invents a ship date costs more than the call it saved, so the refusal path is designed before the answer path.

Will it work for our dealers who do not speak English?

One agent covers twenty-two Indian languages and switches mid-call when the caller does, so a dealer in Coimbatore and a buyer in Ludhiana get the same line. Outside India the same agent runs in the local language and calling hours, which is how the export and European side is handled.

Can it create the order, or only read?

Both, and the difference is deliberate. Every query path is read-only by default. Creating a sales order is the one write most teams want, and it stays behind validation against your master data — anything that fails a check is held for a person rather than posted. You decide which writes are granted and when.

Can an AI agent create a sales order in SAP from a customer purchase order?

Yes, and that is the second thing most manufacturers automate. The customer emails a purchase order as a PDF in their own format; the agent reads it, matches customer, material and price against your master data, creates the sales order and replies with an acknowledgement in the same thread. Every line that does not match cleanly is held for a person rather than posted, because a guessed order costs more than the typing it saved.

Can our CEO ask questions of SAP data without anyone writing SQL?

That is what the reporting copilot is for. The question goes in as plain language and the answer comes back as the number, the chart and the file the figure came from. The model never writes the SQL: it fills a spec of metrics and dimensions that exist in your catalog and code compiles the query, so an unmapped question is refused rather than approximated. It reads the ALV exports your team already downloads, so nothing new has to be built in SAP first.

Does it work with SAP ECC as well as S/4HANA?

Yes, and with SAP Business One. Nothing in the agents depends on a specific release, because the integration point is a service, a mailbox or a file, never a screen. ECC is still the reality in most plants and exports and RFC both work there, so nothing here pushes you toward a migration you had not planned.

Which SAP processes do the agents cover?

Sales order entry from customer purchase orders, order status and delivery ETA calls, spare parts enquiries, warranty and complaint intake, and question answering on FI, SD and MM reporting data. The order agent and the reporting copilot are separate products you can run on their own; on this page they sit alongside the voice line because most manufacturers start with one and add the next.
